# 📻 Radio Communications & Protocols
<TrackBadge variant="tech-comms" />
<TrackBadge variant="field-safety" />

In situations where phones fail, surveillance increases, or coordination must stay fast and local, **radios become essential**. This course covers how to use two-way radios (walkie-talkies), keep communication secure, and build pod-level protocols.

---

## Why Use Radios?

- 📴 Phone service may be down or compromised
- 🔐 Radios reduce reliance on surveillance-prone platforms
- 🧭 Radios work peer-to-peer — no towers or accounts needed

---

## Types of Radios

| Type         | Range           | License Needed? | Best For                          |
|--------------|------------------|------------------|-----------------------------------|
| FRS          | 0.5–2 miles      | No               | Short-range pod comms             |
| GMRS         | 1–5+ miles       | Yes (FCC)        | Neighborhood coordination         |
| HAM          | 2–100+ miles     | Yes + Exam       | Long-distance or disaster ops     |
| Mesh (LoRa)  | 0.5–3 miles/node | No               | Text-only, encrypted mesh networks|

---

## Basic Radio Etiquette

- 🎯 Keep messages **short and clear**
- 🚫 No full names, addresses, or legal risk info over air
- 🎙 Always identify yourself (e.g., “Blue 3 to Red 1”)

---

### Sample Call Protocol

> **[Red 1]**: “Blue 3 to Red 1 — eyes on ICE near 5th and Mission.”  
> **[Blue 3]**: “Copy. Heading that way. ETA 3 minutes.”  
> **[Red 1]**: “Stay in view, no contact. Will update.”  
> **[Blue 3]**: “Understood. Blue 3 out.”

---

## Channel Protocol

- Choose a primary channel before deployment
- Always agree on backup channels (e.g., “If jammed, switch to Ch. 6”)
- Keep chatter minimal on shared channels — move to secondary if needed

---

## Callsigns, Codewords & Pre-Arranged Phrasing

Strong radio discipline relies on clarity, privacy, and consistency. Here’s how to set yours up.

---

### 📛 Choosing Callsigns

Use **descriptive but anonymous** identifiers. A good callsign is short, memorable, and doesn’t reveal legal identity or personal details.

**Best practices:**

- Use colors + numbers for roles (e.g., “Blue 3” = Legal Observer)
- Avoid initials, birth years, or anything personally identifying
- Assign backups in case of radio handoff or tech failure

**Example Pod Map:**

| Role             | Callsign | Backup Callsign |
|------------------|----------|-----------------|
| Field Lead       | Red 1    | Red 5           |
| Legal Observer   | Blue 2   | Blue 4          |
| Care Station     | Green 3  | Green 7         |
| Rideshare Lead   | Yellow 6 | Yellow 9        |

---

### 🗣️ Using Codewords

Assume radios are being listened to. Use codewords to reduce risk and simplify decisions.

**Replace sensitive terms** with neutral or benign alternatives.

| Sensitive Info       | Suggested Codeword  | Notes                              |
|----------------------|---------------------|------------------------------------|
| ICE agent presence   | “Visitors”          | Avoid “ICE” or “police” over air   |
| Immigration van      | “Grey box”          | Refers to surveillance transport   |
| Arrest in progress   | “Package moving”    | Keep it calm and unclear           |
| Location compromised | “Flooded”           | Evacuate that area                 |
| Need for backup      | “Send a jacket”     | Use code instead of “help!”        |

---

### 🔑 Pre-Arranged Phrasing

Agree on specific questions, responses, and alert phrases **before the action**. This reduces confusion and stress.

**Examples:**

- “**Check pulse on Blue 4**” = “Is Blue 4 still active and safe?”
- “**Grey box circling**” = “Unmarked vehicle is doing surveillance”
- “**Transfer complete**” = “Person has safely exited the situation”
- “**Switch water**” = “Move to backup channel”

Practice these in your pod. Adjust based on comfort and clarity.

<Callout type="hint">
Keep a laminated pocket card of codewords and callsigns with your radio.
</Callout>

---

## Drill It Before It’s Real

Set up practice scenarios using only radios and code. Then debrief:

- Did everyone understand the codes?
- Did it feel natural or confusing?
- What would improve clarity without compromising safety?

Drills make discipline second nature. Confidence in language = confidence in the field.

## Encryption & Privacy Tips

- FRS/GMRS radios are **not encrypted** — assume anything you say is public
- Use codewords or pre-arranged phrasing for sensitive situations
- Avoid saying “ICE” or specific names — use phrases like “visitor” or “grey van”

---

## Hardware Setup

🛠 Before using radios:

- Charge fully (bring spares/batteries)
- Test range in your area
- Label each radio with callsign
- Carry headphones for stealth

---

## Radio Drills

Practice builds confidence. Run pod drills monthly:

- Silent check-in (“Tap twice on mic every 5 minutes”)
- Relay practice across terrain
- Emergency “panic” phrase test

---

## Redundancy Plans

Always have backup methods:

- Text trees / Signal groups  
- Flyers with QR codes for regrouping  
- Location meet points (“If all fails, meet at Elm & 14th at 4pm”)

---

## Final Tips

- Radios are **tools**, not magic — they work best with protocols and prep  
- **Teach others** in your pod how to use and troubleshoot them  
- Update channel/callsign plans every 1–2 months to stay adaptable
