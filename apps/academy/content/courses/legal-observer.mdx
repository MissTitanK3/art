# Legal Observer Basics
<TrackBadge variant="observation-legal" />

<Callout type="info">
Legal Observers (LOs) are trained, neutral witnesses. Their job is to document police behavior during protests or actions—not to participate in them. They create legal records, not political ones.
</Callout>

---

## What Is a Legal Observer?

A Legal Observer:

- Acts as a **neutral witness** to law enforcement activity
- Collects evidence of **abuse, misconduct, or unconstitutional behavior**
- Supports protestors by **recording, not intervening**
- Builds legal credibility through clear, impartial documentation

LOs **do not**:

- Intervene or interrupt arrests
- Join chants or actions
- Offer legal advice (unless they are licensed attorneys acting in that role)

## Before You Go: Prep Checklist

- [ ] Notebook and ink pen (non-erasable)
- [ ] Timestamped watch
- [ ] Legal Observer badge or vest
- [ ] Fully charged phone (with location disabled if necessary)
- [ ] Optional: bodycam or backup audio recorder
- [ ] Know local protest and recording laws

<Callout type="hint">
Review local laws around recording. Some states require consent from all parties before you can record audio.
</Callout>

---

## On-Site Protocol

1. **Check in** with your LO coordinator or dispatch
2. Wear your ID visibly
3. Stay on the **sidelines**, not in the crowd
4. Focus on observing police—not protestors
5. Take notes with the following key details:

| Detail                  | Example                                  |
|-------------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| 📍 Location             | 5th & Broadway, NW corner                |
| ⏱️ Time                 | “2:47 PM”                                |
| 👮 Officer identifiers  | “Badge #4421, Officer Jameson”           |
| 📹 Police actions       | “Used baton on seated protestor”         |
| 👤 Protestor impact     | “Maria Lopez detained, arm bleeding”     |

---

## Example Note Format

<div className="p-4 rounded">
  <p><strong>2:47 PM</strong> – Officer Jameson (#4421) shoved protestor w/o warning</p>
  <p><strong>2:49 PM</strong> – Police used pepper spray on crowd, no dispersal order given</p>
  <p><strong>2:53 PM</strong> – 3 individuals detained, names: Maria Lopez, Juan Rivera</p>
</div>


Tips:

- Use ink (not pencil or digital notes) for court reliability
- Don’t editorialize—record **what you see**, not what you assume
- Mark any corrections with a single line (do not erase)

<Callout type="important">
Objective language is crucial. Don’t write “officer was being brutal”—instead, write “officer struck protestor with baton.”
</Callout>

---

## When to Record Video

Recording video or audio depends on your state law. If legal:

- Record from a safe distance
- Narrate neutrally: “Officer moves toward group with baton raised”
- Keep recordings **uncut** and timestamped
- Secure files immediately after the event

<Callout type="warning">
If asked for footage by police, do not comply without legal advice. Only a subpoena or court order can compel release.
</Callout>

---

## Dispersal Orders

If a dispersal order is issued:

- **Leave** unless you have **explicit LO authorization** to stay
- Continue documenting only if it is safe and permitted
- Never obstruct an officer or join a protestor formation

---

## Chain of Custody (Evidence)

To preserve footage or logs as legal evidence:

1. Write your name, date, time, and file name on a cover sheet
2. Document who handles the file at every step
3. Store securely (e.g., encrypted drive or cloud backup)
4. Share with your LO coordinator, not social media

---

## After the Event

- Submit logs and footage to the coordinating legal team
- Participate in debriefs or after-action reports
- Securely archive your materials
- Take care of yourself emotionally and physically

---

## Managing Stress and Burnout

Legal Observation is emotionally intense. You may witness violence without intervening.

- Debrief with a partner or peer support team
- Drink water, eat, rest
- Do grounding exercises: feel your feet, breathe in 4-4-4-4 rhythm
- Take breaks if overwhelmed

<Callout type="info">
It's normal to feel guilt or stress. Observation is protective—even if you couldn’t stop harm in the moment.
</Callout>
