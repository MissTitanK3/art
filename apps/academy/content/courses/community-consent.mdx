# Community Consent
<TrackBadge variant="movement-strategy" />
<TrackBadge variant="community-care" />

<Callout type="info">
Consent is not just personal—it's collective. Community consent is about respecting the agency, safety, and self-determination of entire groups when acting in solidarity or resistance.
</Callout>
---
<Callout type="success">
✅ Example: In 2023, a Portland coalition postponed a protest after migrant families requested discretion—showing community consent in practice.
</Callout>

This training focuses on **consent in grassroots and crisis response settings**—not legal or clinical consent frameworks.

---

## What Is Community Consent?

**Community consent** refers to:

- Seeking approval or input from **those directly impacted** before taking action
- Centering **collective will**, not just individual choices
- Recognizing the history, trauma, and boundaries of a **specific group or place**

It asks:  
**“Who is affected, and did they ask for this?”**

---

## Why It Matters

Taking action without community consent can:

- Reproduce harm—even if unintentionally
- Disempower or override those we claim to support
- Trigger surveillance, retaliation, or trauma
- Erode trust between organizers and communities

---

## Consent Is More Than “Yes or No”

| Level            | Example                                                 | Red Flags                                     |
|------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| Informational     | “Did we explain what’s happening and why?”             | People nod silently but ask no questions      |
| Meaningful        | “Did people feel safe enough to say no?”               | Consent given quickly or hesitantly           |
| Ongoing           | “Are we checking in and updating as plans evolve?”     | Plans shift without telling affected folks    |
| Collective        | “Did the right people/groups help shape this?”         | Only leadership consulted, not base community |
| Language Access   | “Is this info available in the languages people speak?”| Consent forms are only in English             |

It’s not about **getting a signature**. It’s about **honoring boundaries** throughout every stage of action.

---

## Context Is Everything

Before acting, ask:

- What’s the **history** of this issue here?
- Who has been **most impacted** and how have they responded?
- Are we acting **with**, not for, the community?
- Are we replicating **outside saviorism** or extractive attention?

---

## Examples of Violating Consent

| Action Taken                            | Without Consent                       | Harm Caused                                   |
|----------------------------------------|---------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| Filming a raid or protest              | No input from affected families       | Trauma, doxxing, state retaliation            |
| Organizing a vigil in someone's name   | No family/community consultation      | Undermines healing, retraumatization          |
| Launching aid or patrols               | Without local group involvement       | Confusion, duplication, resentment            |

---

## Practicing Respectful Solidarity

Respecting consent means:

- **Listening first** – Don’t rush to act
- **Asking permission** – Not just informing, but inviting participation
- **Deferring leadership** – Uplift those closest to the issue
- **Being accountable** – Own mistakes, even when unintentional

---

## Tools for Practicing Consent

- **Community briefings** – Gather input before launching projects
- **Advisory councils** – Include impacted folks in planning
- **Consent checkpoints** – Pause during actions to re-evaluate support
- **Language access** – Ensure people understand before giving consent
- **Power mapping** – Identify who is most affected vs. most visible

---

## Consent in ICE Tea Actions

In the ICE Tea ecosystem, community consent shapes:

- Whether we respond to a report or stand down
- Who leads a field team (must reflect local dynamics)
- What data we gather and how it's used
- How we document public actions or raids

<Callout type="success">
No one is owed someone else’s story, struggle, or participation. Trust must be earned—and maintained.
</Callout>

---

## When Consent Is Withheld

If a family, community, or affinity group says:

- “Please don’t show up”
- “Don’t film here”
- “We’re not ready”

Then we **stop**. No justification—urgency, optics, or media—outweighs a **direct refusal**.

<Callout type="warning">
Violating community consent in ICE Tea can lead to removal from dispatch roles, retraining, or permanent deactivation.
</Callout>

---

## Building Cultures of Consent

- Normalize asking: “Is this okay with you?”
- Welcome feedback—even if it’s a hard no
- Teach newcomers how to engage respectfully
- Reflect often on **whose consent we forgot to seek**

> “Nothing about us without us” applies **at all levels** of action and decision-making.

## Practice Scenarios

1. A family is being targeted by ICE. You want to organize a rally in front of their home.  
   ❓ What do you do first?

2. You’re coordinating aid at a site where people are hesitant to share names or locations.  
   ❓ How do you respect their agency while offering help?

3. A trusted org asks for no documentation at an event. A volunteer starts filming.  
   ❓ What is your role in upholding that boundary?

4. A Cantonese-speaking elder says “do what you think is best” during a planning meeting.  
   ❓ What cultural norms might be influencing their response? How can you follow up respectfully?

## Reflection Questions

- Have I ever acted without full community consent? What happened?
- What’s the difference between “informed” and “meaningful” consent?
- What would change in our group if we slowed down and asked more?

---

## Consequences for Violations

**ICE Tea Consent Violations Policy**

- First violation: Mandatory retraining + community-informed apology
- Repeated violations: Suspension from field roles
- Egregious harm (e.g., doxxing, non-consensual documentation): Permanent deactivation
