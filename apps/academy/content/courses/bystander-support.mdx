# Bystander Support Tactics
<TrackBadge variant="community-care" />
<TrackBadge variant="field-safety" />

Bystander intervention can prevent escalation, reduce harm, and support community safety. This course teaches you how to safely recognize, assess, and respond to harassment, profiling, or threats—especially targeting immigrants, Black and Brown communities, and queer and trans folks.

---

<Callout type="info">
Your presence matters. You don’t have to be an expert to make a difference. You just need awareness, courage, and a plan.
</Callout>
---
<Callout type="rabbit-hole">
In 2022, bystander footage led to the release of a wrongly detained immigrant in Texas. Your role *can* change outcomes.
</Callout>
---
<Callout type="warning">
⚠️ Documenting ICE presence can carry legal risks. Know your local laws before filming or intervening directly.
</Callout>

## Understand the Context

Bystander intervention is grounded in mutual protection. Harm doesn't always look dramatic — it can be subtle, sustained, or systemic. Microaggressions, targeting, and intimidation thrive in silence.

People who are undocumented, non-English speaking, or part of historically criminalized groups are often less able to safely advocate for themselves. That’s where prepared bystanders come in.

---

## Normalize Barriers to Action

We all carry fears, trauma, responsibilities, or identities that may shape how we respond. That’s normal. That’s *wise*. And it doesn’t make you a bad ally.

What matters is knowing **what’s realistic for *you*** — and practicing how to act within that zone.

<Callout type="hint">
You don’t have to do everything. But everyone can do *something*.
</Callout>

---

## Your Priorities as a Bystander

1. **Center the needs and safety of the targeted person**
2. **Avoid escalating the situation**
3. **Use your presence to shift power**
4. **Document or delegate if direct action isn’t possible**
5. **Support after the incident, not just during**

---

## The 5Ds Framework

These five approaches are your tactical toolkit. You don’t need to use all of them — pick what’s safest and most effective in the moment.

| Tactic    | Best For                         | Risk Level | Example                             |
|-----------|----------------------------------|------------|-------------------------------------|
| Direct    | Clear, safe confrontations       | High       | "That’s racist. Stop."              |
| Distract  | Public places, de-escalation     | Low        | "Excuse me, is this your wallet?"   |
| Document  | Evidence gathering with consent  | Medium     | Record with narration and location  |
| Delegate  | Crowd support or staff escalation| Low        | “Can you go check in on them?”      |
| Delay     | Post-incident solidarity         | None       | “That looked hard. Can I help you?” |

---

### Direct

Calmly call out harmful behavior:

> “That’s not okay.”  
> “Leave them alone.”

**Only use this if:**
- You feel safe
- You are not outnumbered
- The situation won’t escalate by your involvement

Use firm, short statements. Don’t insult or try to overpower the aggressor.

---

### Distract

Interrupt without confrontation:

- Ask for directions  
- Drop something  
- Say: “Hey, don’t I know you?”

<Callout type="hint">
Distraction works best in public places where attention can deter escalation.
</Callout>

---

### Document

Record the incident only if:
- You feel safe  
- The targeted person consents  
- You clearly show the scene and incident  

**Tips:**
- Use landscape mode  
- Narrate the time, place, and action:  
  > “ICE stop, outside McDonald’s, Portland OR, 3:15pm”  
- Use secure apps like **Signal** for sharing

### What to Do With Footage

- ✅ **Upload photos** to your local **Dispatch report** if you're responding
- 📹 **Videos cannot be uploaded to Dispatch yet** — instead:
  - Store videos in a **secure, encrypted folder** (e.g., Proton Drive, Tresorit)  
  - Use **Signal** to send it to a trusted organizer, legal observer, or dispatch lead  
  - Never keep sensitive footage on shared or cloud-synced devices without encryption

<Callout type="info">
Video footage can be powerful—but also risky. Never share publicly without consent. Blur faces or identifying features before uploading anywhere.
</Callout>
---
<Callout type="hint">
Need to submit video? Contact your local dispatch lead or safety coordinator. Let them know you have footage, and ask for a secure handoff method.
</Callout>

---

### Delegate

You don’t have to act alone. Involve others when it helps increase safety or visibility:

- “Can you go check in on them?”  
- “Could you call someone while I stay here?”  
- “Let’s make sure they’re not alone.”

Look for people who can help:
- Store employees  
- Transit operators  
- Legal observers or organizers  

If someone is being detained or harmed, **don’t wait for consent to report** — it may not be safe or possible for them to ask for help.

---

### Delay

If you missed the moment to act, it’s still not too late.

Say:
> “That looked really hard. Can I help?”  
> “Do you want someone to walk with you?”

Offer water, support, or a moment to breathe.

## Cultural Awareness & Trauma-Informed Practice

Different settings require different approaches:

- Direct action might escalate with police or ICE present  
- Language barriers may require **simpler phrases** or **nonverbal communication**  
- Be mindful of your **tone, volume, and body language** — calm energy lowers threat  
- Sometimes, *stepping back* is the safest, kindest move

## What *Not* to Do

- **Don’t film without purpose or consent**
- **Don’t speak for someone else**
- **Don’t argue just to win**
- **Don’t escalate if you can avoid it**
- **Don’t ignore your own limits**

---

## Scripts for Action

**Direct**  
- “Hey, stop. That’s not okay.”  
- “That’s racist/sexist. Cut it out.”

**Supportive**  
- “Are you okay?”  
- “Do you want me to stay with you?”  
- “You didn’t deserve that.”

**Delegate**  
- “Can you go check on them while I stay here?”  
- “Could you get staff while I keep watch?”

## Final Notes

Bystander support isn’t just about stepping in. It’s about shifting culture, building safety, and refusing to look away. You don’t need to be perfect—just present, prepared, and willing to act.
