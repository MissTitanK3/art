<Callout type="warn">
🚨 Most risks in activist organizing are **not individual — they’re collective**. One slip-up can expose a whole pod. This course shows how to create habits, roles, and boundaries that **lower group risk and prevent burnout or compromise**.
</Callout>

---

## 🧠 Why This Matters

It’s not enough for one person to be careful. We need a culture where:

- Nobody becomes the **single point of failure**  
- People don’t overexpose themselves out of urgency  
- Everyone knows what to do when a mistake happens  

Good security isn’t about paranoia. It’s about **protecting each other long-term**.

---

## 🧰 What You’ll Learn

- What “shared exposure” means and how to prevent it  
- When to separate roles (e.g. OTG vs dispatcher)

## 📡 Shared Exposure: The Real Risk

| Scenario | Exposure Risk |
|----------|----------------|
| A dispatcher is also on the ground at actions | Can link digital and physical identities |
| Signal chats mix trained + new members | Increases leak potential |
| A pod only uses one tool or comms method | Creates single point of failure |
| Roles aren't clearly defined | Everyone assumes someone else is watching OPSEC |

🧠 **Shared exposure = When one person’s mistake or visibility increases risk for others.**

---

## 🧱 Baseline Safety Protocols

### 🧍 Role Separation

| Role             | Can Be Done Simultaneously? |
|------------------|------------------------------|
| OTG Field Lead + Dispatcher | ❌ Never |
| Media Poster + Dispatch Coordinator | ⚠️ Only with prior review |
| Pod Coordinator + Watch Reporter | ✅ Usually okay |
| Tech Maintainer + Onsite Comms | ✅ If using secure setups |

💡 **Set hard boundaries**: no person should have access to *all* roles or systems.

---

### 🔁 Exposure Rotation

- Switch who speaks publicly every few weeks  
- Use team/role aliases instead of real names  
- Rotate Signal accounts or handle name (if safe to do)  
- Don’t let the same person always deliver flyers, give speeches, and run comms

📌 **You don’t get points for doing everything. You get risk.**

---

### 📵 Device Hygiene

| Rule | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| Leave phones off during ops unless needed | Prevent passive tracking |
| Use burner phones for dispatch | Isolate presence from identity |
| Never log into multiple roles on one device | Prevents cross-contamination |
| Clear caches and logs weekly | Reduce traceable data |

<Callout type="hint">
Use app cloners or parallel spaces if you must manage multiple Signal IDs — but prefer **physical separation** when possible.
</Callout>

---

## ⚠️ What To Do When Things Go Wrong

### If someone is arrested:
- Remove their access to dispatch tools  
- Assume their phone is compromised  
- Tell their pod NOT to speak about events digitally  
- Wait for **legal support** before resuming related ops  

### If a leak happens (intentional or not):
- Assume all linked members may now be known  
- Pause ops for 24–72 hours for review  
- Rotate tools and accounts  
- Debrief only in secure, preferably offline ways  

<Callout type="warn">
DO NOT “just keep going.” Reassess, regroup, resecure.
</Callout>

## 📋 Minimum Standards for Every Pod

| Category           | Protocol                                                             |
|--------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Onboarding         | New members must pass Level 1 training or enter as Field Support     |
| Roles              | Each person may hold ONE active role per op                          |
| Signal Use         | Use aliases, no faces/names in avatars, archive inactive groups      |
| Offline Sync       | Use printed flyers, intake forms, and verbal updates weekly          |
| Emergency Plans    | Each pod must have at least one: arrest plan, contact tree, retreat site |
| Dispatcher Safety  | Dispatchers should not be on the ground within 72 hours of a major action |

## 🔐 The “Need To Know” Model

Adopt a mindset where people only access the information **they need to do their role** — nothing more.

| Role | Needs To Know |
|------|----------------|
| Outreach volunteer | Area of operation, pod name |
| Pod lead | Volunteer names + safety info |
| Dispatcher | Pod locations (coded), signal handle only |
| Public comms | Summary of ops, no sensitive info |

🧠 This limits the blast radius of any breach.

---

## 🗂️ Tracking Risk Levels (Optional)

Assign roles or pods a **risk tier**:

| Tier | Description                     | Example |
|------|----------------------------------|---------|
| 🔵 Low | No sensitive data, no field ops | Print team |
| 🟡 Medium | Infrequent OTG or tech use | Pod creator |
| 🔴 High | Access to backend, dispatch, or OTG leadership | Core dispatcher |

Match countermeasures to risk tier.

## 🧪 Practice Scenario

🎯 _A new pod member shows up to a field op with their unlocked personal phone. They take a group selfie and post it to Instagram with the caption: “Ready to protect our community 💪 #dispatchteam #ICETeaPod”_

✅ Protocol Response:
1. Remove post immediately  
2. Conduct gentle but serious debrief  
3. Offer OPSEC re-training or pause their field access  
4. Review pod’s onboarding gaps  
5. Have a pod lead file an internal incident log

---

## 📓 Sample Pod Safety Policy (Editable)

> All members agree to:
> - Use only aliases or first names
> - Rotate roles every 2–4 weeks
> - Keep devices separate from fieldwork
> - Avoid public statements without pod review
> - Report safety concerns early — no blame, just safety

You can expand this into a full pod charter or internal safety pledge.
