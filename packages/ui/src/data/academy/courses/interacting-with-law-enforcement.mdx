# Interacting with Law Enforcement
<TrackBadge variant="observation-legal" />
<TrackBadge variant="field-safety" />

Dealing with police or ICE during direct action, rapid response, or community defense situations can be one of the most stressful and high-risk moments a person may face. This lesson gives you the tools to respond calmly, legally, and strategically — whether you're being approached, witnessing an encounter, or acting in a support role.

<Callout type="info">
⚖️ This training focuses on U.S. law. Always verify your local or state-specific regulations and consult legal observers when possible.
</Callout>

<Callout type="hint">
Ground yourself in <a href="/academy/kyr-basics">Know Your Rights (KYR) Basics</a> and <a href="/academy/fourth-amendment-rights">Fourth Amendment Rights</a>. For documentation safety and retention, see <a href="/academy/documentation-safety">Documentation & Safety</a> and <a href="/academy/after-action-data-hygiene">After‑Action Data Hygiene</a>.
</Callout>

> 📊 **85% of rights violations occur when individuals don’t know how to respond.**

---

## Core Principles

- **Stay calm and grounded.** Slow breath, steady posture, and non-threatening body language.
- **Know your rights.** Clear, lawful responses prevent manipulation.
- **De-escalate.** Your presence is to defuse, not provoke.
- **Support quietly.** Center those most vulnerable—don’t draw attention to them.
- **Document responsibly.** Videos and notes may become legal evidence.

---

## Know Your Rights

| Right                     | Description                                                  |
|--------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Remain silent**         | You do not have to answer questions from police or ICE.      |
| **Attorney access**       | Ask for one and stop talking until they arrive.              |
| **Refuse search**         | You do not have to allow searches of your bag, phone, car.   |
| **Right to film**         | You may record public officials in public spaces.            |
| **Right to leave**        | Ask: *“Am I being detained, or am I free to go?”*            |

<Callout type="warning">
Asserting your rights does not always protect you from harm. Always assess the risk and your environment.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info">
Language access matters. If interpretation is needed to assert rights, coordinate with volunteers who hold the <a href="/academy/translator-micro-badge">Translator Micro‑badge</a> or <a href="/academy/asl-interpreter-micro-badge">ASL Interpreter Micro‑badge</a>.
</Callout>

## ICE vs Police

| Agency | Purpose                 | Tactics Used                    | Power Limits |
|--------|-------------------------|----------------------------------|--------------|
| ICE    | Immigration enforcement | Surveillance, raids, traffic stops | Cannot enter homes without a **judicial** warrant |
| Police | Criminal enforcement    | Arrests, patrols, community stops | Must have reasonable suspicion to detain |

---

## Warrant Types

### Comparison Table

| Type | Judicial Warrant | Administrative Warrant |
|------|------------------|------------------------|
| Authority | Signed by a judge | Issued by ICE |
| Legal Power | Grants entry to homes | Does **not** grant entry |
| Compliance | Legally required | **Not** required |

📌 **Always ask:** “Can you slide the warrant under the door?”

---

## If You’re Approached

- Breathe. Don’t run or argue.
- Ask: *“Am I being detained?”*
  - If **yes**: Stay silent. Ask for an attorney.
  - If **no**: Walk away calmly.
- Say: *“I do not consent to a search.”*
- Don’t reach into bags or pockets suddenly.

<Callout type="success">
You are not required to show ID unless you are driving or in a state with stop-and-identify laws.
</Callout>

---

## If You Witness an Encounter

- Start recording **from a safe distance**.
- Narrate facts: time, location, agent description.
- Do **not** intervene physically unless there's **immediate** danger.
- Use coded alerts with your team (e.g., “Blue umbrellas up”).

---

## De-escalation Techniques

### Phrases

- “I’m just observing for everyone’s safety.”
- “We’re staying calm and legal here.”
- “Am I free to go?”
- “I do not consent to a search.”

### Non-Verbal Cues

- Keep palms visible and relaxed.
- Avoid sudden movements.
- Step slowly, speak quietly.

<Callout type="tip">
Avoid inflammatory phrases like “You’re violating my rights.” Stay clear, not confrontational.
</Callout>

---

## Role-Based Approaches

### 👁️ Legal Observers

- Don’t chant or protest.
- Wear ID if possible.
- Record details: badge numbers, agent names, timestamps.

### 🛡️ Field Support (Marshals, Care Teams)

- Use steady voice and body posture.
- Offer grounding reminders: “Let’s breathe, you’re not alone.”
- Create gentle physical buffers if safe.

### 📷 Runners or Techs

- Use encrypted storage or burner phones.
- Upload footage immediately.
- Share with dispatch if needed—never social media.

<Callout type="info">
Handle media carefully: minimize faces/identifiers; use encrypted channels; prefer photos over video, and for video share a secure link (no direct uploads). See <a href="/academy/documentation-safety">Documentation & Safety</a> and <a href="/academy/after-action-data-hygiene">After‑Action Data Hygiene</a>.
</Callout>

### 🩺 Medic Protocols

- Ask first: “Are you hurt? Do you need help?”
- Look for visible injuries or signs of distress.
- **Don’t touch anyone without consent** — unless they are unconscious or clearly unable to respond.
- If a person can't consent but appears seriously injured, use **medically implied consent** to provide aid.
- Document what you see — not what you assume.
- Report neglect, harm, or use-of-force injuries to legal observers or designated safety roles.

<Callout type="warning">
Always balance care with consent. Touch only when it’s asked for, welcomed, or necessary to prevent further harm.
</Callout>

## Raids and Home Visits

You are not required to open the door unless a **judicial warrant** is shown. ICE may attempt deception.

### Script for the Door

> "I do not consent to entry. Please slide the warrant under the door."

If they cannot show a valid warrant signed by a judge, do not open.

<Callout type="hint">
Harden devices and minimize on‑device data, especially near borders or when traveling. See <a href="/academy/digital-security-basics">Digital Security Basics</a> and <a href="/academy/burner-phones-security-culture">Burner Phones & Security Culture</a>.
</Callout>

## Grounding and Mental Support

If you feel panic, dissociation, or freeze:

- **Touch reality**: belt loop, sidewalk, necklace.
- **Use the 5-4-3-2-1 method**:  
  - 5 things you see  
  - 4 things you feel  
  - 3 things you hear  
  - 2 things you smell  
  - 1 thing you taste  
- **Slow breath mantra**: “I am here. I am safe.”

<Callout type="info">
It’s okay to step away and regroup. Safety includes mental well-being.
</Callout>

---

## If It Escalates

- Don’t run or fight.
- Say: “I do not consent. I want a lawyer.”
- Stay silent. Don’t sign anything.
- Memorize emergency contact or write it on your arm.

<Callout type="info">
If this happens during a dispatch, coordinate with <a href="/academy/responding-to-dispatch-calls">Responding to Dispatch Calls</a> and escalate via <a href="/academy/dispatch-coordination">Dispatch Coordination</a>. For arrests, route to <a href="/academy/court-support">Court Support</a> and <a href="/academy/legal-followthrough-jail-support">Legal Follow‑through & Jail Support</a>.
</Callout>

## Emergency Script

1. “I invoke my right to remain silent.”  
2. “I do not consent to a search.”  
3. Call: [Trusted Contact Name] at [Number]  
4. Repeat if needed.
