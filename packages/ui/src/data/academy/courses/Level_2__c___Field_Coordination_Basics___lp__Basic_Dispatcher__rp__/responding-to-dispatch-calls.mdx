## Overview

Dispatch calls connect trained volunteers to verified incidents.  
Responding quickly—but safely—helps protect communities facing enforcement or distress events.  
This lesson covers **what to expect, how to respond, and when to hold back.**

---

## Learn & Do

### What a Dispatch Call Looks Like

A dispatch call is a **verified alert** for trusted volunteers. It may include:

- ICE or law enforcement presence  
- Community support requests  
- Reports of detention or surveillance  
- Calls for legal observers, care, or logistics support  

Typical message:

```
🚨 CALL FOR COMMUNITY SUPPORT 🚨

📍 Location: [Area or intersection]
📋 Action: [Roles needed or support requested]
🧠 Notes: [Short summary or context]
🕓 Timing: [Now / 30 min / tonight]
📲 Reply in Signal thread if available
```

Never repost, screenshot, or forward calls outside verified Signal threads.

---

### How to Respond

1. ✅ **Reply promptly** (or mark availability through link):  
   > “Available. Can arrive in 20 min.”  
2. 🧠 **State your role or limits:**  
   > “Can do Legal Observing, no transport.”  
3. 🚙 **Coordinate quietly:**  
   Discuss meet-up zones in private threads.  
4. 🤫 **Keep chatter minimal:**  
   No emojis, reactions, or off-topic replies.

<Callout type="info">
You are never obligated to respond. Consent, safety, and readiness always come first.
</Callout>

---

### If You Can’t Go

Respond clearly so others can plan:
- “Unavailable but monitoring.”  
- “Out of range—can log data cleanup later.”  
- “Can coordinate remotely if needed.”

This avoids confusion and reduces false coverage assumptions.

---

### Preparing for a Call-Up

Keep a **go kit** ready:

- 🧥 Weather-ready clothing  
- 📱 Signal device + power bank  
- 🧾 Notepad or encrypted note app  
- 🧃 Water, snacks, first-aid basics  
- 🪪 KYR card or volunteer badge  

Checklist before leaving:
- Signal name matches your alias  
- Dispatch alerts are unmuted  
- You’ve reviewed area map and exits  

---

### Trust & Coverage

Only trusted Level 2+ volunteers receive dispatch activations.  
Expect to:
- Work solo, in pairs, or small teams  
- Report presence briefly (“👀 2 blocks east”)  
- Rotate after ~2 hours for safety and clarity  
- Log any observations securely after

Always protect consent, avoid escalation, and follow pod lead instructions.

---

### When *Not* to Respond

Skip the call if:
- You feel unstable or exhausted  
- You’re unsure of legality or readiness  
- You’re in a vulnerable area  
- You can’t travel safely  

<Callout type="warning">
A shaky responder can endanger others. Clarity beats urgency.
</Callout>

---

### After the Call

Once clear of the site:
1. ✅ Mark yourself as “clear” in the thread.  
2. 📝 Log key notes or timestamps securely.  
3. 💬 Send a short debrief to dispatch lead if needed.  
4. 🩵 Ground yourself: eat, hydrate, rest, and decompress.

<Callout type="hint">
Documentation hygiene: Use minimal, time-bound retention.  
Delete sensitive logs after review. Never store raw dispatch threads in shared apps.
</Callout>
