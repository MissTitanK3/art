# 🤟 ASL Interpreter Micro-Badge
<TrackBadge variant="community-care" />
<TrackBadge variant="field-safety" />
<TrackBadge variant="asl" />

<Callout type="success">
**Key Principle:** You provide **access, not advocacy**.  
Your mission is clarity, neutrality, and confidentiality — ensuring DHH (Deaf or hard-of-hearing) participants receive equal, accurate information.
</Callout>

---

## Why It Matters

DHH participants are often excluded from live updates or legal briefings.  
ASL interpreters make mutual aid work **inclusive, compliant, and safer** under stress.  
During actions, access can save lives.

---

## 1. Environmental Adaptation

| Environment | Technique | Risk Control |
|--------------|------------|---------------|
| Low Light | Exaggerate expressions, use gentle light or tactile signs | Avoid blinding others |
| Loud Area | Stand between speaker and DHH partner | Maintain clear visual line |
| Crowded | Use discreet markers (blue band, sleeve tag) | Conceal when threat increases |

<Callout type="warning">
Never interpret while in motion or under physical duress.  
Pause, stabilize, then resume.
</Callout>

---

## 2. Legal & Crisis Vocabulary

Memorize and drill key action phrases:

```
"Detained" (not "arrested")
"You have the right to remain silent"
"Medical attention needed?"
"Stay calm — follow us"
```

<Callout type="hint">
Download an **offline ASL legal glossary**.  
Study with:  
- [Interacting with Law Enforcement](/courses/interacting-with-law-enforcement)  
- [Court Support](/courses/court-support)
</Callout>

---

## 3. Team Coordination

Establish pre-agreed hand cues:

| Signal | Meaning |
|---------|----------|
| 🖐️ | Pause interpretation (threat nearby) |
| ✊ | Switch to tactile signing |
| 🤙 | Request backup interpreter |

Rotate interpreters every **20–30 minutes** for clarity and safety.

---

## 4. Positioning Guide

| Scenario | Ideal Position | Risk Mitigation |
|-----------|----------------|-----------------|
| Protest Street | 45° angle to both parties | Avoid direct police view |
| Legal Briefing | 3–5 ft in front of speaker | Ensure clear line of sight |
| Evacuation | Tactile signing if visual lost | Pre-plan contact method |

<Callout type="info">
Use **black gloves** or contrast sleeves for low-light visibility.  
Maintain **eye-level** consistency — not higher or lower than DHH partner.
</Callout>

---

## 5. Certification Requirements

- ≥ 90 % accuracy in ASL crisis drills  
- Demonstrated field simulation under stress  
- Signed Ethics Pledge:

> “I will interpret accurately and neutrally.  
> I will maintain confidentiality.  
> I will switch out when fatigued or unsafe.”

---

## 6. Field Kit

- 🧤 Black gloves (contrast)  
- 📝 Mini notepad (backup comms)  
- 🩹 Tactile patch or wrist marker  
- 🔦 Soft flashlight (diffused)  

<Callout type="hint">
Learn **regional ASL variants** and **protest-specific signs** such as “riot,” “police line,” and “move back.”
</Callout>

---

## 7. Role-Specific Risks

| Risk | Prevention |
|------|-------------|
| Mistaken as leader | Remove identifiers when inactive |
| Legal phrasing drift | Align with legal observers pre-action |
| Fatigue or sensory overload | Enforce 20–30 min rotation |
| Interference from media | Shield DHH participant’s privacy |

---

## 8. Quick Ethics Reference

✅ Do:  
- Match tone, not opinion  
- Keep confidentiality absolute  
- Clarify missed content privately  

❌ Don’t:  
- Improvise or editorialize  
- Speak for participants  
- Record without explicit consent
