# 🫱🏽‍🫲🏾 Community Consent
<TrackBadge variant="movement-strategy" />
<TrackBadge variant="community-care" />

<Callout type="info">
Use clear, approachable language. Keep steps small and predictable.  
Consent is not just personal — it's collective. Community consent respects the agency, safety, and self-determination of entire groups when acting in solidarity or resistance.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
Community consent means **seeking permission and participation** from those most affected before taking action in their name or space.  
It ensures that support work strengthens, not overrides, local leadership.

Without consent, well-intentioned efforts can reproduce harm, attract surveillance, or erode trust.  
This course provides frameworks and scenarios to help organizers act **with**, not **for**, communities.

---

## Learn & Do

### 1. What Is Community Consent?
**Community consent** involves:
- Seeking approval from directly impacted people.  
- Centering collective will, not just individual decisions.  
- Understanding the histories, traumas, and boundaries of a place.  

**Ask:** “Who is affected — and did they ask for this?”

---

### 2. Why It Matters
Acting without consent can:
- Recreate harm or trauma.  
- Disempower local organizers.  
- Expose vulnerable people to retaliation.  
- Damage long-term trust networks.

<Callout type="success">
✅ Example: In 2023, a Portland coalition postponed a protest after migrant families requested discretion—showing community consent in action.
</Callout>

---

### 3. Levels of Consent

| Level | Example | Red Flags |
|--------|----------|-----------|
| Informational | “Did we explain what’s happening and why?” | People nod silently, unclear about details |
| Meaningful | “Do people feel safe to say no?” | Consent given quickly or under pressure |
| Ongoing | “Are we updating as plans change?” | Plans shift without informing affected people |
| Collective | “Did the right people help shape this?” | Only leadership consulted, not the base |
| Language Access | “Is this in their language?” | Materials only in English |

Consent is **continuous**, not a one-time approval.

---

### 4. Context and Power
Before acting, always ask:
- What’s the **history** of this issue here?  
- Who has been **most affected**, and how?  
- Are we acting **with** the community, or **for** them?  
- Are we replicating **saviorism** or extraction?

---

### 5. Examples of Violating Consent

| Action | Without Consent | Harm Caused |
|---------|-----------------|--------------|
| Filming a raid | No family consultation | Trauma, doxxing, retaliation |
| Organizing a vigil | No community input | Re-traumatization |
| Launching patrols | No local coordination | Confusion, resentment |

---

### 6. Practicing Respectful Solidarity
- **Listen first** — Don’t rush to act.  
- **Ask permission**, not forgiveness.  
- **Defer leadership** to locals.  
- **Stay accountable** when mistakes happen.

**Tools**
- Community briefings  
- Advisory councils  
- Consent checkpoints  
- Power mapping  
- Translation and interpretation

### 7. Consent in ART Actions
In the Always Ready Tools network:
- Dispatchers act only with community approval.  
- Field leadership must reflect local dynamics.  
- Data collection is minimal, consent-based, and time-limited.  
- Documentation requires explicit permission.  

<Callout type="success">
No one is owed someone else’s story, struggle, or participation. Trust must be earned—and maintained.
</Callout>

<Callout type="hint">
Before acting, align with [Responding to Dispatch Calls](/courses/responding-to-dispatch-calls), [Care & Emotional Support](/courses/care-support), and [After-Action Data Hygiene](/courses/after-action-data-hygiene).
</Callout>

### 8. When Consent Is Withheld
If a community or family says:
- “Please don’t show up.”  
- “Don’t film here.”  
- “We’re not ready.”  

✅ **Stop.** Urgency or optics never override refusal.

<Callout type="warning">
Violating consent can result in retraining, suspension, or removal from ART roles.
</Callout>

<Callout type="important">
When consent intersects with legal risk, coordinate with [Court Support](/courses/court-support) and [Legal Follow-through & Jail Support](/courses/legal-followthrough-jail-support).  
For policy references, see [Admin Tools & Access Roles](/courses/admin-tools).
</Callout>

---

### 9. Building Cultures of Consent
- Normalize asking “Is this okay with you?”  
- Welcome feedback, even when it’s a “no.”  
- Teach newcomers to engage respectfully.  
- Reflect on **whose consent** was overlooked.

> “Nothing about us without us” applies to every level of decision-making.

---

### 10. Practice Scenarios
1. A family under ICE threat requests privacy — do you rally or pause?  
2. Aid site participants fear exposure — how do you adapt?  
3. An org bans documentation — how do you enforce that?  
4. A non-English-speaking elder defers — how do you confirm informed consent?  

Use these for pod discussion or role-play.

---

<Callout type="success">
**Certification readiness:**  
Be able to explain the five levels of consent, identify red flags, and respond appropriately when consent is unclear or denied.
</Callout>
