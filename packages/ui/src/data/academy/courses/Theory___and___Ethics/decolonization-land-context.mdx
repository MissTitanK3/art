## Overview
Most mutual aid and organizing work happens on **stolen Indigenous land**.  
Ignoring that context harms trust and legitimacy.  
Decolonization means shifting from symbolic gestures to **material solidarity** — supporting Indigenous sovereignty, land defense, and leadership in ongoing struggles.

This lesson helps pods recognize colonial histories, assess land context, and build relationships rooted in respect and repair.

---

## Learn & Do

### 1. Solidarity Framework

#### Research (Before You Act)
- Use [Native-Land.ca](https://native-land.ca) to identify nations and treaties.  
- Review tribal websites for current campaigns and land defense efforts.  
- Check local archives for treaty violations or ongoing disputes.

#### Relationship (Before You Speak)
- Offer labor before asks: “Can our pod assist with your next supply run?”  
- Follow communication protocols — contact councils where required.  
- Compensate Indigenous advisors; avoid free emotional labor.

#### Redistribution (Beyond Symbolism)

| Symbolic Gesture | Material Action |
|------------------|-----------------|
| Land acknowledgment at events | Donate 1% of pod funds to a land tax or Indigenous org |
| Solidarity graphics | Amplify Indigenous campaigns directly |
| “Decolonization” rhetoric | Share physical resources — storage, space, or supplies |

---

### 2. Pod Decision-Making Audit
Ask before major actions:
- Who historically controlled this land?  
- Who is most impacted now?  
- Have we consulted them, and with consent?  
- Are we deferring leadership appropriately?

<Callout type="warning">
Never demand Indigenous leaders “educate” you for free.  
Bring specific offers: “Would volunteer drivers or supply runs be most helpful?”
</Callout>

<Callout type="hint">
See [Community Consent](/courses/community-consent) and [Documentation Safety](/courses/documentation-safety) to protect privacy in communications and documentation.
</Callout>

### 3. Land & Migrant Justice Intersections
Key overlaps:
- Border walls desecrate sacred sites (e.g., Tohono O’odham Nation).  
- ICE sometimes partners with tribal police.  
- Many Indigenous people are also migrants (e.g., Maya communities crossing borders).  

Solidarity actions:
- Provide bilingual legal observers at Indigenous-led protests.  
- Coordinate water or supply drops for desert camps.  
- Support Indigenous border communities resisting surveillance.

### 4. Harm Reduction & Repair

| Mistake | Repair Approach |
|----------|----------------|
| Spoke for a nation without consent | Publicly correct and cede platform |
| Used cultural imagery without permission | Remove materials, donate to affected community |
| Ignored treaty or land rights | Pause plans, consult elders, adjust accordingly |

---

### 5. Decolonial Pod Practices
- **Meetings:** Acknowledge *current* struggles, not just historic ones.  
- **Resource allocation:** Prioritize Indigenous-led efforts before internal needs.  
- **Conflict resolution:** Use restorative circles over punitive measures.  
- **Accountability:** Document shared agreements respectfully and with consent.  

---

### 6. Checklist for Pods
- [ ] Researched ongoing Indigenous land struggles.  
- [ ] Compensated Indigenous partners.  
- [ ] Audited pod spaces for contested or sacred land.  
- [ ] Added Indigenous contacts (with consent) to emergency Signal groups.  

<Callout type="success">
**Solidarity is a verb.** Start small but concrete:  
Offer one vehicle, one space, or one budget share for Indigenous-led work.
</Callout>
