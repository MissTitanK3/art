## Overview
Pods often face funding instability and supply shortages. Many groups turn to foundations or corporations for help — but those sources often carry **conditions, data collection, or control mechanisms** that undermine independence.

**Solidarity economics** means organizing resources cooperatively through:
- Transparency instead of hierarchy  
- Mutual benefit instead of profit  
- Shared infrastructure instead of competition  

This ensures pods remain **autonomous, ethical, and trusted**.

## Learn & Do

### 1. Resource Mapping
Inventory your collective capacity before seeking new funds.

**Skills Inventory**
- List every member’s capabilities (medical, legal, mechanical, comms, facilitation, cooking).  
- Identify unused skills that could reduce expenses (e.g., a mechanic maintaining vehicles).  

**Asset Mapping**
- Track existing vehicles, tools, kitchens, radios, or storage.  
- Ask: *“What do we already have that we’re underusing?”*  

---

### 2. Funding Source Risk Assessment

| Source Type | Pros | Cons | Mitigation |
|--------------|------|------|-------------|
| **Grassroots Donations** | High autonomy | Unpredictable | Build sustaining donor circles |
| **Foundation Grants** | Large sums | Strings attached, heavy reporting | Use fiscal sponsor & clear boundaries |
| **Community Economies** | Local control, sustainable | Slower to grow | Pair with skill trades or events |
| **Corporate/Government** | Fast cash | Surveillance, public risk | Avoid unless emergency-critical |

---

### 3. Transparent Accounting
Transparency builds trust but must protect privacy.

- **Blind balance reporting:** Share total amounts without revealing vendors or names.  
- **Three-person rule:** Minimum two verifiers for every withdrawal.  
- **Quarterly public summaries:** Redact identities and sensitive info.  
- **Digital tools:**  
  - OpenCollective (public-facing transparency)  
  - Encrypted chat updates for internal reports  

<Callout type="hint">
Avoid spreadsheets in public clouds. Use encrypted local files or private servers with limited access.
</Callout>

---

### 4. Shared Infrastructure
Build mutual reliance instead of duplication.

**Equipment Sharing**
- Maintain logs, assign repair funds, clarify usage order.  

**Cross-Pod Resource Matrix**
- Identify who holds which vehicles, kitchens, or generators.  
- Create emergency access agreements to reduce downtime.

### 6. Quick Action Steps
1. Complete a **skills and asset inventory** before fundraising.  
2. Create **secure accounting with blind totals and multi-verifier control**.  
3. Build **regional equipment-sharing agreements**.  
4. Establish **at least one recurring local funding stream** (donor circles, workshops, trades).  

---

### 7. Risks & Red Lines
- Never let one person hold full control of pod funds.  
- Avoid sources that request sensitive member or location data.  
- Balance **transparency with OPSEC** — no detailed budgets online.  

---

### 8. Case Study
**Pod X:** Relied on unstable grants, faced burnout.  
Shifted to a **50-person donor circle** giving $20–50 monthly.  
Result: **$3,000/month in unrestricted, low-risk funds** and regained autonomy.
