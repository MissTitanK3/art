Every act of resistance carries risk. This course helps pods **assess and respond responsibly**, protecting participants, communities, and long-term trust.

## Overview
Acting without assessing risk can endanger everyone.  
Understanding risk helps you:
- Make informed decisions under pressure  
- Reduce harm to vulnerable people  
- Protect community credibility  
- Sustain resistance over time  

> The strongest teams aren’t the boldest — they’re the most prepared.

---

## Learn & Do

### 1. Types of Risk

| Type | Example | Mitigation |
|------|----------|-------------|
| **Physical** | Confrontation with ICE or police | Situational awareness, de-escalation |
| **Legal** | Arrest, surveillance, phone seizure | KYR training, legal observers, anonymity |
| **Emotional** | Witnessing trauma | Grounding techniques, buddy system |
| **Digital** | Device tracking, chat leaks | Signal, burner phones, E2E encryption |
| **Reputational** | Breaking trust with partners | Consent protocols, transparency |

---

### 2. Personal Risk Assessment
Before you act, ask:
1. Who am I? What visibility or privilege do I carry?  
2. What am I carrying? Any sensitive data or legal exposure?  
3. What am I entering? Volatile space or invited action?  
4. What’s my exit plan? Safe route and contact point?  
5. Who could be affected by me? Could my presence increase others’ risk?  

---

### 3. Collective Risk & Collective Care
Responsible teams plan together:
- 🔍 **Pre-brief:** Roles, maps, fallback points  
- 🧭 **Check-in:** Duress codes, comms tests  
- ✋ **Signal needs:** Hand or verbal cues  
- 🛠️ **Debrief:** Process, repair, learn  

<Callout type="success">
Teams that rehearse protocols recover faster when things go wrong.
</Callout>

---

### 4. When to Say “No”
Refusing unsafe action is leadership. Step back if:
- You’re unwell physically or emotionally  
- You haven’t trained for the role  
- You don’t understand the context  
- You’re not ready to accept the risk  

**“I’m not ready.”** is responsible — not selfish.

---

### 5. Safety by Role

| Role | Common Risks | Mitigation |
|------|---------------|-------------|
| Legal Observer | Intimidation, gear seizure | Vest, backup, secure note storage |
| Medic | Vicarious trauma, crowd panic | Buddy system, PFA training |
| De-escalator | Targeting, aggression | Partner work, exit strategy |
| Rideshare Coordinator | Tracking, compromised data | Pseudonyms, pre-verified lists |
| Media Support | Arrest, doxxing | Avoid livestreams, encrypt footage |

---

### 6. Worst-Case Scenarios

**If Arrested**
- Say only: *“I want a lawyer. I do not consent to a search.”*  
- Stay silent and don’t unlock your phone.  
- Contact legal support as soon as possible.  

**If Device Seized**
- Use full-disk encryption + auto-lock timers.  
- Disable biometrics before actions.  
- Wipe unneeded data or media.  

**If You Suspect Infiltration**
- Do not accuse publicly. Escalate privately.  
- Watch for inconsistent stories or coercive pressure.  
- Use internal channels, not open chats, to flag issues.

---

### 7. Scenario Practice
🧩 **At an ICE raid:**  
You’re de-escalation certified. A family member is panicking.  
✅ Correct: Support and ground the family first. Calm presence protects.  

📸 **Someone is filming faces:**  
✅ Correct: Warn privately about doxxing risk. Respect autonomy but protect safety.

---

### 8. Preparation Checklist
- [ ] Knows personal role and risk  
- [ ] Cleared unnecessary data from device  
- [ ] Shared emergency contact plan  
- [ ] Packed essentials (mask, ID, legal number)  
- [ ] Has check-in time post-action  
- [ ] Practiced safety protocol this month  

---

### 9. Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|------|----------|
| **E2E Encryption** | “End-to-end,” only sender and recipient can read messages |
| **Duress Word** | Code that discreetly signals distress |
| **KYR Training** | Know Your Rights legal training |
| **Metadata** | Hidden data (e.g., GPS tags in photos) |
| **Doxxing** | Public exposure of private info |
