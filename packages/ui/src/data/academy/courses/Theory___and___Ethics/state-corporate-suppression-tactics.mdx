## Overview
Social movements are often targeted by **state and corporate suppression**: infiltration, predictive policing, surveillance partnerships, and online manipulation.  
Understanding these tactics allows pods to respond strategically without panic or infighting.

This course connects lessons from **COINTELPRO** and **modern corporate counter-organizing** to everyday risk management.

---

## Learn & Do

### 1. Historical Lessons
- **1960s (COINTELPRO):** Infiltration, fake letters, rumor campaigns.  
- **2000s (Fusion Centers):** Shared data between federal, state, and private actors.  
- **2020s:** Predictive policing, facial recognition, and corporate astroturfing.  

Patterns repeat: divide, discredit, destabilize.

---

### 2. Recognizing Common Tactics

**Infiltration & Provocateurs**
- Push risky or illegal actions prematurely.  
- Demand access to logistics or finances.  
- Create interpersonal tension or rumor cycles.  

**Surveillance Signs**
- Unfamiliar vehicles near recurring meeting sites.  
- Individuals photographing participants without reason.  
- Sudden new online “friends” with vague profiles.  

**Digital Manipulation**
- Bot networks amplifying internal conflict.  
- Fake screenshots or DMs shared to sow distrust.  
- Targeted doxxing or phishing against visible members.  

---

### 3. Structured Response Protocols

| Behavior | Possible Cause | Action |
|-----------|----------------|--------|
| New member asks about safehouses | Naive curiosity or probing | Limit access; monitor quietly |
| Strange vehicle near meetings | Coincidence or stakeout | Log plate numbers; vary routes |
| Person pushes property damage | Provocateur or poor judgment | Separate; escalate to leads |

**Controlled Burn Technique**
- Introduce harmless, false details to suspected channels.  
- Observe if information spreads externally.  
- Never confront directly without group consensus.  

<Callout type="hint">
Suspicion is data, not proof. Always collect, document, and confirm before acting.
</Callout>

---

### 4. Digital Threat Mitigation
- Use **encrypted tools** (Signal, Matrix) — never SMS or email for sensitive info.  
- Use **VPNs and airplane mode** when not communicating.  
- Scrub photos with tools like **ObscuraCam**.  
- Separate **personal and organizing accounts** completely.

### 6. Quick Action Steps
1. Verify identity before assigning sensitive roles.  
2. Use **compartmentalization** — share only what’s needed.  
3. Keep **secure incident logs** (offline or encrypted).  
4. Escalate quietly — never accuse publicly without evidence.  

---

### 7. Risks & Red Lines
- **Paranoia** can destroy trust faster than infiltration.  
- **Over-secrecy** isolates pods from allies.  
- **Public accusations** without verification endanger people and credibility.  

<Callout type="important">
Balance discretion with openness. The goal is resilience, not fear.
</Callout>

---

### 8. Scenario Drill
A new volunteer urges sabotage and photographs meetings.  
✅ Log behavior discreetly.  
✅ Restrict access to planning.  
✅ Bring documentation to pod leads.  
✅ Decide on next steps by consensus (controlled burn or removal).
