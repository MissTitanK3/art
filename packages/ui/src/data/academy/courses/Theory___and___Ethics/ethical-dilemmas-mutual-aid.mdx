## Overview
Mutual aid pods often face hard ethical choices:
- Who to prioritize when resources are limited.  
- When to risk escalation to protect others.  
- How to address internal harm or unsafe behavior.  

Without a shared process, these moments can divide pods or create further harm.  
This course builds collective clarity through frameworks, simulations, and reflection to align decisions with shared values.

---

## Learn & Do

### 1. Preparation
1. Read a short zine on mutual aid ethics.  
2. Reflect on one past dilemma your pod has faced.  
3. Identify your group’s core values (solidarity, safety, equity, etc.).  

<Callout type="info">
Include translators or interpreters when needed.  
See [Translator Micro-badge](/courses/translator-micro-badge) and [ASL Interpreter Micro-badge](/courses/asl-interpreter-micro-badge).
</Callout>

---

### 2. Core Learning Objectives
1. **Case Study Analysis:** Identify conflicting values in real-world scenarios.  
2. **Values Mapping:** Name the principles guiding each potential decision.  
3. **Consensus Tools:** Use quick, structured frameworks for urgent calls (dot-voting, modified consensus).  
4. **Accountability Practices:** Apply transformative justice and pod mapping to repair harm.  
5. **Muscle Memory:** Build familiarity through simulation and review.

<Callout type="hint">
Anchor all exercises to shared safety frameworks like [Community Consent](/courses/community-consent), [Data Ethics Pledge](/courses/data-ethics-pledge), [KYR Basics](/courses/kyr-basics), [De-escalation Basics](/courses/deescalation-basics), and [Bystander Support](/courses/bystander-support).
</Callout>

---

### 3. Case Study Practice
Use one or two dilemmas per meeting. Discuss:
- What are the values in tension?  
- What harm might each choice create or reduce?  
- How do we decide quickly and fairly?  

Examples:
- 🌱 **Foundational:** Handling repeated no-shows in distribution.  
- ⚡ **Advanced:** Suspected infiltration by hostile actors.  

<Callout type="warning">
Some cases involve violence or community harm.  
Facilitators should allow self-care breaks, use a “pause” signal, and focus on healing, not blame.
</Callout>

---

### 4. Decision Tools
- **Quick Consensus:** Use dot-voting or two-option polls for speed.  
- **Modified Consensus:** Seek “can live with” rather than perfect agreement.  
- **Values Check:** Pause to ask, “Does this match our commitments?”  
- **Dilemma Journals:** Track and debrief recurring choices to refine your protocol.

---

### 5. Pod Implementation Steps
1. Run a workshop monthly with 1–2 case studies.  
2. Develop **simple decision trees** for common dilemmas (resource strain, escalation, withdrawal).  
3. Always debrief after major incidents.  
4. Create and revise a **pod-specific protocol** for ethical decision-making.  

<Callout type="hint">
Follow [Documentation Safety](/courses/documentation-safety) and [After-Action Data Hygiene](/courses/after-action-data-hygiene) when recording notes.  
Keep sensitive data minimal and time-bound.
</Callout>

---

### 6. Risks & Things to Watch
- Forcing consensus can delay urgent action.  
- Revisiting trauma carelessly can re-harm members.  
- Speed-focused decisions can silence marginalized voices.  
Balance clarity with care.

---

### 7. Pod Readiness Checklist
- [ ] Can analyze case studies and name core values.  
- [ ] Can use at least one consensus tool under pressure.  
- [ ] Can facilitate trauma-aware discussions.  
- [ ] Have a written pod-specific dilemma protocol.

- Did speed or fear override consent or safety?  
- What one habit could make your next decision more inclusive or grounded?
