# 🗣️ Managing and Conducting Meetings (Virtual & Physical)
<TrackBadge variant="pod-leadership" />
<TrackBadge variant="movement-strategy" />

<Callout type="critical">
**Meetings are systems, not conversations.**  
They require rhythm, containment, and trust to produce clarity without burnout or leaks.
</Callout>

This course builds practical facilitation skills for both digital and in-person meetings, with emphasis on **security, inclusion, pacing, and situational awareness**.

---

## 1. Meeting Fundamentals

Every meeting needs:
- **A purpose:** why you are meeting.  
- **An agenda:** what needs resolution.  
- **A facilitator:** who keeps flow and time.  
- **A scribe:** who captures only outcomes.  
- **A close:** when, how, and by whom the meeting ends.  

> Unclear beginnings waste time. Unclear endings waste trust.

---

## 2. Virtual Meeting Management

### Recommended Tools
- Use **Signal**, **Jitsi**, or **Matrix/Element** for sensitive or encrypted work.  
- Disable AI summaries, cloud recording, and voice assistants.  
- Prefer **audio-only** if bandwidth or privacy is limited.  

### Practices
- Start with a **sound and presence check** (“Can everyone hear?”).  
- Confirm physical surroundings: *“If you’re not alone, please mute or move to privacy.”*  
- Share screen only for visuals essential to the topic.  
- Use a **shared timer** for pacing long sessions.

---

## 3. Physical Meetings

### Space Setup
- Pick a **neutral space** without cameras, smart devices, or voice assistants.  
- Use **Faraday pouches** or a phone-drop table.  
- Position seats in a **circle** or **horseshoe** to keep visibility equal.  
- Control whiteboard use — erase before anyone leaves.  

### Entry & Exit
- Establish ground rules: respect, confidentiality, hand signals.  
- Begin on time; late arrivals wait until break.  
- Close with a **recap and commitments** — no side-talk during wrap-up.

---

## 4. Digital Listeners and Surveillance Awareness

### Identify
- Hidden microphones (smart speakers, TVs, wearables).  
- Active Bluetooth or Wi-Fi devices.  
- Meeting apps that auto-transcribe or store audio.

### Mitigate
- Kill network access; check RF spectrum if possible.  
- Use **Faraday boxes** or run in low-signal rooms.  
- Rotate locations and times for recurring sessions.  

<Callout type="warning">
If the room can talk to the internet, the room can leak your meeting.
</Callout>

---

## 5. Facilitation Dynamics: Reading and Pacing the Room

Facilitation is controlled rhythm. The goal is to **move energy without domination**.

### Pacing
- Use the **“energy wave”** model: open calm → build engagement → peak → close calm.  
- Watch for attention drops every 20–30 minutes; pause or rotate speakers.  
- Keep transitions explicit: “Let’s move from planning to next steps.”

### Emotional Regulation
- Keep tone steady.  
- Slow your speech when tension rises.  
- Validate emotion, but redirect to structure:  
  > “I hear frustration — let’s note it and come back after the action items.”

### Grounding Tools
- Use a **talking piece** (object passed to indicate who speaks).  
- Use a **stack** (facilitator tracks raised hands).  
- Encourage **step up / step back** — frequent speakers yield space.  

---

## 6. Ensuring Everyone Is Heard

### Techniques
1. **Structured Rounds:** each person speaks once before open discussion.  
2. **Reflective Listening:** facilitator paraphrases key points to confirm understanding.  
3. **Silence as Tool:** 5–10 seconds of quiet after questions to allow thought.  
4. **Temperature Checks:** quick hand signs (“+” for agreement, “–” for concern).  

### Managing Dominance
- Gently interrupt: *“Let’s pause to hear others.”*  
- Reinforce equity: *“We haven’t heard from [name] yet.”*  
- Redirect derails to a **parking lot list** for follow-up.

---

## 7. The Closing Sequence

Every meeting should end the same way, creating closure and accountability.

1. Recap key decisions.  
2. Confirm assigned actions, owners, and due dates.  
3. Ask explicitly:  
   > “Any **questions, comments, concerns, or compliments** before we close?”  
4. Schedule next contact (time, medium, facilitator).  
5. Thank participants and confirm recording or notes are secure.

This phrasing — *questions, comments, concerns, or compliments* —  
signals openness while ending on positive tone, inviting feedback without derailing focus.

---

## 8. Documentation & Information Hygiene

- Record **decisions and actions**, not raw dialogue.  
- Store notes in **encrypted, access-controlled space**.  
- Distribute minutes via secure channel (Signal, Matrix, or offline sync).  
- Delete temporary documents after confirmation.  

---

## 9. Accessibility and Inclusion

- Provide interpreters or captioning if needed.  
- Summarize visually for neurodivergent or multilingual participants.  
- Use **time limits** to prevent fatigue.  
- Check emotional tone regularly: *“Is everyone comfortable continuing?”*  

---

## 10. Final Checklist

- [ ] Purpose and agenda set  
- [ ] Facilitator, scribe, timekeeper assigned  
- [ ] Security and digital-listener check complete  
- [ ] Pacing plan defined (opener → engagement → close)  
- [ ] Space created for all voices  
- [ ] Ended with recap and feedback prompt  

<Callout type="success">
**Strong meetings are disciplined containers.**  
They protect time, voices, and information — and build the trust that keeps a movement alive.
</Callout>
