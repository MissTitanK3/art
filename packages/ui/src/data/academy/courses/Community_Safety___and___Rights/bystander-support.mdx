# 🧡 Bystander Support Tactics
<TrackBadge variant="community-care" />
<TrackBadge variant="field-safety" />

<Callout type="info">
Use clear, approachable language. Keep steps small and predictable.  
Bystander intervention prevents escalation, reduces harm, and supports community safety. You don’t need to be an expert — just aware, calm, and prepared.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
Bystander action is a cornerstone of community defense. It’s grounded in **mutual protection**, not heroism.  
This course builds confidence in recognizing harm, deciding how to intervene safely, and supporting people under threat — especially those most targeted by policing, profiling, and harassment.

---

## Learn & Do

### 1. Principles
Your priorities:
1. Center the needs and consent of the targeted person.  
2. Avoid escalating the situation.  
3. Use your presence to shift power and attention.  
4. Delegate or document if direct action isn’t safe.  
5. Offer care after the incident, not just during.  

> Everyone can do something. Few can do everything.

---

### 2. The 5Ds Framework

| Tactic | When It Helps | Risk | Example |
|--------|----------------|------|----------|
| **Direct** | Clear, calm confrontation | High | “That’s racist. Stop.” |
| **Distract** | Defusing public tension | Low | “Excuse me, is this your wallet?” |
| **Document** | Gathering evidence ethically | Medium | Record with narration & location |
| **Delegate** | Getting others to help | Low | “Can you check in on them?” |
| **Delay** | After-incident care | None | “Do you want me to walk with you?” |

---

### 3. Applying Each Tactic

**Direct:** Speak briefly and calmly.  
Use only if safe and not outnumbered.  
→ “That’s not okay.” “Leave them alone.”

**Distract:** Shift focus without confrontation.  
→ Ask for directions, drop something, start unrelated conversation.

**Document:**  
Only with consent and if safe to do so.  
- Record horizontally and narrate: *“ICE stop, Portland OR, 3:15pm.”*  
- Blur faces before sharing.  
- Share securely via **Signal** or encrypted storage.  

<Callout type="important">
Never post footage online without consent. Follow <a href="/courses/documentation-safety">Documentation Safety</a> and <a href="/courses/after-action-data-hygiene">After-Action Data Hygiene</a>.
</Callout>

**Delegate:**  
Ask for help. Involve staff, drivers, or trained observers.  
→ “Could you call someone while I stay nearby?”

**Delay:**  
If the moment passes, offer presence.  
→ “That looked hard. Are you okay?”  
→ Offer water, listening, or accompaniment.

---

### 4. Context Awareness
Different environments require different tactics:
- Avoid direct confrontation when police or ICE are present.  
- Use nonverbal communication if language barriers exist.  
- Keep voice calm and body language open.  
- Step back if your presence raises risk.

<Callout type="info">
Language access: For multilingual settings, pair with the <a href="/courses/translator-micro-badge">Translator</a> and <a href="/courses/asl-interpreter-micro-badge">ASL Interpreter</a> micro-badges.
</Callout>

---

### 5. What *Not* to Do
- Don’t film without purpose or consent.  
- Don’t speak over or for the person harmed.  
- Don’t escalate for ego or argument.  
- Don’t ignore your own limits.

---

### 6. Practice Scenarios
<Callout type="warning">
Some scenarios may trigger stress. Take breaks.
</Callout>

Choose a tactic for each:
- ICE agents question a farmworker.  
- A teen is trailed by store security.  
- A trans person is mocked in a courthouse.  
- A woman is yelled at in Spanish on a bus.  

<Callout type="important">
Role-play these with a partner. Confidence grows through practice.
</Callout>
