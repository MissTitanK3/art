# 🔫 Weapon Safety & Firearms 101
<TrackBadge variant="field-safety" />
<TrackBadge variant="direct-action" />

<Callout type="critical">
**Never point a weapon at anything you do not intend to destroy.**
This course teaches recognition, distance, and safe-handling fundamentals — not combat training.
</Callout>

Mutual-aid and defense teams sometimes encounter firearms in the field.  
This module builds shared literacy for **safety, legality, and de-escalation** around weapons.  
The goal is **incident prevention**, not escalation.

---

## 1. Universal Safety Rules

1. Treat every firearm as loaded.  
2. Keep your finger off the trigger until ready to fire.  
3. Never aim at anything you don’t intend to shoot.  
4. Be certain of your target and what lies beyond it.  

Repetition under calm conditions prevents panic errors under stress.

---

## 2. Safe Handling & Storage

- Verify clear chamber before transfer.  
- Use chamber flags and safeties when available.  
- Store ammo separate from weapons.  
- Employ locking cases or cable locks during transport.  

Field pods should designate a **Safety Officer** to check every transfer and carry log.

---

## 3. Recognizing Firearms in the Field

Know visual distinctions:

| Type | Typical Range | Common Uses |
|------|----------------|--------------|
| Handgun | 5–25 m | Personal defense, police issue |
| Rifle | 50–400 m | Hunting, security, long-range threat |
| Shotgun | 5–50 m | Crowd deterrent, riot control |

If sighted in public: note **count, type, bearing, distance**, and **avoid confrontation**.

---

## 4. Legal & Ethical Boundaries

- Firearm laws vary by state; check local statutes before any handling.  
- Never accept possession if prohibited or unlicensed.  
- No open carry inside public-aid zones.  
- Document violations through observation channels, **not social media**.

<Callout type="warning">
Safety literacy ≠ authorization.  
Only licensed individuals perform lawful armed security where permitted.
</Callout>

---

## 5. De-Escalation Around Armed Individuals

1. Maintain calm body language, visible empty hands.  
2. Keep 6 ft+ distance; never reach toward the weapon.  
3. Use verbal cues like *“We’re stepping back for safety.”*  
4. If threats escalate, **disengage and report** through dispatch.

---

## 6. Field Safety Integration

Pods working near potential firearms should:

- Include one trained **Safety Marshal** per 5–10 volunteers.  
- Practice **weapon-sighting drills** (identify → cover → communicate → report).  
- Carry trauma kits rated for ballistic injury (tourniquet, hemostatic gauze).  

Monthly review: simulate a weapon-sighting scenario and evaluate calm response.

---

## 7. Gear Checklist

- Eye & ear protection  
- Chamber flag and lock set  
- Trauma kit (CAT TQ + pressure dressing)  
- Field notebook for incident logging  
- Laminated local firearm law summary  

---

- [ ] Knows local carry/transport regulations.

Familiarity reduces fear, and disciplined habits prevent tragedy.
</Callout>
