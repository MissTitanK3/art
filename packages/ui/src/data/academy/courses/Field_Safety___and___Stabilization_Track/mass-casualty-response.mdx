## Overview
In large actions, disasters, or targeted attacks, multiple people may be injured simultaneously.  
This is called a **Mass Casualty Incident (MCI)** — a situation where the **need for medical care exceeds available resources**.  
Your pod’s role is not to replace emergency responders, but to **bridge the gap** between chaos and organized care.

You’ll learn to:
- Stabilize the scene.
- Prioritize lifesaving actions.
- Move information — not panic.
- Coordinate handoffs safely.

> Calm organization, not heroics, saves lives.

---

## Learn & Do

### 1. Scene Safety and Assessment
Before treating anyone:
- Scan for **active threats** (violence, vehicles, fire, gas, structural instability).
- Designate a **Safety Officer** to secure or flag the danger area.
- Mark a **Safe Zone** and identify **two exits**.

If unsafe: **retreat and report**. Do not become the next casualty.

---

### 2. Establish Command and Roles
Quickly form a minimal command structure:
| Role | Responsibility |
|------|----------------|
| **Incident Lead** | Makes decisions, tracks situation overview |
| **Triage Lead** | Classifies patients and sets treatment order |
| **Medic Team** | Stabilizes and preps for transport |
| **Comms/Runner** | Relays info to dispatch and incoming support |
| **Logistics Support** | Manages supplies, vehicles, and restock |

> Even small pods can adapt this model — one person may hold multiple roles.

---

### 3. Triage Systems (START / RPM)
Use **START**: *Simple Triage And Rapid Treatment*  
or **RPM**: *Respiration, Pulse, Mentation*

**RPM Steps:**
1. **Respiration:**  
   - Breathing > 30/min → *Red (Immediate)*  
   - Not breathing → reposition airway once. If still none → *Black (Deceased)*
2. **Pulse:**  
   - No radial pulse → *Red (Immediate)*
3. **Mentation:**  
   - Cannot follow commands → *Red*  
   - Can follow → *Yellow (Delayed)*  

Color tags or tape can simplify triage:
- 🟥 Red – Immediate  
- 🟨 Yellow – Delayed  
- 🟩 Green – Minor  
- ⬛ Black – Deceased / Expectant

> Never argue over color tags. The first label stands until reassessed by a medic.

---

### 4. Medical Priorities
Focus on **MARCH** order:
1. **Massive bleeding** – tourniquet or pressure  
2. **Airway** – position, clear obstruction  
3. **Respiration** – open chest wounds (seal if possible)  
4. **Circulation** – check pulse, manage shock  
5. **Head injury/Hypothermia** – insulate, protect from exposure  

Use what you have. *Improvised beats inaction.*

---

### 5. Communication Flow
In an MCI, clarity outranks speed.  
**Use 3-line reports** to dispatch or radio:

> “RED – 3 injured, 1 unconscious – North entrance of park.”

If multiple pods report, dispatch logs:
- Time received  
- Type (injury, fire, collapse, etc.)  
- Number affected  
- Current hazards  

For degraded comms, use runners or written tags.

<Callout type="hint">
Use signal or LoRa brevity codes:  
`MCI` = Mass Casualty Incident  
`MED-X` = Medical extraction requested  
`LOG-REQ` = Supply or transport needed
</Callout>

---

### 6. Evacuation & Transport Coordination
- Assign a **Transport Lead** to organize safe routes.
- Use **pre-mapped exits and fallback zones**.
- Never overload vehicles; prioritize based on triage category.
- Record **destination, patient tag color, and driver**.

If formal EMS arrives, hand over calmly:
> “We triaged using START. 3 Red, 4 Yellow, 2 Green. 1 Black. Map notes here.”

---

### 7. Decontamination & Environmental Hazards
If chemical exposure (tear gas, pepper spray, smoke):
- Move victims to fresh air.
- Strip contaminated clothing.
- Flush with clean water (never milk or oils).
- Do **not** touch contaminated surfaces without gloves.

If fire, collapse, or flood:
- Switch to **Rescue Triage** — focus on extraction and safety, not treatment.

---

### 8. Documentation & Data Hygiene
Keep minimal data:
- Time, triage color, care given, destination.  
- No full names unless essential for family/legal follow-up.

After event:
- Securely destroy any raw notes or comms logs.
- Transfer anonymized summary to dispatch or regional admin.

---

### 9. Psychological First Aid (PFA)
After mass injury, **emotional shock** spreads faster than trauma.
Offer:
- Water, grounding (“Can you feel your feet?”)
- Calm tone, not comfort platitudes.
- Safe space to sit and breathe.
- Avoid speculation about outcomes.

> Emotional triage is part of the job.

---

### 10. After-Action Review & Debrief
Hold a structured debrief within 48 hours:
- What went right / what broke down?  
- Were roles clear?  
- Did we overextend?  
- Did anyone need follow-up care?  

Follow with group grounding or mutual care circle.
