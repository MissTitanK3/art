# Field Safety & Situational Awareness

Being in the field during ICE presence or state violence puts you at direct risk. This course trains you to assess threats, make safe decisions, and remain effective under pressure.

Pair this with <a href="/courses/escalation-evacuation-protocols">Escalation & Evacuation Protocols</a>, <a href="/courses/dealing-with-chaos">Dealing With Chaos</a>, and offline fallbacks in <a href="/courses/comms-collapse">Comms Collapse</a> and <a href="/courses/app-free-tactics">App‑Free Tactics</a>. <Callout type="info">

## Core Concepts

### Situational Awareness

- **Observe** surroundings, entrances/exits, and behaviors.
- **Orient** to your role, location, and objective.
- **Decide** based on current safety and team signals.
- **Act** only when confident in the plan and coverage.

> The OODA loop (Observe–Orient–Decide–Act) helps you avoid hesitation or panic under pressure.

---

## Threat Recognition

Learn to spot:

- Aggressive or erratic police movement
- Vehicle kettling and flanking patterns
- Unmarked vans or plainclothes agents
- Undercover provocateurs mimicking protest gear
- Panic triggers or sudden crowd shifts

<Callout type="warning">
Undercover agents often wear masks or carry protest signs. Watch for isolated actors encouraging escalation.
</Callout>

## What to Carry

| Gear               | Why                                      |
|--------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| ID & emergency info| In case of arrest or injury               |
| Mask & goggles     | Tear gas, facial recognition shielding    |
| Gloves             | Protection + hygiene                      |
| Basic medkit       | Bleeds, burns, and eye flush              |
| Burner phone       | Prevent tracking                          |
| Hydration pack     | Keeps hands free, prevents dehydration    |
| Emergency cash     | Transit escape or basic support ($20–50) |
| Water & snacks     | Energy and care for others                |

Avoid: tools, projectiles, flyers — anything that could be used as evidence of intent.

<Callout type="info">
Harden your devices and practice compartmentalization. See <a href="/courses/digital-security-basics">Digital Security Basics</a> and <a href="/courses/burner-phones-security-culture">Burner Phones & Security Culture</a>.
</Callout>

## Crowd Dynamics

- Stay near **edges** if not embedded in the action.
- Watch crowd flow — surges = crush hazard.
- Move **calmly and diagonally** through crowds.
- Assist those who fall or freeze.
- If arms can’t be raised, exit immediately.

<Callout type="tip">
Use agreed-on hand signals to guide others without shouting.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info">
Language access matters in the field. If interpretation may be needed, coordinate with teammates who hold the <a href="/courses/translator-micro-badge">Translator Micro‑badge</a> or <a href="/courses/asl-interpreter-micro-badge">ASL Interpreter Micro‑badge</a>.
</Callout>

## Field Hand Signals

- ✋ **Palm out** — Stop / hold position  
- 🔁 **Circle overhead** — Regroup at fallback point  
- 👉 **Two taps on shoulder** — Follow me  
- ❤️ **Hand over heart** — I need medical help  

---

## Know Your Role

- Understand your **task**: medic, scout, observer, etc.
- Stay in your assigned **zone**
- Check in frequently
- Never go solo during high-risk ops

---

## Radio & Signal Protocols

- Use encrypted or burner devices if possible
- Speak in short, clear phrases
- Never name individuals or locations over open channels
- Pre-agree on code words and fallback locations

<Callout type="hint">
When networks degrade, switch to runners and brevity codes. For device and metadata hygiene, review <a href="/courses/digital-security-basics">Digital Security Basics</a>.
</Callout>

---

## When It Escalates

- **Don’t panic**. Breathe deeply.
- Use rehearsed movements.
- Exit the area unless your role requires staying.
- Notify your team and dispatcher with status.

<Callout type="info">
If documenting, minimize faces and follow <a href="/courses/documentation-safety">Documentation & Safety</a>. Keep retention short per <a href="/courses/after-action-data-hygiene">After‑Action Data Hygiene</a>.
</Callout>

## Risk Zones to Avoid

| Zone                  | Why                                      |
|-----------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| Narrow alleys         | Easy to trap or isolate people           |
| Rooftops              | Visibility, sniper risk                  |
| Unmarked parked vans  | Risk of sting or detention               |
| Residential areas     | Collateral targeting, legal complexity   |

---

## Trust Your Body

Your body knows before your brain does.  
If you feel tense, nauseous, frozen, or dizzy — take a breath and check your surroundings.

> “Is it safe to stay?” If unsure, step back and reassess.

---

## Team Protocols

- Check in/out with dispatcher
- Assign fallback locations
- Use green/yellow/red zone language
- Confirm roles before deployment

---

## What to Do If...

### Kettling Begins

- Police block exits or form containment lines
- Move diagonally to crowd edge
- Look for safe exits: alleys, unlocked doors, transit
- Use hand signals to coordinate exit
- Document officer lines if safe

### Tear Gas Is Deployed

- Move **crosswind**, not against wind
- Cover mouth with cloth soaked in vinegar/lemon
- Flush eyes with **water or saline** (never milk or oils)

### Arrests Are Happening

- Record only from a distance
- Note names, time, location
- Do not intervene—just document

<Callout type="hint">
Know your rights and escalation pathways. See <a href="/courses/kyr-basics">KYR Basics</a>, and route post‑incident support to <a href="/courses/court-support">Court Support</a> and <a href="/courses/legal-followthrough-jail-support">Legal Follow‑through & Jail Support</a>.
</Callout>

### Medical Emergency

- Signal medic if present
- Clear space
- Use basic aid (compress, elevate, flush) if trained

## Summary

- Stay calm and observant
- Know your role and don’t improvise under stress
- Prioritize safety for yourself and others
- Situational awareness is your first and last line of defense

<Callout type="success">
Every safe return strengthens the movement. Bravery without discipline is a liability.
