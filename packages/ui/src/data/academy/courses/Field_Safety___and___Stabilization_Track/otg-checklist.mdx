## Overview

Field work introduces stress, unpredictability, and surveillance.  
This course provides a repeatable deployment framework that balances readiness with care.  
A clear checklist helps your pod:

- Prevent confusion and safety breakdowns  
- Operate with minimal tech reliance  
- Handle escalation calmly  
- Integrate new members quickly  

This becomes your pod’s **operational muscle memory**.

---

## Learn & Do

### ✅ Before Deployment

- [ ] Confirm time, location, and fallback meetup  
- [ ] Assign roles (Outreach, Safety, Comms, Support)  
- [ ] Pack gear: flyers, water, sanitizer, clipboard, Signal zine  
- [ ] Charge radios or devices  
- [ ] Review safety, escalation, and fallback plans  
- [ ] Confirm communication channels and collapse protocol  
- [ ] Ask: *“Does anyone feel off or unsafe today?”*  
- [ ] Reaffirm: *“Anyone can pause operations if unsafe.”*

---

### 🧰 Field Gear Essentials

| Item | Purpose |
|------|----------|
| Flyers, KYR cards, pod signups | Outreach materials |
| Hat, water, snack | Energy + sun protection |
| Phone/radio or map | Comms or navigation |
| Gloves, sanitizer | Safe hand-offs or cleanup |
| ID or patch (optional) | Visual trust signal |
| Clipboard + pens | Logs and forms |
| Mini first aid kit | Quick support |
| Collapse protocol card | Backup for comms failure |

---

### 📍 On-Site Protocol

- Choose **shaded, high-visibility, low-surveillance** areas  
- Confirm pod roles verbally  
- Offer materials with consent: “Can I share this with you?”  
- Build trust through calm, friendly engagement  
- Use short scripts, avoid debates  
- Regularly scan for ICE/police presence  
- Keep body language low-threat: visible hands, slow movement  
- Maintain spacing between volunteers  
- Assign a “quiet observer” role for scanning  
- Check in every 30–45 minutes for hydration and stress  

<Callout type="hint">
Use calm posture and gentle tone. If someone walks away, that’s not failure — it’s consent culture in action.
</Callout>

---

### 🧠 Optional Field Activities

| Action | Use With Caution |
|--------|------------------|
| Logging community concerns | Only if anonymous + consensual |
| Threat mapping | Avoid GPS or tagged photos |
| KYR readings | Good for busy public areas |
| Handing out water/snacks | Trust-building, watch local norms |
| Passive displays | Avoid blocking walkways |
| Taking photos | Only with consent, no faces |
| Scenario prompts | “What would you do if ICE showed up?” |
| Coordination with orgs | Ask permission before sharing space |

---

### ⚠️ Situational Warnings

- Never mention immigration or legal status aloud  
- Do not document conversations without consent  
- Avoid federal sites or visible surveillance towers  
- Never promise legal outcomes  
- If conflict arises, **de-escalate and disengage**

<Callout type="hint">
Documentation hygiene: Store field notes offline, encrypted, and delete temporary data after review. ART apps do not archive field logs.
</Callout>

---

### 🛑 If Things Escalate

- [ ] Step back and de-escalate  
- [ ] Do not argue or shout  
- [ ] Regroup with pod, don’t scatter  
- [ ] Use fallback signal/site  
- [ ] Notify pod lead if retreat required  
- [ ] Log events only once safe  

---

### 🔁 After Deployment

- [ ] Everyone accounted for  
- [ ] Debrief + emotional check-in  
- [ ] Log new contacts securely  
- [ ] Restock supplies  
- [ ] Note risks or exposure  
- [ ] Decide: pause, adapt, or repeat  
- [ ] Schedule next review or check-in  

<Callout type="info">
Use the **Crisis Debrief Sheet** if an incident or conflict occurred.
</Callout>

---

### 🧠 Tips for New Pods

- Do a dry-run before first outing  
- Pair new members with experienced ones  
- Normalize frequent safety check-ins  
- Start with low-risk outreach (like water tables)  
- Keep extra flyers, gloves, and role cards ready
