## 🧠 Why This Matters

Chaos isn’t powerful — it’s dangerous.  
Clear protocol helps:

- Prevent safety breakdowns  
- Keep the pod synced without over-relying on tech  
- Respond to issues without panic  
- Rotate new members in with confidence  

This checklist becomes your pod’s **muscle memory.**

---

## ✅ Before Deployment

- [ ] Confirm time, location, and fallback meetup  
- [ ] Assign roles: Outreach, Watch, Safety, Comms, Support  
- [ ] Pack gear (flyers, water, sanitizer, clipboard, Signal zine)  
- [ ] Charge devices or check radios  
- [ ] Review safety plan (escalation, retreat, fallback)  
- [ ] Confirm Signal group or fallback contact  
- [ ] Review collapse protocol (if Signal fails)  
- [ ] Check-in: _“Does anyone feel unsure or off today?”_  
- [ ] Reaffirm: _“We all have the right to pause if it feels unsafe.”_

## 📍 On-Site Protocol

<Callout type="info">
This is your guide for how to actually move once you’re on location. These steps apply whether you're tabling, canvassing, responding to an alert, or running a CDC. Even experienced organizers skip basics under stress — this keeps everyone grounded.
</Callout>

### Core Deployment Steps

- [ ] Choose a **shaded, high-visibility, low-surveillance** spot (avoid choke points or alleyways)  
- [ ] Confirm **pod roles** verbally (Outreach, Safety, Comms, etc.)  
- [ ] Offer materials **without pressure** — don't force engagement  
- [ ] Use **consent-based engagement**: "Can I share this with you?" before offering info  
- [ ] **Start light**: Water, stickers, or conversation often builds more trust than flyers alone  
- [ ] **Use scripts**, not rants — and never confront hostile people  
- [ ] **Scan the area** regularly: mark ICE/police activity, suspicious watchers, or nearby support  
- [ ] Keep **low-profile body language** when approaching people (hands visible, avoid fast movement)  
- [ ] **Avoid crowding**: keep a few feet of space between organizers  
- [ ] Maintain a “**quiet person role**” — someone scanning for issues without being on the front line  
- [ ] Check in every 30–45 minutes: hydration, stress, tension, body fatigue, gut checks  
- [ ] Don’t assume who is “in charge” — speak up if anything feels off

### Optional OTG Activities (Situational)

| Action | Use With Caution |
|--------|------------------|
| 📝 Logging community concerns | Only if info is anonymous + consensual |
| 📍 Threat mapping on paper or map | Avoid GPS-tagged phones or digital location logging |
| 📣 Reading or sharing short KYR scripts | Great for high-traffic or low-literacy environments |
| 🌱 Offering pod cards or safety pledges | Use only if person shows interest or asks |
| 🧊 Distributing water/snacks | Great trust-builder, but stay aware of local context |
| 🛠️ Setting up passive displays | Use crates, boards, zines — don’t block foot traffic |
| 📷 Taking photos of your setup | Only with consent, never include faces or surroundings |
| 🧠 Practicing scenario prompts | Great for downtime: “What would you say if ICE showed up right now?” |
| 🤝 Coordinating with nearby orgs | Check first if they’re okay with visibility or sharing space |

---

### Situational Warnings

- ⚠️ Do not name undocumented status, legal status, or past criminalization out loud — even casually  
- ⚠️ Never document conversations without explicit permission  
- ⚠️ Be cautious near federal buildings, large cameras, or known surveillance towers  
- ⚠️ Do not promise legal outcomes — offer connections, not guarantees  
- ⚠️ If someone’s behavior seems volatile, **de-escalate and disengage**, don’t argue  

<Callout type="hint">
Use **calm body language** and lead with care. A smile and a flyer often open more doors than urgency. If someone walks away, that’s not failure — it’s practice in consent-based organizing.
</Callout>

<Callout type="hint">
Documentation hygiene: Use minimal, time-bound retention. Avoid storing raw chat logs or sensitive field notes in shared apps. Prefer encrypted, access-controlled notes and delete temporary files after review. ART does not support in-app field log archiving; use secure external tools if needed and always get explicit consent before sharing plans or protocols.
</Callout>

## 🛑 If Things Escalate

- [ ] Step back and de-escalate  
- [ ] Do **not** engage verbally unless safe  
- [ ] Regroup with your pod, don’t scatter  
- [ ] Use your fallback signal or site  
- [ ] Notify a pod lead if retreat is needed  
- [ ] Log the event only when safe and grounded  

---

## 🔁 After Deployment

- [ ] Everyone accounted for and debriefed  
- [ ] Any new contacts logged securely  
- [ ] Materials restocked or stored  
- [ ] Risk or exposure notes flagged  
- [ ] “What worked? What felt off?” shared  
- [ ] Decide: pause, escalate, or repeat  
- [ ] Check-in scheduled if needed

<Callout type="info">
Use the **crisis debrief sheet** if anything serious occurred — even if no one was harmed.
</Callout>

<Callout type="important">
Consent & legal: Always ask before sharing plans, feedback, or field logs. Pair this course with Documentation Safety, Data Ethics Pledge, Legal Observer, and After‑Action Data Hygiene for best practices.
</Callout>

---

## 🧠 Tips for Newer Pods

- Do a dry-run setup before your first outing  
- Carry extra flyers and role cards for on-the-spot support  
- Bring one experienced lead + one new person per pod  
- Practice low-stakes outreach (e.g. water handouts) first  
- Normalize frequent check-ins, even if nothing’s wrong
