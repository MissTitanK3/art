# 🪖 Interacting with the National Guard & Active Military Presence
<TrackBadge variant="field-safety" />
<TrackBadge variant="direct-action" />
<TrackBadge variant="legal-support" />

<Callout type="info">
When the National Guard or active-duty military operate inside U.S. borders, the situation has shifted beyond standard law enforcement.  
This module teaches **recognition, de-escalation, and safety protocols** for activists, mutual aid pods, and dispatchers working under these conditions.
</Callout>

---

## 🧭 Overview

Military deployments during protests, crises, or “civil unrest” can be intimidating and confusing.  
These forces often claim a **law enforcement support role**, but their **rules of engagement (ROE)** and training differ sharply from police.

Understanding **their hierarchy, posture, and mission scope** keeps volunteers safe and reduces escalation.

> “You can’t de-escalate what you don’t understand. Calm starts with clarity.”

---

## 🎯 Learn & Do

- Recognize **National Guard vs. Active Duty** vs. **Private Contractor** presence.  
- Understand **legal authorities** and limits of domestic military involvement.  
- Practice **de-escalation language** suited for armed, non-police personnel.  
- Document military operations safely for accountability.  
- Protect volunteers’ rights while avoiding confrontation or provocation.  

<Callout type="warning">
**Do not treat armed troops like local police.**  
They answer to a military chain of command and often have orders, not discretion.
</Callout>

---

## 🪖 1. Who They Are — and What They Can (and Can’t) Do

| Type | Activated By | Typical Role | Legal Limits |
|------|---------------|--------------|---------------|
| **National Guard (State Control)** | Governor | Crowd control, checkpoints, logistics | Can enforce state law under state orders |
| **National Guard (Federalized)** | President / DoD | Border or “civil support” ops | Follows federal ROE, often less flexible |
| **Active Duty Military** | DoD (Title 10) | Support to civil authorities only | **Cannot** perform direct law enforcement (Posse Comitatus Act) |
| **Private Military Contractors** | Local / Federal agency | Security, transport | No police power — subject to normal civilian law |

<Callout type="hint">
Ask yourself: “Are they wearing state insignia or federal patches?”  
State = local control. Federal = less accountability for on-scene negotiation.
</Callout>

---

## 👁️ 2. Recognition and Identification

Look for:

- **Uniform Markings:** State Guard = state flag patch; Federal troops = U.S. Army / branch insignia.  
- **Gear Style:** Military units wear **plate carriers, helmets, rifles**, not standard police vests.  
- **Behavioral Cues:** They move in **squads**, not solo. Commands use hand signals.  
- **Vehicles:** HMMWVs, armored personnel carriers (APCs), or logistics trucks indicate Guard deployment, not regular police.  

> Rule of thumb: The more organized and silent the formation, the more likely they are under military command.

---

## ⚖️ 3. Legal Context — What Rights Still Apply

Even under Guard activation:
- You still have **First Amendment rights** to assemble and document.  
- You still have **Fourth Amendment protection** from unreasonable search/seizure.  
- You are **not required** to answer operational questions or consent to being filmed.  

However:
- Military units often **operate under “temporary emergency orders”**, granting them authority to restrict movement within designated zones.  
- Always **ask, don’t argue**:  
  > “Sir/Ma’am, am I being detained, or may I move to safety?”  

If they tell you to leave, move calmly.  
Disobeying a direct military order in a declared zone can carry harsher penalties than disobeying police.

---

## 🧠 4. Psychological Dynamics

Military training prioritizes **mission completion** over negotiation.  
Their perception of threat escalates faster than civilian law enforcement.

De-escalators must project **neutrality, control, and predictability.**

| Behavior | Interpretation by Troops | Safer Alternative |
|-----------|--------------------------|--------------------|
| Rapid movement | Potential threat | Slow, visible hands |
| Loud arguments | “Hostile crowd” | Quiet coordination |
| Filming at close range | Possible provocation | Step back, zoom in |
| Pointing or gesturing | Command behavior | Keep arms relaxed at sides |

> Calm body language reduces perceived threat more than any verbal assurance.

---

## 🗣️ 5. Communication Strategies

- Use **clear, neutral phrases**:  
  - “We’re here as community support.”  
  - “We’re moving back for safety.”  
  - “We respect your space.”  
- Address with neutral respect: “Sir / Ma’am” or “Officer.”  
- Avoid sarcasm, profanity, or accusatory tone — troops are trained to respond to *tone shifts* as danger signals.  

If asked who you are:  
> “I’m with a community safety and medical team.”  
Avoid political framing or organizational identifiers.

<Callout type="info">
**Never volunteer identifying data or names of others.**  
If pressed, calmly say: “I can’t provide that without consent.”
</Callout>

---

## 🧱 6. Field Positioning & Movement

- Keep **clear exit paths** and **visual cover** (walls, vehicles).  
- Never cross between soldiers unless directed.  
- Avoid approaching from behind or flanking angles.  
- Do not shine lights or phone cameras directly at armed personnel.  

If gunfire or flashbangs occur:
1. Drop low.  
2. Move crosswind to escape path.  
3. Reunite at fallback point per your pod’s plan.  

---

## 📷 7. Documentation & Evidence Safety

- Film **from a safe distance** (50+ feet preferred).  
- Capture **unit markings, insignia, vehicle numbers**, not faces.  
- Store footage **offline** or on **encrypted drives** only.  
- Never livestream troop movement; delay posts at least 12 hours.  

> Document for evidence, not engagement.

---

## 🧩 8. Supporting the Community During Deployment

- Offer **food, water, and rest info** to civilians trapped by curfews or checkpoints.  
- Track reported detentions and **relay to dispatch or legal teams**.  
- Provide **psychological grounding** — fear spreads faster than violence.  
- Monitor for **racially biased enforcement patterns** or excessive force.  
- Coordinate **safe dispersal** zones with medics and care pods.

<Callout type="warning">
Do not challenge curfews or barricades unless part of an authorized humanitarian operation.  
Your safety and witness value matter more than symbolic confrontation.
</Callout>

---

## 🧩 9. After the Interaction

1. Record what you saw — time, place, insignia, orders given.  
2. Debrief with your pod and log under **Field Safety** protocols.  
3. Report verified information only through secure channels.  
4. Avoid emotional sharing on social media; misstatements can trigger reprisals.
