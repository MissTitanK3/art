# 💛 Care & Emotional Support
<TrackBadge variant="community-care" />

<Callout type="info">
You don’t need to be a therapist to support others. What people remember is how **safe** they felt in your presence.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
Emotional care is foundational to resilient organizing.  
This module teaches how to provide grounding, choice-based support for people in distress — and how to care for yourself afterward.  
You’ll learn how to:
- Recognize trauma responses  
- Offer consent-based emotional grounding  
- Maintain your own stability  
- Debrief safely after intense events  

---

## Learn & Do

### 1. Understanding Emotional Support
Emotional support means **being present without fixing**.  
You help others feel safe by listening, slowing down, and maintaining boundaries.

**Your role:**
- Listen without judgment  
- Ask permission before acting  
- Stay calm and predictable  
- Respect silence and cultural differences  

---

### 2. Recognizing Trauma Responses
People may:
- Freeze or go blank  
- Cry, shake, or lash out  
- Dissociate or laugh nervously  
- Struggle to breathe or focus  

<Callout type="warning">
Avoid calling these “overreactions.” They’re protective body responses, not choices.
</Callout>

If language interpretation or accessibility is needed, see [Translator Micro-badge](/courses/translator-micro-badge) or [ASL Interpreter Micro-badge](/courses/asl-interpreter-micro-badge).

---

### 3. Core Tools You Can Offer

| Tool | Use | Avoid When |
|------|-----|-------------|
| Calm breathing | Panic, dissociation | Feels intrusive |
| Water/food | Grounding | Person can’t swallow safely |
| Gentle tone | High emotion | Danger demands firmness |
| Simple choices | Restoring agency | Too many options confuse |
| Nonverbal presence | Speechless distress | Boundaries unclear |
| Silence | Overwhelm | They ask for response |

<Callout type="success">
“Would you like to sit with me?” can mean more than any speech.
</Callout>

---

### 4. Grounding in Crisis
1. **Ask consent** — “Can I sit here with you?”  
2. **Avoid touch unless invited.**  
3. **Use names or gentle orientation cues.**  
4. **Breathe together slowly.**  
5. **Speak simply and softly.**  

If you’re coordinating with dispatch or medics, review [Responding to Dispatch Calls](/courses/responding-to-dispatch-calls).

---

### 5. Roles in Emotional Support Teams

| Role | Focus |
|------|--------|
| Field Supporter | Water, shade, grounding check-ins |
| Emotional Anchor | Stays with overwhelmed individuals |
| Aftercare Lead | Debriefs and restores team calm |
| Trauma Buddy | Peer assigned for mutual care |

Everyone shares care responsibility. Rotate roles to avoid burnout.

---

### 6. Staying Grounded Yourself
**Before:**  
🧍 Pause → 🫁 Breathe → 🤲 Check tension → ✅ Proceed  

**During:**  
- Keep posture open and voice low  
- Use short affirmations: “You’re safe.” “You did well.”  

**After:**  
- Debrief with peers  
- Eat, hydrate, rest  
- Don’t carry others’ stories  

For safe reflection handling, review [After-Action Data Hygiene](/courses/after-action-data-hygiene).

---

### 7. Practice Scenarios

**1. Crying after a raid**  
Sit nearby, offer water, let silence work.  
🔁 Reflect: What helps them feel safe?

**2. Shaking teammate**  
Normalize and ground: “That was a lot. I’m here.”  
🔁 Reflect: When to give space vs. stay close?

**3. Panic post-arrest**  
Guide to 5-4-3-2-1 grounding.  
🔁 Reflect: What calms your own nerves?

**4. Cultural boundaries**  
Respect refusals of touch. Offer verbal comfort instead.  
🔁 Reflect: What gestures are universally respectful?

---

### 8. What Not to Do
- Don’t demand calm or storytelling  
- Don’t compare experiences  
- Don’t minimize pain (“At least you’re okay”)  
- Don’t abandon your limits  

<Callout type="warning">
Boundaries are safety tools. Compassion without boundaries leads to burnout.
</Callout>
