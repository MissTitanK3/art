# 🕊️ De-escalation Basics
<TrackBadge variant="field-safety" />
<TrackBadge variant="community-care" />

<Callout type="info">
De-escalation is not about control. It’s about reducing tension, preserving dignity, and minimizing harm.
</Callout>

<Callout type="hint">

> Over 80% of heated conflicts de-escalate when met with calm, non-reactive presence.

---

## Overview
De-escalation uses communication, empathy, and awareness to reduce conflict or agitation without force.  
It helps in street actions, community defense, and mutual aid spaces where tension or fear can rise quickly.  
You’ll learn how to:
- Read conflict dynamics and triggers  
- Use tone, distance, and language to lower stress  
- Know when not to engage  
- Support peers and debrief safely afterward  

---

## Learn & Do

### 1. Core Principles
1. Preserve **your safety first**.  
2. Engage, don’t confront.  
3. Recognize emotion before logic.  
4. Keep calm body language.  
5. Slow the pace and buy time.

---

### 2. Body Language & Positioning

| Action | Purpose |
|--------|----------|
| Open palms, relaxed shoulders | Signals safety |
| Sideways stance | Reduces threat perception |
| Avoid hard eye contact | Prevents challenge (context-dependent) |
| Speak slowly | Encourages calm mirroring |
| Leave space for exit | Prevents feeling trapped |

> 👁️ Cultural note: In some cultures, direct eye contact signals aggression or disrespect.

Never block someone’s path or corner them.

---

### 3. Verbal Techniques
- Keep a **low, calm tone**.  
- Describe, don’t accuse: “I’m noticing tension here.”  
- Replace “you” statements with “I” or “we.”  
- Offer **choices** instead of demands.  
- Reflect feelings: “That sounds frustrating.”  

| Avoid | Try Instead |
|--------|--------------|
| “Calm down!” | “Let’s breathe together.” |
| “You’re overreacting.” | “This seems really hard right now.” |
| “Just relax.” | “I’m here. You’re not alone.” |

<Callout type="warning">
Reasoning fails in fight-or-flight states. Calm the body first, then the mind.
</Callout>

---

### 4. Power-Aware Communication
- Remove visible markers of authority when possible.  
- Defer to others with shared identity in racialized or gendered conflicts.  
- Use community language to connect (“We’re here for safety”).  
- Step back if you’re escalating or not trusted.

---

### 5. When Not to Engage
Avoid direct intervention if:
- Weapons are visible or likely  
- You’re alone or outnumbered  
- The person is in severe crisis beyond your skill  
- Police presence increases danger  

Signal for backup or observe safely.

<Callout type="info">
If documenting, follow [Documentation Safety](/courses/documentation-safety) and never post without consent.  
For law enforcement interactions, review [Know Your Rights (KYR) Basics](/courses/kyr-basics).
</Callout>

---

### 6. De-escalating Police Encounters

| ✅ DO | 🚫 DON’T |
|--------|-----------|
| Stay calm and verbal | Argue or shout |
| Film from safe distance | Interfere physically |
| State rights once | Repeat confrontations |
| Record badge details | Approach from behind |

Coordinate with [Responding to Dispatch Calls](/courses/responding-to-dispatch-calls).  
If arrests occur, contact [Court Support](/courses/court-support) and [Legal Follow-through & Jail Support](/courses/legal-followthrough-jail-support).

### 7. Active Listening

- Don’t interrupt.  
- Validate: “That makes sense.”  
- Reflect: “You felt ignored.”  
- Ask: “What do you need right now?”  
- Offer grounding: “Want to step aside with me?”  

<Callout type="success">
Listening is the most reliable de-escalation tool.
</Callout>

#### Kid-Friendly Adaptation
- Listen with eyes and heart.  
- Wait your turn to talk.  
- Repeat what you heard.  
- Ask, “What would help right now?”  
- Offer safety through presence.

---

### 8. Conflict Styles & Triggers
Common conflict approaches:
- Compete  
- Avoid  
- Accommodate  
- Compromise  
- Collaborate  

Know your **stress triggers** and warning signs. Plan exits before conflict peaks.

---

### 9. Real-World Applications
- Protest panic: lower voice, give water, step back.  
- Volunteer disagreement: pause both, reflect shared goals.  
- Officer shouting: hands visible, minimal speech, record if safe.

---

### 10. Crisis Scenario Example
Someone is rocking, muttering, and unresponsive.  
**Steps:**  
1. Clear crowd space.  
2. Approach at safe distance.  
3. Ground with calm tone: “You’re safe. Can you feel your feet?”  
4. Offer sensory item (cold water, fabric).  
5. Signal trained support.

---

### 11. De-escalation Toolkit

| Tool | Function |
|-------|-----------|
| Voice & tone | Regulates pace |
| Body posture | Signals safety |
| Questions | Redirects focus |
| Silence | Lowers adrenaline |
| Allies | Swap in/out when needed |
