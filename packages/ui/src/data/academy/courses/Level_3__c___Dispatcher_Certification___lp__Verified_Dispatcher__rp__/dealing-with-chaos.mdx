## Overview

Chaos is normal in fieldwork. Radios drop, reports conflict, emotions spike.  
This module trains dispatchers and leads to **absorb disruption, reorient fast, and maintain safety.**

---

## Learn & Do

### Common Chaos Triggers

- Conflicting or false reports  
- Comms failures (Signal outage, burnout, phone loss)  
- Escalation from police or agitators  
- Tech errors or missing responders  
- Overwhelm or panic under stress  

<Callout type="warning">
It’s not about avoiding failure — it’s about spotting it early and acting with integrity.
</Callout>

---

### When Police Lie

Expect misinformation. Officers may:
- Deny a stop while detaining someone.  
- Claim to “help” while enforcing.  
- Provide false locations or timing.  

Trust **verified eyes and logs**, not official statements.  
Confirm using observer notes, video, or field corroboration.

---

### Rapid Reassessment Protocol (RRP)

When operations collapse or conditions shift:

1. **Pause + Zoom Out** – Name what’s real.  
2. **Reassign Roles** – Who’s active, who’s missing, who’s safe?  
3. **One Channel, One Message** – Use a single comms route for clarity.  
4. **Update Logs Briefly** – Note what changed and why.  
5. **Debrief Later** – Reflection prevents repetition.

<Callout type="hint">
If Signal fails, use [Runners & Rides](/courses/runners-and-rides) for physical handoffs.
</Callout>

---

### Emotional Regulation for Dispatchers

During chaos, your tone sets the emotional weather for the whole team.  

- Take one deep breath before responding.  
- Speak slowly, with grounding phrases:  
  > “Take a breath. Here’s the new plan.”  
- Avoid panic signaling — urgency ≠ emergency.  
- Model calm, not control.  

<Callout type="info">
You’re not a therapist, but your regulation shapes everyone else’s performance.
</Callout>

---

### Escalation Protocols

If field conditions spiral:

1. Ask: “Is anyone unsafe?”  
2. Contact the **legal observer or safety lead**.  
3. Pull responders back or relocate.  
4. Log what occurred for later care and accountability.  

> Ending a dispatch for safety is leadership, not failure.

<Callout type="info">
Coordinate with [Court Support](/courses/court-support) and [Legal Follow-through & Jail Support](/courses/legal-followthrough-jail-support) when detention or legal escalation occurs.
</Callout>

### Incident Aftercare

Every major incident deserves a **care-centered debrief.**

**Debrief Log:**
- What went right or wrong?  
- Who needs follow-up?  
- What can improve next time?  

**Care Practices:**
- Encourage rest and hydration.  
- Offer mutual peer check-ins.  
- Rotate leadership to prevent burnout.  

<Callout type="hint">
See [Crisis Debriefing & Mutual Care](/courses/crisis-debrief-care) and [Care & Emotional Support](/courses/care-support) for detailed models.
</Callout>

### Chaos Toolkit

Useful during high-pressure ops:
- Prewritten fallback messages  
- Rapid shutdown checklist (who to notify, what to log)  
- Local contact tree (tech, legal, admin)  
- Post-incident debrief template
