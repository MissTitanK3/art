# 🧩 Consensus & Rapid Decision Frameworks
<TrackBadge variant="pod-leadership" />
<TrackBadge variant="movement-strategy" />
<TrackBadge variant="admin" />

<Callout type="info">
Good decision-making is what keeps movements democratic *and* responsive.  
This course gives pods and regions clear, ethical frameworks for reaching consensus, acting fast, and documenting decisions transparently.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
Movements collapse when decisions become:
- Too slow to respond to emergencies  
- Too centralized to trust  
- Too emotional to sustain  

This course teaches how to **scale consent and consensus models** to different conditions — from calm planning to chaotic, real-time decisions.

You’ll learn:
- The difference between *consent*, *consensus*, and *coordination*  
- How to make decisions under pressure  
- How to document choices for accountability  
- When to pause, when to act, and when to delegate

> “Consensus is not everyone agreeing — it’s everyone being heard before moving forward.”

---

## Learn & Do

### 1. Decision Spectrum
Not all decisions need the same process.

| Type | Method | Example | Timeframe |
|------|---------|----------|------------|
| **Routine** | Pre-approved protocol | Daily supply drops | Minutes |
| **Operational** | Small-group consent | Change staging route | Hours |
| **Strategic** | Full consensus | Partner with another org | Days |
| **Emergency** | Delegated rapid call | Tear gas deployed, comms fail | Seconds |

Pods and regions must **pre-define** which decisions fall into which category.

<Callout type="hint">
The right framework is the one that fits the urgency and risk — not the one that feels most “democratic” in the abstract.
</Callout>

---

### 2. The Consensus Model (When Time Allows)
Consensus works best for **medium to long-term** planning and values-based decisions.

**Core steps:**
1. **Present the issue** clearly and briefly.  
2. **Clarify the scope** — what’s being decided, what’s not.  
3. **Invite input** — collect all concerns.  
4. **Synthesize proposals** that include dissent where possible.  
5. **Test for consent:**  
   - 👍 Support  
   - 👋 Can live with it  
   - ✋ Block (substantial ethical or safety concern)

Blocks trigger structured discussion — not personal defense.

**Document** all outcomes in pod notes or regional logs.

---

### 3. The Consent Model (When Time Is Limited)
Consent differs from consensus — it asks:
> “Is there any *reasoned objection* that would cause harm if we proceed?”

If none, proceed — even without full enthusiasm.  
This keeps things moving while still honoring safety and ethics.

**Use consent** when:
- Stakes are medium  
- Time is short  
- Team is trained in trust and transparency  

---

### 4. Rapid Decision Framework (RDF)
In high-stress situations, switch to the **RDF protocol** — a structured fallback when speed matters more than agreement.

**RDF Steps:**
1. **Name the threat:** “We have 2 minutes before dispersal.”  
2. **Identify leader-on-duty:** Preassigned or rotating role.  
3. **Request input:** “Anyone have strong objections?”  
4. **Decide:** Use leader judgment or majority of present team.  
5. **Communicate:** State decision and signal clearly.  
6. **Review later:** Always debrief afterward.

> Make fast decisions *reversible* when possible.

---

### 5. Decision Logging & Transparency
Every decision should leave a short paper or digital trail:
- Timestamp  
- Participants  
- Decision summary  
- Objections or conditions  
- Follow-up responsibility

This prevents memory bias and keeps accountability decentralized.

**Recommended tools:**
- Offline note templates  
- Encrypted pad or ART dispatch logs  
- Rotating “scribe” role in each pod  

---

### 6. Handling Dissent & Power Imbalance
Not all disagreement is bad — but unmanaged dissent drains trust.

| Pattern | Response |
|----------|-----------|
| **Silent withdrawal** | Ask privately what feels unsafe or unheard |
| **Repeated objection** | Clarify values misalignment; document |
| **Person vs. policy** | Refocus discussion on principles |
| **Leader override** | Always require written justification after |

Dissent doesn’t slow teams down — unspoken dissent does.

---

### 7. Cross-Pod and Regional Coordination
When multiple pods or regions are involved:
1. Define a **decision hub** — one signal or LoRa thread for coordination.  
2. Each pod sends **one delegate** with clear boundaries (what they can decide).  
3. Record all regional calls in a shared encrypted document.  
4. Use **time-boxed rounds** to prevent drift (e.g., “5-minute max per update”).

---

### 8. Ethical Decision Filters
Before any final call, run it through **3 quick tests:**

| Filter | Question |
|---------|-----------|
| **Safety** | Does this increase or reduce risk to people on the ground? |
| **Consent** | Have those affected had a chance to say no? |
| **Transparency** | Can this decision be explained openly without shame? |

If any answer is “no,” postpone or modify.

<Callout type="warning">
Speed never justifies harm.  
If acting fast endangers the people you serve, the decision is invalid.
</Callout>

---

### 9. Delegation & Trust
Rapid systems depend on delegation — but delegation depends on trust.

**Assign decision authority** by:
- Clear role: “Comms Lead may call an evacuation.”  
- Clear limits: “No one can release data or statements alone.”  
- Clear review: “All urgent decisions logged within 24 hours.”

Rotate decision authority often to prevent hierarchy calcification.

---

### 10. Post-Decision Review
Every rapid call deserves a short debrief:
- What did we decide and why?  
- Did the outcome align with our values?  
- Were dissenters heard?  
- Should we codify this process for next time?  

> Every debrief is a rehearsal for the next emergency.
