When phones fail or surveillance increases, **radios keep pods coordinated**. This course teaches you how to use walkie-talkies effectively, communicate with clarity, and maintain operational privacy in the field.

---

## Why Radios Matter

- 📴 **Phones can fail** — towers jammed or data throttled  
- 🔐 **Radios reduce surveillance risk**  
- 🧭 **Peer-to-peer communication** — no cell towers or accounts required  

---

## Types of Radios

| Type | Range | License | Best For |
|------|--------|----------|----------|
| **FRS** | 0.5–2 miles | No | Pod-level comms |
| **GMRS** | 1–5+ miles | Yes (FCC) | Neighborhood coordination |
| **HAM** | 2–100+ miles | Yes + Exam | Regional & disaster ops |
| **Mesh (LoRa)** | 0.5–3 miles/node | No | Text-only encrypted mesh |

---

## Basic Radio Etiquette

- 🎯 Keep messages **short, clear, calm**  
- 🚫 No names, addresses, or legal details  
- 🎙 Identify roles: “Blue 3 to Red 1”  

---

### Sample Exchange

> **[Red 1]**: “Blue 3 to Red 1 — eyes on visitors near 5th and Mission.”  
> **[Blue 3]**: “Copy. Heading there. ETA 3 minutes.”  
> **[Red 1]**: “Stay visible, no contact. Will update.”  
> **[Blue 3]**: “Understood. Blue 3 out.”

---

## Channel Protocols

- Pick a **primary** and a **backup** channel  
- Say “**Switch water**” or agreed cue before moving  
- Keep shared channels clear; move to secondary for chat  

---

## Callsigns, Codewords, and Pre-Arranged Phrasing

Good comms protect privacy while staying fast. Practice your setup until it feels natural.

### 📛 Callsigns

Use anonymous, functional identifiers.  
Avoid initials, dates, or revealing terms.

| Role | Callsign | Backup |
|------|-----------|--------|
| Field Lead | Red 1 | Red 5 |
| Legal Observer | Blue 2 | Blue 4 |
| Care Station | Green 3 | Green 7 |
| Rideshare | Yellow 6 | Yellow 9 |

---

### 🗣 Codewords

Assume open monitoring. Replace sensitive info with neutral terms.

| Meaning | Codeword | Intent |
|----------|-----------|--------|
| ICE/police presence | “Visitors” | Keeps tone calm |
| Surveillance van | “Grey box” | Avoids escalation |
| Arrest in progress | “Package moving” | Neutral phrasing |
| Location unsafe | “Flooded” | Evacuate |
| Need assistance | “Send a jacket” | Replaces “help” |

---

### 🔑 Pre-Arranged Phrases

Plan your language before deployment.

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---------|----------|
| “Check pulse on Blue 4” | Is Blue 4 active/safe? |
| “Grey box circling” | Surveillance vehicle nearby |
| “Transfer complete” | Person is safe |
| “Switch water” | Move to backup channel |

<Callout type="hint">
Keep a laminated pocket card of callsigns and codewords with your radio.
</Callout>

## Drills and Practice

Practice builds clarity and calm.

- Simulate short-range and obstacle scenarios  
- Run timed drills with only radio contact  
- Debrief: clarity, tone, and timing

Confidence in your codes equals safety under stress.

## Encryption & Privacy

- FRS/GMRS = **public**, no encryption  
- Use **codewords** for sensitive events  
- Assume **open airwaves**; speak accordingly  
- LoRa or digital mesh = private but limited range  

---

## Hardware & Setup

🛠 Checklist:
- Fully charge radios (bring spares)  
- Test range before deployment  
- Label with callsign  
- Use earpiece for discretion
