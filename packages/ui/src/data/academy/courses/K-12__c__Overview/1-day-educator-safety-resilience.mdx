# 1-Day Educator Safety & Resilience Workshop
<TrackBadge variant="default" />

<Callout type="info">
This session gives teachers practical skills they can use the next school day, without requiring new policies or specialized equipment.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
This day-long workshop introduces four pillars of school-safe resilience:
- Student safety and intervention  
- Trauma-informed support  
- Student rights in everyday contexts  
- Clear, calm school-community communication  

Educators leave with quick-reference tools, shared vocabulary, and confidence to navigate challenging student and family situations.

**Expected outcomes**
- Recognize early signs of escalation and apply verbal de-escalation  
- Provide basic stabilization during emotional overwhelm  
- Support rights-respecting interactions with authority figures  
- Communicate proactively without increasing panic or exposure

## Learn & Do

### Session 1: Safety & Intervention Essentials
**Topics**
- Bystander support
- Verbal de-escalation steps
- Protecting dignity in tense moments

**Practice Task**
Roleplay a hallway conflict using a 3-step calming script.

---

### Session 2: Trauma-Informed School Culture
**Topics**
- Stress responses in children vs adults
- Grounding methods for classrooms
- Peer support structures among staff

**Practice Task**
Create a mini “grounding station” plan for your room (2 items, clear rules).

#### Continuity Care for Elementary Students
Young children often show fear through behavior before words. Teachers can help keep routines stable when families face stress.

**Topics**
• Gentle check-ins that do not ask “why”  
• Who is allowed to pick the student up (without asking status)  
• Safe caregiver communication when phones or schedules change  
• Maintaining stable classroom routines after a scare  

**Early Signs to Notice**
• Sudden clinginess or separation anxiety  
• Stomach aches before school  
• Change in drawing themes (fear, uniforms, handcuffs)  
• New silence around family topics  

**Practice Task**
Create a calm pickup transition plan:
- 2 adult contacts approved by the caregiver
- Short script for explaining changes (“Adults are making sure you stay safe.”)
- One activity that helps the child feel secure while waiting  

<Callout type="warning">
Do not ask or guess immigration status. Let caregivers choose what they share.
</Callout>

#### Continuity Care for Middle School Students
Adolescents may hide worry to protect peers or family. Clear options reduce panic without forcing disclosure.

**Topics**
• Privacy-respecting communication  
• Helping students identify **two safe adults** for emergencies  
• When to gently loop in counselors  
• Supporting friendship networks that reduce isolation  

**Early Signs to Notice**
• Abrupt grade drops or missing assignments  
• Humor masking fear or anger spikes  
• Rumor-driven panic about law enforcement stories  
• Avoiding rides home or hanging back after school  

**Practice Task**
Draft a “quiet stabilization” plan:
- A trusted location (library, counselor corner)  
- A 1-minute grounding routine  
- A discreet support signal with a teacher (“Notebook on desk”)  

<Callout type="warning">
Never imply consequences for what a student shares about home life.
</Callout>

#### Continuity Care for High School Students
Older youth manage complex fear, responsibility, and secrecy. Support should reinforce autonomy while removing avoidable risk.

**Topics**
• Consent-centered crisis planning  
• Clarifying **who to call** if a caregiver is suddenly unavailable  
• Protecting confidential communications with families  
• Helping students maintain school goals during instability  

**Early Signs to Notice**
• Protecting siblings at their own expense  
• Sudden need for work hours or caregiving roles  
• Stop-outs, absences, or late arrivals unexplained  
• Expressed fear of officials or paperwork  

**Practice Task**
Create a **private continuity card**:
- Preferred contact order (student-chosen)
- Code language for “I need help now”
- One academic stability priority (attendance, assignment portal, counselor check-ins)

<Callout type="warning">
If a student reveals sensitive risk, **do not** document status. Escalate only for safety, not surveillance.
</Callout>

---

### Session 3: Student Rights & Community Consent
**Topics**
- Know Your Rights Basics in school context
- Recording and documentation do/don’t
- Consent-centered parent engagement

**Practice Task**
Case walk-through: A student films a stressful encounter.  
Identify what the teacher’s role is and isn’t.

---

### Session 4: Coordinated Communication Under Stress
**Topics**
- Clear language for families during crises
- Micro-reporting (short, factual updates)
- Documentation & safety considerations

**Practice Task**
Draft a 3-sentence crisis update that avoids blame or triggers.

<Callout type="warning">
Keep student data private. Do not collect or store sensitive identity information.
</Callout>
