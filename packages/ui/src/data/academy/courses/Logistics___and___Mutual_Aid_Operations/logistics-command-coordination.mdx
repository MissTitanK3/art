## Overview
During crisis events, supply and communication channels fragment.  
Without a structured logistics command, pods duplicate effort, lose track of stock, or miss urgent needs.  
This module defines **field-tested coordination methods** to synchronize volunteers, warehouses, and dispatch systems.

You’ll learn how to:
- Establish a logistics command node under stress  
- Coordinate requests, sourcing, and delivery transparently  
- Maintain accountability without bureaucracy  
- Operate during tech failure or isolation  

> “Command is not control — it’s synchronization.”

---

## Learn & Do

### 1. Building a Logistics Command Node
Every large-scale operation needs a **coordination nucleus** that tracks movement, stock, and requests.

**Minimum structure:**
- **Comms Lead:** Handles incoming requests  
- **Logistics Officer:** Assigns runs and manages stock  
- **Transport Coordinator:** Tracks drivers/runners  
- **Supply Verifier:** Confirms quality, quantity, and chain of custody  
- **Safety Lead:** Confirms readiness and security before departure  

Use whiteboards, printed manifests, or encrypted tablets. The key is clarity and redundancy, not fancy software.

---

### 2. Chain of Custody & Verification
Trust comes from traceability.

Each handoff should record:
- Item  
- Quantity  
- From / To  
- Timestamp  
- Condition  
- Verified by (initials or call sign)

Paper manifests are acceptable if digital fails.  
Digitize when possible for accountability and audit.

<Callout type="warning">
Never transport personal medical data, names of recipients, or GPS-tagged logs. Use coded IDs or aliases for privacy.
</Callout>

---

### 3. Rapid Request Verification
In chaotic situations, false or duplicated requests waste resources.  
Verification should take **under 2 minutes**.

**Checklist:**
1. Who is making the request? (Pod, dispatcher, or field team)  
2. What is the exact need and quantity?  
3. Can another source confirm? (cross-check Signal thread or dispatch log)  
4. Is it life-safety critical or deferrable?  
5. Assign unique request ID.

If in doubt, mark **pending verification** and revisit after immediate needs are covered.

---

### 4. Dispatch Integration
All logistics should connect to dispatch operations.

| Function | Coordination Method |
|-----------|--------------------|
| Field Request | Entered through Watch or dispatcher intake |
| Assignment | Logistics node matches to resource holder |
| Transport | Runners or drivers notified via secure comms |
| Confirmation | Arrival verified by both ends |
| Update | Inventory and dispatch record updated |

When systems are offline, use radio or phone fallback with pre-agreed code phrases.

---

### 5. Mobile Command Setup
If stationary hubs are unsafe or inaccessible, deploy **mobile logistics command posts**.

**Requirements:**
- Power (inverter, battery, solar)  
- Printed or laminated manifests  
- Radio/Signal device with secure group access  
- Folding table or flat surface for routing layout  
- Emergency signage (supply, med, comms)  

These can operate from vehicles, community centers, or tents.

---

### 6. Offline Coordination Tools
Plan for **connectivity loss** as default, not exception.

**Offline kit includes:**
- Local mesh or LoRa node with offline packet sync  
- USB or SD data transfer for manifests  
- QR or short code request forms  
- Printed rotation lists and call signs  
- Physical map with zones and rally points  

After restoration, sync logs and resolve duplicates manually.

---

### 7. Safety & Accountability in Transit
Before deployment, every driver or runner confirms:
- Destination and fallback  
- Supply manifest count  
- Radio/Signal check-in times  
- Contact if delayed or detained  
- Emergency procedure if route fails  

If a team misses two check-ins, activate **Search & Verify Protocol** (contact, reroute, or pause deployment).

---

### 8. Coordinating Across Regions
When local capacity collapses:
1. Identify neighboring region supply leads.  
2. Use shared resource format (item, qty, ETA, status).  
3. Confirm transport and border protocols (jurisdiction, permits).  
4. Maintain consistent labeling between regions.  
5. Record inter-region shipments in audit logs.

> Inter-region trade succeeds on clarity, not speed.

---

### 9. Demobilization & Post-Action Recovery
When an operation ends:
- Reconcile manifests and remaining stock.  
- Sanitize and repackage reusable gear.  
- Record lessons learned and shortages.  
- Identify equipment to repair or replace.  
- Archive anonymized data for training use.

Close the loop by debriefing all coordinators and updating checklists.
