# 🧩 Community Defense Center (CDC) Logistics & Site Operations
<TrackBadge variant="logistics" />

<Callout type="info">
This course covers how to keep a Community Defense Center running once it’s active. It focuses on maintaining order, resource flow, communication, and community safety—without burnout or confusion.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
Setting up a CDC is only the beginning. Once open, it becomes a living system: volunteers rotate, supplies move, and the surrounding community engages.  

Without clear logistics, a CDC can quickly slide into chaos—duplicated tasks, missing supplies, or unsafe crowding. This course gives you a working model to **run daily operations**, balance care and security, and make real-time adjustments when conditions change.

You’ll learn how to:
- Maintain safe and organized physical layouts.  
- Coordinate volunteer shifts and supply flow.  
- Handle security incidents and medical needs responsibly.  
- Keep transparent communication across pods and dispatch.  

---

## Learn & Do

✅ **1. Establish site zones**
- Divide your space into clear functional areas:
  - **Welcome / Intake** – first contact, orientation, accessibility info.  
  - **Supplies / Distribution** – food, hygiene, mutual aid goods.  
  - **Medical / Wellness** – first aid, hydration, emotional support.  
  - **Comms / Dispatch** – radios, maps, event logs.  
  - **Quiet or Decompression Area** – for rest and emotional regulation.  
- Mark each zone with clear signage and paths for accessibility.  

🔍 *Task:* Sketch your CDC layout on paper. Label zones, entry points, and a secure staff-only area.

✅ **2. Coordinate supply flow**
- Track incoming and outgoing supplies with simple logs (paper or digital).  
- Assign one **Supply Steward** per shift to handle inventory and resupply requests.  
- Group supplies by urgency:  
  - *Tier 1:* Medical, water, food.  
  - *Tier 2:* Shelter, hygiene.  
  - *Tier 3:* Outreach, educational, comfort.  
- Keep backup caches at least one block away for continuity if displaced.

🧩 *Example:* During a busy CDC day, you realize the hydration station is out of cups. Instead of pausing operations, your Supply Steward pulls from the Tier 2 backup cache while a volunteer restocks.

---

✅ **3. Manage volunteer flow**
- Use a **Check-in / Check-out Log** for all volunteers.  
- Brief every new volunteer on:  
  - Site layout and current priorities.  
  - Safety, consent, and communication protocols.  
  - When to escalate to dispatch or site lead.  
- Rotate breaks to prevent burnout and emotional fatigue.  

⚙️ *Task:* Build a short rotation chart showing who is on each zone for a 4-hour shift.

---

✅ **4. Communication and situational updates**
- Hold brief **huddles** at set intervals (e.g., every 2 hours).  
- Keep one **Comms Coordinator** linked to dispatch or radio.  
- Use color or status boards for visible updates:
  - Green – stable operations  
  - Yellow – supply issue or staff shortage  
  - Red – safety or medical event  
- Summarize incidents in short bullet notes for AAR review.

---

✅ **5. Handle safety and escalation**
- Identify the **Safety Point** on-site (a clearly marked visible area).  
- All conflicts or medical concerns route through this point.  
- Use de-escalation and consent-based approaches before calling for external help.  
- Document incidents factually: time, event, action taken, and who responded.  

<Callout type="warning">
⚠️ Always separate “support space” from “security space.”  
Never mix wellness or first aid with enforcement or intervention zones.
</Callout>

---

✅ **6. Maintain accessibility and inclusion**
- Ensure ramps, seating, and rest zones are available.  
- Provide multilingual signage and quiet communication options (text, icons).  
- Check lighting, sound levels, and crowding regularly.  
- Rotate interpreters or accessibility stewards if available.

---

✅ **7. Plan for closing or transition**
- Announce closing at least 30 minutes ahead.  
- Account for all supplies, logs, and first-aid kits.  
- Debrief the team and record lessons learned.  
- Notify dispatch or regional coordination when the site status changes.  

🔍 *Task:* Draft a short “End of Day” checklist with steps to close your CDC safely.
