## 🧭 Overview
Movements survive by evolving strategically while holding a moral compass.  
This module helps teams plan for **long-term impact** — not just reactionary action — by rooting tactics in shared values, transparent decision-making, and sustainability.

> Every choice builds the future. Strategy defines what kind.

---

## 🧩 Learn & Do

### 1. Align Values with Tactics
Before acting, define your **movement ethics statement**:
- Why does this movement exist?  
- What harm will we refuse to reproduce?  
- How do we define success beyond visibility?  
- Who are we accountable to?

Turn those principles into **operational filters** for decision-making:
> “Does this action build power without replicating oppression?”

---

### 2. Strategic Layering
Use the **three horizons** model to guide growth:

| Horizon | Focus | Example |
|----------|--------|----------|
| Immediate | Safety & survival | Rapid response, legal defense |
| Mid-Term | Infrastructure | Pod networks, logistics hubs |
| Long-Term | Transformation | Education, policy, culture shift |

A balanced strategy works across all three at once.

---

### 3. Building Feedback Loops
Healthy movements measure impact and adapt.

1. Collect anonymous feedback after every major action.  
2. Hold internal “what worked / what didn’t” debriefs.  
3. Archive lessons into a living strategy document.  
4. Revisit principles quarterly to prevent drift.

<Callout type="hint">
Metrics aren’t just numbers — they include trust, safety, and community morale.
</Callout>

---

### 4. Preventing Ethical Drift
Signs of ethical drift:
- Urgency replaces consent  
- Visibility outweighs safety  
- Leadership becomes unquestioned  
- Transparency declines  

Countermeasures:
- Rotate leadership roles  
- Publish meeting notes openly  
- Encourage dissent as contribution  
- Review harm and repair processes regularly

---

### 5. Coordination Ethics
Cross-region and cross-movement collaboration require **trust boundaries**:
- Share only relevant data, not full datasets  
- Credit contributors properly  
- Respect local autonomy — don’t override regional judgment  
- Use consent-based escalation for resource or strategy sharing

> The network’s integrity is more important than any single event.

---

### 6. Sustainability in Strategy
- Schedule rest into campaign timelines  
- Budget for care work, not just gear  
- Train replacements for every role  
- Archive institutional memory for continuity

Long-term ethics require **continuity**, not endless urgency.
