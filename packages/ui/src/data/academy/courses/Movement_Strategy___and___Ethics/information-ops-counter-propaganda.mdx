# 🧠 Information Operations & Counter-Propaganda for Activists
<TrackBadge variant="movement-strategy" />

<Callout type="info">
Movements thrive on trust and coordination. This course helps teams recognize and neutralize information warfare tactics—without feeding them or compromising safety.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
Disinformation and infiltration campaigns are not abstract threats—they are active strategies used to weaken grassroots networks. These tactics exploit emotional triggers, distort truth, and erode trust between crews, pods, and dispatchers.  

Common modern examples include:
- **False reports during protests** intended to misdirect volunteers or draw people into unsafe zones.  
- **Fake screenshots or AI-generated statements** attributed to known organizers to create infighting.  
- **Bot amplification** of divisive or fear-inducing content to fracture online movements.  

This course trains activists, dispatchers, and regional admins to identify these patterns early, contain their spread, and build organizational immunity against coordinated manipulation.

---

## Learn & Do

✅ **1. Recognize common tactics**
- **False urgency:** “ICE is raiding now!” messages without verifiable location data.  
- **Identity hijacking:** cloned or renamed social media accounts mimicking real members.  
- **Emotional manipulation:** framing events to incite despair or moral panic.  
- **Divide-and-conquer:** narratives that pit pods, ethnic groups, or ideological factions against each other.  

🔍 *Task:* Review the last week of public posts in your region’s chat or Signal group. Identify one example that could fit a disinformation tactic—even if unintentional.

---

✅ **2. Verify before amplifying**
- Cross-check through trusted regional or dispatch channels.  
- Confirm the **who**, **where**, and **when** of any claim before acting.  
- Use metadata, hashes, or screenshot timestamps *only when ethically safe*.  
- Privately archive questionable posts in a secure folder for later verification.  

🔍 *Task:* Create a small “Verification Protocol” checklist your team can use during active operations.

✅ **3. Contain internal rumors**
- Correct misinformation inside Signal or Matrix groups early and calmly.  
- Use one **designated communicator** for official clarifications.  
- Never debate false claims publicly—doing so can increase their reach.  
- Encourage teammates to check for “confirmation requests” before reposting.  

🧩 *Example:* During a protest weekend, a fake screenshot claims an organizer was arrested. Instead of reposting, the dispatcher team verifies directly with trusted contacts, then posts a calm, factual correction in all regional channels.

---

✅ **4. Strengthen trust infrastructure**
- Rotate group moderators every few months to reduce overcentralization.  
- Require **two confirmations** for high-risk intel (e.g., police movement).  
- Train members in *“assume confusion, not malice”* to reduce paranoia cycles.  
- Maintain a short list of **verified local contacts** for real-world cross-checking.  

⚙️ *Task:* Map your region’s current information flow. Identify choke points or single sources of truth that might need backup.

---

✅ **5. Build counter-narratives strategically**
- Use short, factual updates instead of emotional rebuttals.  
- Pair corrections with **actionable context**, not outrage.  
- Debunk the falsehood *and* explain the likely intent behind it (e.g., “This seems designed to create panic and mistrust. Let’s stay focused on facts.”).  
- When possible, issue statements through community-trusted pages or relay networks.

---

✅ **6. Practice mental resilience**
- Recognize fatigue, cynicism, and despair as intentional outcomes of information warfare.  
- Use **“pause protocols”**: when overwhelmed, step away, breathe, verify, then re-engage.  
- Create emotional support loops between dispatchers and field volunteers to decompress after misinformation surges.  

<Callout type="warning">
⚠️ Never publicly accuse individuals of infiltration or deception without verified evidence.  
Such accusations can backfire, fracture networks, and endanger lives.
</Callout>
