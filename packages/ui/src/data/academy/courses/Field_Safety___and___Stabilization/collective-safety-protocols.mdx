# 📋 Security Protocols for Collective Safety
<TrackBadge variant="default" />

<Callout type="warning">
🚨 Most risks in activist organizing are **collective**, not individual.  
This module teaches how to create clear roles, boundaries, and recovery plans that protect everyone from compromise or burnout.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
When one person overexposes or breaks protocol, an entire pod can be jeopardized.  
Collective safety requires culture, not paranoia: boundaries, rotation, and accountability.  
You’ll learn:
- How to prevent shared exposure  
- When to separate roles  
- What to do after leaks, arrests, or device compromise  
- Minimum standards for any pod or dispatch team  

---

## Learn & Do

### 1. Understanding Shared Exposure

| Scenario | Risk |
|----------|------|
| Dispatcher also works on the ground | Connects digital + physical identity |
| Chats mix trained + new members | Easier leaks |
| One comms tool for all roles | Single point of failure |
| Undefined duties | Nobody watches OPSEC |

**Shared exposure** means one person’s error endangers everyone.  
Resilience comes from distributed responsibility.

---

### 2. Baseline Safety Protocols

#### 🧍 Role Separation

| Role Pair | Allowed? |
|------------|-----------|
| OTG Field Lead + Dispatcher | ❌ Never |
| Media Poster + Dispatch Coordinator | ⚠️ With review |
| Pod Coordinator + Watch Reporter | ✅ Usually safe |
| Tech Maintainer + Comms | ✅ If isolated devices |

💡 No one should hold all access keys or system privileges.

#### 🔁 Exposure Rotation
- Change who handles public roles regularly.  
- Use aliases or role handles (not real names).  
- Rotate Signal accounts or burner devices safely.  
- Limit repeated exposure at events or press moments.

> You don’t get credit for doing everything. You get risk.

---

### 3. Device Hygiene

| Rule | Purpose |
|------|----------|
| Phones off during ops | Block passive tracking |
| Use burners for dispatch | Compartmentalize identity |
| One role per device | Stop cross-contamination |
| Clear caches/logs weekly | Reduce trace data |

<Callout type="hint">
If multiple Signal IDs are required, use cloned apps or secondary devices—but prefer physical separation.
</Callout>

See also [Burner Phones & Security Culture](/courses/burner-phones-security-culture) and [Digital Security Basics](/courses/digital-security-basics).

---

### 4. When Things Go Wrong

**Arrest:**
1. Revoke access immediately.  
2. Assume device compromise.  
3. Cease digital discussion of related ops.  
4. Wait for legal confirmation before resuming.

**Leak:**
1. Pause ops 24–72 hrs.  
2. Rotate tools and passwords.  
3. Review exposure chain offline.  
4. Retrain and reset protocols.

<Callout type="warning">
Don’t push forward after exposure. Pause, review, and resecure.
</Callout>

---

### 5. Minimum Standards for Pods

| Category | Protocol |
|-----------|-----------|
| Onboarding | Level-1 completion or supervised entry |
| Roles | One active role per op |
| Signal | Alias handles, no faces in avatars |
| Offline Sync | Weekly printed updates |
| Emergency Plan | Contact tree, arrest & retreat plan |
| Dispatcher Safety | No field work 72 hrs post-action |

See [Documentation Safety](/courses/documentation-safety) and [After-Action Data Hygiene](/courses/after-action-data-hygiene) for retention practices.

---

### 6. “Need-to-Know” Model

| Role | Information Access |
|------|--------------------|
| Outreach Volunteer | Location + pod alias |
| Pod Lead | Limited member info |
| Dispatcher | Coded pod locations |
| Public Comms | General summaries only |

Reduce the blast radius of any breach.

---

### 7. Risk Tier Assessment

| Tier | Description | Example |
|------|--------------|----------|
| 🔵 Low | No sensitive data | Print team |
| 🟡 Medium | Occasional field or tech work | Pod creator |
| 🔴 High | Core dispatch or backend access | Admins |

Match countermeasures (e.g., encryption, rotation) to tier.

---

### 8. Practice Scenario
> A new member posts a group selfie from a field op with hashtags identifying the pod.

**Corrective Steps**
1. Remove the post.  
2. Conduct calm debrief and retraining.  
3. Review onboarding process.  
4. File internal incident log.  

Coordinate with [Runners & Rides](/courses/runners-and-rides) for on-site handoffs or [Bystander Support Tactics](/courses/bystander-support) for public interactions.

---

### 9. Sample Pod Safety Policy
> - Use only aliases or first names  
> - Rotate roles every 2–4 weeks  
> - Keep field and digital devices separate  
> - No public posts without review  
> - Report safety issues early, without blame  

Turn this into your pod’s safety charter or code of conduct.
