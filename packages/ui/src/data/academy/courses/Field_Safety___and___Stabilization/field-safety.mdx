## Overview
Field safety means maintaining awareness, composure, and communication in unpredictable conditions.  
You’ll learn to:
- Read threats and environmental cues  
- Move calmly and strategically under pressure  
- Coordinate through trusted signals and fallback plans  
- Protect yourself, teammates, and vulnerable participants  

---

## Learn & Do

### 1. Core Situational Awareness
Follow the **OODA Loop**:
1. **Observe**: Notice exits, crowd flow, and officer movement.  
2. **Orient**: Understand your role and surroundings.  
3. **Decide**: Choose safe, informed actions.  
4. **Act**: Move with intention, not panic.

> Calm observation prevents confusion and injury.

---

### 2. Threat Recognition

Watch for:
- Aggressive police formations  
- Kettling patterns or vehicles closing in  
- Unmarked vans or plainclothes agents  
- Provocateurs trying to incite escalation  
- Sudden crowd surges or panic triggers  

<Callout type="warning">
Undercover agents may mimic protest gear or language. Watch for isolated actors urging confrontation.
</Callout>

---

### 3. Essential Gear

| Gear | Purpose |
|------|----------|
| ID + emergency contact | For arrest or injury |
| Mask + goggles | Protect against gas, cameras |
| Gloves | Protection + hygiene |
| Small medkit | Bleeds and burns |
| Burner phone | Reduces tracking risk |
| Hydration pack | Keeps hands free |
| Cash ($20–50) | Transit or emergency aid |
| Snacks | Sustains energy |

Avoid carrying anything that could imply intent (tools, projectiles, flyers).

<Callout type="info">
Harden devices and compartmentalize data. See [Digital Security Basics](/courses/digital-security-basics) and [Burner Phones & Security Culture](/courses/burner-phones-security-culture).
</Callout>

---

### 4. Crowd Dynamics

- Stay on **edges** if not part of frontline roles  
- Move diagonally to reduce crush risk  
- Maintain open sightlines and exits  
- Help fallen or frozen people up calmly  

<Callout type="tip">
Use hand signals instead of shouting to maintain calm and coordination.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info">
Language access saves time. Coordinate with members holding [Translator Micro-badge](/courses/translator-micro-badge) or [ASL Interpreter Micro-badge](/courses/asl-interpreter-micro-badge).
</Callout>

---

### 5. Field Hand Signals

| Signal | Meaning |
|--------|----------|
| ✋ Palm out | Stop / Hold position |
| 🔁 Circle overhead | Regroup / fallback |
| 👉 Two shoulder taps | Follow me |
| ❤️ Hand over heart | Need medical help |

---

### 6. Know Your Role
- Define your role (medic, scout, observer).  
- Stay within your assigned zone.  
- Check in with team leads frequently.  
- Never go alone into red zones.

---

### 7. Radio & Signal Protocols
- Use encrypted or burner comms when possible.  
- Keep messages short and coded.  
- Never share names or sensitive locations.  
- Establish fallback words for regrouping.  

<Callout type="hint">
When networks fail, rely on runners and brevity codes.  
See [Digital Security Basics](/courses/digital-security-basics).
</Callout>

---

### 8. Escalation Responses

**If situation worsens:**
- Breathe.  
- Move with practiced motions.  
- Exit unless critical role requires staying.  
- Notify dispatcher or buddy with location code.

<Callout type="info">
If recording, blur faces and follow [Documentation Safety](/courses/documentation-safety) and [After-Action Data Hygiene](/courses/after-action-data-hygiene).
</Callout>

---

### 9. Risk Zones to Avoid

| Zone | Risk |
|------|------|
| Narrow alleys | Containment or assault risk |
| Rooftops | Visibility + sniper risk |
| Parked vans | Possible detainment |
| Residential blocks | Legal exposure, bystanders |

---

### 10. Reading Your Body
If you feel dizzy, tense, or frozen—step back, breathe, and check environment.  
Your instincts often detect danger before your conscious mind.

> Ask: “Is it safe to stay?”  
If unsure, withdraw and regroup.

---

### 11. Team Protocols
- Check in/out with dispatcher  
- Assign fallback zones  
- Use green/yellow/red risk language  
- Confirm all roles before deployment  

---

### 12. Emergency Responses

#### Kettling
- Move diagonally toward edge  
- Identify alleys or open buildings  
- Use hand signals for coordination  
- Document from distance if safe  

#### Tear Gas
- Move crosswind, not into wind  
- Cover mouth/nose with damp cloth  
- Flush eyes with **water or saline only**

#### Arrests
- Record from distance  
- Note time, place, and names  
- Do not intervene physically  

#### Medical Emergency
- Signal medic  
- Clear space  
- Apply basic aid if trained  

<Callout type="hint">
Know your rights. Review [KYR Basics](/courses/kyr-basics) and link affected people with [Court Support](/courses/court-support) and [Legal Follow-through & Jail Support](/courses/legal-followthrough-jail-support).
</Callout>
