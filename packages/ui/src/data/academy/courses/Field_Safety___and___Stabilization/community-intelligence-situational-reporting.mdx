# 🛰️ Community Intelligence & Situational Reporting
<TrackBadge variant="field-safety" />

<Callout type="info">
This course teaches how to observe and report clearly under pressure. Good reporting keeps everyone safer, reduces confusion, and strengthens coordination across pods and dispatch teams.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
During rapid response or mutual aid deployments, confusion spreads faster than facts. Clear, structured situational reporting—sometimes called **community intelligence**—helps your region act on verified data instead of rumor.  

The goal isn’t to spy or collect personal information. It’s to **accurately describe what’s happening, where, and what’s needed**, without exposing anyone to risk.  

This skill is critical during:
- Field actions or protests  
- Disaster response or supply drops  
- Community Defense Center (CDC) operations  
- Evacuations or escalations involving state actors  

You’ll learn to identify what details matter, communicate them effectively, and protect sensitive data at all times.

---

## Learn & Do

✅ **1. Observe without bias**
- Focus on **what you see and hear**, not what you assume.  
- Note time, location, and environmental context first.  
- Avoid political commentary or speculation in your report.  
- Describe behavior, clothing, or vehicle markings factually and without personal identifiers.  

🔍 *Task:* During your next patrol or event, practice observing for 5 minutes silently. Afterwards, write a short description using only verifiable facts.

---

✅ **2. Structure your report**
- Use the **“5W1H” model**: Who, What, Where, When, Why, and How.  
- Prioritize **time and place**, then add verified details.  
- Example:  
  “At 14:32 near MLK & 13th, unmarked black SUV parked facing south, occupants remained inside for 10 minutes. No visible insignia.”

✅ **3. Transmit safely**
- Send your report via **secure Signal or offline mesh** if available.  
- Never post sensitive updates to public social media.  
- If network visibility is a risk, delay non-urgent reports until in a safe zone.  
- Avoid including identifying photos unless explicitly authorized by dispatch.  

🧩 *Example:* You witness a police vehicle patrolling near a food drop. You text dispatch:  
  “Two marked vehicles, 16:10, 9th & Pine, no interaction, observed 3 min.”  
  Not: “Cops everywhere harassing people!!”  

---

✅ **4. Distinguish levels of priority**
- **Critical - Red:** Imminent threat to safety. Needs dispatch action now.  
- **Important - Orange:** May affect operations; dispatch should log and monitor.  
- **Routine - Green:** Informational, for context or post-event analysis.  

⚙️ *Task:* Review three past reports from your region and re-label them using these levels.

---

✅ **5. Maintain chain of trust**
- Verify who you are reporting to and why.  
- Log the time, sender, and channel of each report.  
- Use unique report IDs if your region supports them.  
- Store summaries in encrypted or offline-safe storage if archiving is required.

<Callout type="warning">
⚠️ Do not collect or transmit personal identifying data (faces, license plates, names) unless absolutely necessary and approved by dispatch leadership.
</Callout>

---

✅ **6. Debrief and learn**
- After each deployment, review what reports were useful or unclear.  
- Convert notes into structured logs for *After-Action Review (AAR)*.  
- Share templates or improvements with nearby pods.
