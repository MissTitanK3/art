## Overview

Reviewing reports is a crucial safeguard in the ART ecosystem. It ensures that all public data is trustworthy and that no one is put at risk due to panic, error, or misinformation.  
As a reviewer, you are the **final checkpoint** before information becomes public.

---

## Learn & Do

### Why Review Reports?

Before publication, every report must:
- Be verified for accuracy and safety  
- Meet privacy and metadata standards  
- Provide clear, actionable facts — not rumors  

Reviewers protect both the community and the platform’s integrity.

---

### The Report Review Lifecycle

1. **Report Submitted**  
2. **Automated Filters** (spam, GPS errors)  
3. **Human Review**  
   - Accept  
   - Send Back  
   - Reject  
   - Publish or Archive  

Each stage ensures precision before public posting.

---

### What You Receive

Each submission may include:
- 📍 Location (GPS + text)
- 🕒 Time (auto + user-provided)
- 📝 Description of event
- 📸 Optional media
- ☎️ Contact info (may be anonymous)

Advanced reviewers may also see:
- 🔁 Prior reports from the same device/IP
- 🧭 Duplicate detection
- 🧾 Media metadata (EXIF, GPS, timestamp)

---

### Valid Reports Checklist

| Field | Requirement |
|--------|--------------|
| Location | Real and specific |
| Time | Recent and realistic |
| Description | Firsthand, factual |
| Media | Optional but should align with claim |

---

### Reasons to Reject

- Vague or secondhand info (“I heard…”)  
- Panic or false claims  
- Duplicate submissions  
- GPS or timestamp anomalies  
- Reused text or suspicious phrasing  

If unclear but not malicious, **Send Back** for clarification.

---

### Practice Examples

#### ✅ Example A  
> “Saw 4 ICE agents outside courthouse on 7th Street at 10:30am. Black SUV, CA plates.”  
**Accept** — Meets all standards.

#### ❌ Example B  
> “They’re everywhere. ICE is rounding up people!”  
**Reject** — Too vague, panic-inducing.

#### 🟡 Example C  
> “ICE checkpoint on Main & Rose, 3 cars turning people away.”  
**Send Back** — Clarify agent identity or observation.

---

### Respecting Anonymity

Many reporters face high risk.  
- Never identify or trace users  
- Avoid metadata probing beyond the platform  
- Redact faces, plates, or signage  
- Delete temporary review files after completion  

<Callout type="warning">
Even well-intentioned leaks can expose individuals. Respect consent and privacy at all times.
</Callout>

---

### Context & Judgment

Cross-check patterns:
- Is this location known for ICE presence?  
- Are there matching reports nearby?  
- Is timestamp consistent with others?  

<Callout type="hint">
Use encrypted, time-bound notes for documentation.  
Avoid saving review data in personal or shared apps.
</Callout>

---

### Red Flags for Disinformation

Watch for:
- Old timestamps marked “urgent”  
- Repeated phrasing across reports  
- Extreme tone or exaggeration  
- Image inconsistencies (weather, lighting)  
- GPS far from claim site  

When unsure, **Tag for Senior Review** and document reasoning.

---

### Escalation Process

If something feels off:
- Tag for partner review  
- Leave a clear moderation note  
- Use internal channels (Signal/Discord) for rapid discussion  

Maintain transparency — the audit trail reinforces trust.

---

### Decision Table

| Action | Use When |
|--------|-----------|
| ✅ Publish | Verified and accurate |
| 🔄 Send Back | Minor clarification needed |
| ❌ Reject | Clearly false or unverifiable |

---

### Legal & Ethical Boundaries

- Do not reverse-search beyond approved tools.  
- Never share media or logs externally.  
- Always redact identifying visuals unless consented.

### Reliable Reviewing Habits

- Keep the live map open for context  
- Focus on observed, not emotional, details  
- Review calmly — accuracy matters more than speed  

> “Our credibility lives or dies in the details. Be rigorous, be human, be brave.”
