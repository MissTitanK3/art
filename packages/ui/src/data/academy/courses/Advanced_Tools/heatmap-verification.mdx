# Heatmap Verification
<TrackBadge variant="default" />

<Callout type="info">
Use clear, approachable language. Keep steps small and predictable.
</Callout>

---

## Overview

The ART heatmap shows recent verified/ unverified law enforcement or ICE presence reported by the public.  
This course trains verifiers to confirm accuracy, protect privacy, and prevent harmful misinformation.

Verification decisions directly affect public safety, so each step emphasizes accuracy, ethics, and consent.

---

## Learn & Do

### Why Verification Matters

Verified reports help communities:
- Avoid danger zones  
- Mobilize support quickly  
- Recognize enforcement patterns  

> Verified reports reduce false alarms by over **80%** in our network.

<Callout type="warning">
Misleading reports can create panic, mistrust, and risk of harm.
</Callout>

---

### The Verification Pipeline

1. **Automatic Filter**  
   Removes spam, duplicates, and vague entries.  

2. **Human Review**  
   A certified verifier checks accuracy and requests clarification securely (e.g., via Signal).  

   <Callout type="info">
   Language access matters. Coordinate interpreters through  
   [Translator Micro-badge](/courses/translator-micro-badge) or  
   [ASL Interpreter Micro-badge](/courses/asl-interpreter-micro-badge).
   </Callout>

3. **Decision Point**  
   - ✅ Verify & Publish  
   - 🟡 Request Clarification  
   - ❌ Mark as Unverifiable  
   - 🟢 Escalate to Senior Verifier

---

### Step-by-Step Verification

#### 1. Read Carefully
Check for:
- Clear location (address, landmark, or intersection)  
- Valid timestamp  
- Descriptions of agents, vehicles, or uniforms  
- Attached media  

<Callout type="hint">
If edits might change meaning, ask the reporter to update the text themselves.
</Callout>

#### 2. Check for Red Flags
- “ICE is everywhere today” (too vague)  
- Future timestamps  
- Secondhand claims  
- Reused media or stock photos  

---

### Map & Location Check

Use OpenStreetMap, Google Maps, or internal tools to:
- Confirm location exists  
- Spot implausible entries  
- Validate via Street View  
- Compare with nearby verified reports  

<Callout type="warning">
Watch for spoofed coordinates or copy-pasted language from older posts.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info">
Sensitive sites (e.g., shelters) require dispatch coordination.  
See [Dispatch Coordination](/courses/dispatch-coordination) and  
[Responding to Dispatch Calls](/courses/responding-to-dispatch-calls).
</Callout>

---

### Decision Table

| Action | Use When… |
|--------|------------|
| ✅ Verify & Publish | Confirmed time and location |
| 🟡 Request Clarification | Missing detail but still plausible |
| ❌ Mark as Unverifiable | Too vague, unverifiable, or misleading |
| 🟢 Escalate | Suspicious media or repeat reporter |

---

### Common Mistakes

- Rushing urgent reports  
- Trusting tone over facts  
- Skipping location validation  
- Editing user text directly  

---

### Reviewing Media

When handling attached photos or videos:
- Confirm media matches claimed time/place  
- Reverse search suspicious images  
- Redact plates, faces, or identifiable signage  
- Never re-upload media outside ART  

<Callout type="important">
Media can contain GPS or device data (EXIF). Use it only for internal verification.
</Callout>

<Callout type="hint">
For safe handling, see  
[Documentation & Safety](/courses/documentation-safety) and  
[After-Action Data Hygiene](/courses/after-action-data-hygiene).
</Callout>

---

### What Counts as Valid?

| Field | Must Be… |
|--------|-----------|
| Location | Real, specific, verifiable |
| Time | Recent (ideally within 12h) |
| Observation | Firsthand or directly witnessed |
| Media | Optional but supportive |

---

### Verifier Ethics

Verifiers hold community trust. You must:
- Remain factual, not emotional  
- Uphold [Data Ethics Pledge](/courses/data-ethics-pledge)  
- Follow [Digital Security Basics](/courses/digital-security-basics) for metadata hygiene  
- Reject unverifiable reports, even if they “feel true”

<Callout type="warning">
Publishing must not endanger people.  
Balance urgency with consent and privacy.  
See [Community Consent](/courses/community-consent).
</Callout>

---

### Practice Scenarios

#### Scenario 1: Routine Verification
> “ICE at the Shell station on Broadway. 3 unmarked SUVs. Detained someone at 3:00 PM.”

✅ Confirm the location  
✅ Cross-check other reports  
✅ Match media to time and place  
✅ Verify & Publish

#### Scenario 2: Escalation
> “Detention van outside Walmart.”  
> Photo metadata shows it’s from another state.

🟢 Escalate to Senior Verifier  
📝 Flag potential disinfo  
📁 Log internally for pattern tracking  

---

<Callout type="warning">
Respect safety, consent, and privacy in every step.
</Callout>
