# 🤝 Conflict Repair & Community Mediation
<TrackBadge variant="community-care" />
<TrackBadge variant="movement-strategy" />

<Callout type="info">
Healthy conflict keeps pods strong. Learn to repair harm through accountability, not blame — and to design mediation that sustains relationships without replicating carceral systems.
</Callout>

---

## 📘 Overview
Tension, fatigue, and misunderstanding are inevitable in organizing work.  
Pods that treat conflict as a **normal part of community life** recover faster and grow stronger.

This course introduces **restorative repair frameworks** that center care, consent, and responsibility over punishment.

> Repair is not about who is right — it’s about how we stay in relationship while addressing harm.

---

## 🧭 Learn & Do

### 1. Understanding Harm vs. Conflict
| Type | Definition | Example |
|------|-------------|----------|
| Conflict | A disagreement that can be resolved through dialogue | Two members disagree on priorities |
| Harm | An action causing emotional, physical, or relational damage | Breach of trust, unsafe decisions, or exclusion |

**Goal:** Address conflict early so it doesn’t harden into harm.

---

### 2. Preparing for Repair Conversations
1. **Pause first.** Never start in anger or exhaustion.  
2. **Ask consent:** “Would you be open to a repair conversation when we both have space?”  
3. **Choose space intentionally:** Neutral, quiet, and safe.  
4. **Use facilitators** if emotional charge or power imbalance is high.

<Callout type="warning">
Never pressure anyone to participate in mediation. If safety is uncertain, begin with separate listening sessions or safety planning instead.
</Callout>

---

### 3. Core Principles of Mediation
- **Voluntary:** Everyone chooses to participate.  
- **Confidential:** Nothing shared leaves the circle without consent.  
- **Non-punitive:** The goal is understanding, not discipline.  
- **Accountability-focused:** Acknowledge impact, not intent.  
- **Containment:** One issue per session; no past stacking.  

<Callout type="info">
Pods can designate **Peer Mediators** trained in neutrality, consent protocols, and note-free listening.  
Pair this with [Peer Support & Check-In Circles](/courses/peer-support-checkins) to sustain ongoing repair culture.
</Callout>

---

### 4. Structure of a Repair Conversation
**Opening**
- Ground with breathing or silence  
- State intent: “We’re here to understand what happened and how to move forward.”  
- Agree on boundaries (no interruptions, time limits)

**Middle**
- Person A shares impact (no blame statements)  
- Person B reflects what they heard before responding  
- Identify needs or requests for repair

**Closing**
- Agree on clear next steps  
- Confirm consent to share outcomes with pod or mediator  
- Schedule follow-up if needed

> “Repair ends when both parties feel seen — not when everyone agrees.”

---

### 5. Accountability Without Expulsion
Not all harm means exclusion.  
Use a **tiered response** before isolation:

| Level | Approach | Example |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| 1 | Reflect & adjust | Naming a communication issue, re-setting norms |
| 2 | Repair & restore | Apology, changed behavior, role support |
| 3 | Pause & restructure | Temporary step back with plan for re-entry |

Only in ongoing danger or abuse should permanent separation be considered.

---

### 6. When to Seek Outside Help
- Abuse, stalking, or ongoing harassment  
- Mental health crisis impacting safety  
- Legal or housing threats requiring neutral mediation  

Options: restorative justice collectives, mutual-aid mediators, or trauma-informed counselors.
