# 🫂 Peer Support & Check-In Circles
<TrackBadge variant="community-care" />
<TrackBadge variant="field-safety" />

<Callout type="info">
Support begins before crisis. This course teaches how to hold structured, emotionally safe check-ins that reduce burnout, prevent isolation, and strengthen pod trust.
</Callout>

---

## 🧭 Overview
Mutual aid, dispatch, and field work put volunteers under continuous emotional load.  
Without space to release tension or speak honestly, teams lose cohesion and safety awareness.

**Check-In Circles** are lightweight, trauma-informed rituals that help pods sustain each other — not through therapy, but through presence, boundaries, and care.

> “We meet to connect, not to fix.”

---

## 🧩 Learn & Do

### 1. Circle Basics
A Check-In Circle is a **structured conversation** that invites reflection and grounding.

| Element | Purpose |
|----------|----------|
| Opening | Create calm and shared focus |
| Round Sharing | Voice feelings without cross-talk |
| Reflection | Acknowledge themes, not solutions |
| Closing | Reaffirm support and next steps |

**Ideal frequency:** once every 2–4 weeks, or after any major action.  
**Duration:** 30–60 minutes. Keep it predictable and consistent.

---

### 2. Opening the Circle
1. Begin with silence, stretching, or breathing.  
2. Read a grounding prompt, for example:  
   > “Take one breath for yourself, one for your team, and one for the community.”  
3. Name boundaries: “We share from experience, not judgment.”  
4. Remind everyone that **passing is always okay**.

<Callout type="hint">
Virtual circles can start with emojis, gestures, or short body check-ins (“How’s your energy right now?”). Keep it sensory, not analytical.
</Callout>

---

### 3. Active Listening Skills
- **Reflect:** “What I’m hearing is…”  
- **Validate:** “That makes sense; thank you for sharing.”  
- **Avoid fixing:** Offer presence, not solutions.  
- **Hold pauses:** Silence is part of processing.  
- **Check tone:** Curiosity over certainty.

<Callout type="info">
Listening is the action. The purpose is containment, not repair — that comes later in conflict or trauma courses.
</Callout>

---

### 4. Emotional Safety & Boundaries
- Use **“I” language** only.  
- No recording or note-taking unless agreed.  
- Do not share others’ stories outside the circle.  
- Stop immediately if someone dissociates or cries uncontrollably — breathe, pause, ask consent before continuing.  
- Close with a group grounding (deep breath, stretch, or affirmation).

<Callout type="warning">
Participation is always voluntary.  
Forcing disclosure or advice breaks psychological safety.
</Callout>

---

### 5. Running a Circle in the Field
For quick operations (e.g., before or after deployments):
1. Gather in a small circle or group chat.  
2. Ask three short prompts:
   - “How’s your body?”  
   - “Anything you need the team to know?”  
   - “One thing you’re grateful for today?”  
3. End with a unison word or gesture (“Together,” “Safe,” thumbs-up).  
4. Return to dispatch or field flow.

This version can take 5–7 minutes and still reset morale.

---

### 6. Handling Strong Emotions
If distress surfaces:
- Validate emotion (“It’s okay to feel that.”)  
- Offer grounding (“Can we take a breath together?”)  
- Defer deep processing to a later one-on-one or **Peer Support Lead**.  
- Never minimize, argue, or redirect too quickly.

> Emotional overwhelm ≠ failure. It’s data about capacity.

---

### 7. Roles for Sustained Circles
| Role | Function |
|------|-----------|
| Facilitator | Holds structure, time, and boundaries |
| Wellness/Support Lead | Monitors distress and follow-up needs |
| Timekeeper | Keeps rounds balanced |
| Participants | Speak or pass; uphold respect |

Rotate facilitators monthly to prevent emotional fatigue.
