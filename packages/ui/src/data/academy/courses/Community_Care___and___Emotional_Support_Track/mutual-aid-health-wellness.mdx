# 🧺 Mutual Aid Health & Wellness Logistics
<TrackBadge variant="community-care" />
<TrackBadge variant="logistics" />

<Callout type="info">
You can’t help the community if your team collapses. This course covers practical ways to sustain volunteer energy, prevent burnout, and integrate wellness into every operation.
</Callout>

---

## 🧭 Overview
Pod endurance depends on more than supplies — it requires hydration, nutrition, rest, and environmental safety.  
This course bridges **logistical planning and care infrastructure**, so field actions, CDCs, and dispatch operations remain sustainable across hours or days.

> A rested, hydrated, fed volunteer is twice as effective — and four times safer.

---

## 🧩 Learn & Do

### 1. Volunteer Rotation System
- Assign **shift leads** who monitor fatigue and hydration.  
- Use short rotations: 90–120 minutes max in direct sun or stress zones.  
- Pair experienced members with newer ones for observation.  
- Establish a **mandatory rest policy** before any redeployment.  

💡 *Tip:* Create a visual rest tracker (colored wristbands or board markers) to see who’s off-duty or active.

---

### 2. Hydration & Nutrition Planning
| Condition | Hydration Goal | Food Guidance |
|------------|----------------|----------------|
| Mild heat (70–80°F) | 1L/hr active time | Light carbs, fruit, salt snacks |
| High heat (85–95°F) | 1.5–2L/hr | Electrolyte mix every 2 hrs |
| Cold/windy | 0.5–1L/hr | High-fat warm foods, thermos liquids |

- Bring **ice-safe coolers** and insulated containers for long events.  
- Label all shared containers to avoid contamination.  
- Rotate water-bearing volunteers so one person isn’t overloaded.

<Callout type="warning">
Never share hydration packs or bottles between people. Cross-contamination spreads illness quickly during prolonged actions.
</Callout>

---

### 3. Rest Zones & Recovery Planning
- Set up a **quiet zone** with chairs, shade, and hand sanitizer.  
- Minimum ratio: **1 rest area per 10 active volunteers.**  
- Use privacy cloth or parked vans to block visibility.  
- Provide basic care supplies: electrolyte tabs, wet wipes, sunblock, spare socks.  

⏱ *Recommended schedule:* 10–15 min cool-down every 90 min, longer if temp > 90°F.

<Callout type="hint">
Pair rest zones with emotional support spaces — silence, soft music, or hydration prompts can stabilize field anxiety.
</Callout>

---

### 4. Environmental Health & Hygiene
- Use **handwashing stations** at all food and first aid points.  
- Keep a separate bin for medical waste (gauze, gloves).  
- Disinfect surfaces and shared tools at least once daily.  
- Assign a **Wellness Lead** responsible for shade, sanitation, and rest enforcement.

🧴 Quick formula for disinfectant: 1 part bleach to 9 parts water in labeled spray bottles. Never mix bleach with ammonia or vinegar.

---

### 5. Tracking Morale & Early Burnout
- Include “How are you feeling?” in every rotation check-in.  
- Watch for:
  - Withdrawal or irritability  
  - Reduced communication  
  - “Overhelping” without resting  
  - Missed meals or skipped hydration  

Use private notes like:
> “Volunteer B skipped rest twice today, appears dizzy — reassign for recovery.”

Keep notes short, factual, and delete after the operation.

<Callout type="warning">
Health and morale notes are **temporary operational data**, not permanent records. Delete or shred logs post-deployment.
</Callout>

---

### 6. Weather & Environmental Adaptation
- **Heat:** prioritize cooling towels, shade, rest cycles.  
- **Cold:** double-layer socks, avoid cotton, rotate warm-up breaks.  
- **Rain:** waterproof containers, non-slip mats, towel-dry electronics.  
- **Smoke or poor air:** distribute masks (N95+), reduce physical activity, establish indoor fallback.  

Create a **“climate kit”** customized per region — label and store with logistics supplies.

---

### 7. Wellness Command Role Integration
Pods may assign one **Wellness Lead** in larger actions who:
- Tracks rotation and hydration  
- Flags emerging health or morale concerns  
- Coordinates with Safety and Logistics Leads  
- Oversees rest zone setup and cleanup  

This person functions like a *field medic for morale and hydration.*
