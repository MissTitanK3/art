## Why It Matters

When community members are detained, pods must move fast — funding bail, reaching counsel, and offering care.  
Done poorly, these efforts can **breach confidentiality** or **retraumatize** people already in crisis.

Jail support is where trust, ethics, and logistics intersect.

---

## What You’ll Learn

1. **Bail Coordination** – Locating, vetting, and posting bail safely.  
2. **Legal Liaison Work** – Bridging communication between pods, families, and lawyers.  
3. **Confidentiality Protocols** – Secure record-keeping and minimal data use.  
4. **Post-Release Support** – Providing trauma-informed care.  
5. **Jail Navigation** – Understanding intake, phone access, meds, and visitation.  
6. **Burnout Prevention** – Role rotation, check-ins, and boundaries.

## Quick Start Steps

- Build an encrypted list of **bail funds and legal contacts**.  
- Train a **dedicated liaison** for each pod.  
- Keep minimal encrypted notes.  
- Prepare **post-release care kits** (water, blanket, snack, contact sheet).  
- Rotate volunteers: **6-hour max shifts**.

<Callout type="hint">
Follow [Documentation & Safety](/courses/documentation-safety) and [After-Action Data Hygiene](/courses/after-action-data-hygiene).  
Align with the [Data Ethics Pledge](/courses/data-ethics-pledge).
</Callout>

## Bail Coordination Basics

- Vet funds through trusted pods or registration records.  
- Never use personal accounts for bail transfers.  
- Use offline tracking sheets for custody updates.  
- Encrypt any financial data if stored digitally.  

<Callout type="info">
Avoid cross-pod PII centralization.  
Practice device hygiene from [Digital Security Basics](/courses/digital-security-basics).
</Callout>

---

## Legal Liaison Scripts

**Attorney Example:**  
> “We have X arrestees from Y action. Confirming custody status and representation. No case details will be discussed over this channel.”

**Family Example:**  
> “Your loved one is in custody and safe. We’ll share verified updates when confirmed. Legal contact info is available here.”

Confirm consent before involving interpreters or relaying sensitive updates.

## Trauma-Informed Post-Release Care

- Offer quiet, food, water, warmth.  
- Ask before touch or photos.  
- Listen, don’t interrogate.  
- Connect to care networks:  
  - [Care & Emotional Support](/courses/care-support)  
  - [Crisis Debriefing & Mutual Care](/courses/crisis-debrief-care)

<Callout type="hint">
Plan transport in advance. See [Runners & Rides](/courses/runners-and-rides).
</Callout>

## Jail Navigation Guide

| Task | Tips |
|------|------|
| Processing | Track average intake times per facility |
| Locating | Use jail portals or call intake desk directly |
| Visitation | Know policies; schedule in advance |
| Medication | Advocate for prescribed meds access |

<Callout type="warning">
Do not post updates or identifying info online.  
Always confirm consent before sharing any case status.
</Callout>

---

## When to Escalate

- Contact **National Lawyers Guild** if no local counsel.  
- Activate **missing person protocol** if detainee disappears.  
- Escalate immediately for **injuries or medical neglect**.

<Callout type="info">
Coordinate with [Court Support](/courses/court-support) and [Dispatch Coordination](/courses/dispatch-coordination).  
If active during a live response, log updates through [Responding to Dispatch Calls](/courses/responding-to-dispatch-calls).
</Callout>

---

## Risks & Red Lines

- Never share case facts over unencrypted channels.  
- Never misrepresent bail or outcomes.  
- Never promise confidentiality you can’t ensure.  
- Immediately secure devices and notes if targeted.  

<Callout type="important">
Retain only what’s essential. Encrypt everything else or destroy it securely.
</Callout>
