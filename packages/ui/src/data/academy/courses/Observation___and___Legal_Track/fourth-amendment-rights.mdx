# Understanding Your Fourth Amendment Rights
<TrackBadge variant="observation-legal" />
<TrackBadge variant="court-support" />

*Based on Myranda Kazos’ independent study guide — [Read and support](https://substack.com/home/post/p-168170845)*

The Fourth Amendment protects us from “unreasonable searches and seizures.” But what counts as unreasonable? Over the years, the courts have built a tangled web of exceptions, carve-outs, and bright-line rules — some empowering us, others eroding our rights.

This course breaks down major legal cases that define how and when cops can stop, search, or detain you — and when they can’t.

<Callout type="info">
This is general legal education, not legal advice. Jurisdictions vary and case law evolves. For practical street scripts and immediate actions, also review <a href="/courses/kyr-basics">Know Your Rights (KYR) Basics</a>.
</Callout>

## Course Sections

### 1. **Stops and Frisks**
- *Sibron v. NY*: No automatic frisk after a stop
- *Minnesota v. Dickerson*: “Plain feel” doctrine limits what can be seized
- *Arizona v. Johnson*: Frisks during traffic stops must be based on suspicion

### 2. **Traffic Stops & Occupant Orders**
- *Pennsylvania v. Mimms*: Cops can always order a driver out
- *Maryland v. Wilson*: Passengers too, if nervous or suspicious
- *Commonwealth v. Gonsalves*: Needs an objective basis
- *State v. Sprague*: Consent to search ≠ coercion

### 3. **Roadblocks & Checkpoints**
- *Michigan v. Sitz*: Sobriety checkpoints = OK
- *Indianapolis v. Edmond*: Drug checkpoints ≠ OK
- *Illinois v. Lidster*: Info-gathering stops allowed

### 4. **Border Searches**
- *U.S. v. Montoya de Hernandez*: Body cavity searches require “non-routine” suspicion
- *U.S. v. Ramsey*: Lower expectation of privacy at international borders
- *Flores-Montano*: Vehicle searches at the border don't need suspicion

<Callout type="warning">
Device privacy at the border is different. Minimize on‑device data when traveling. Review <a href="/courses/digital-security-basics">Digital Security Basics</a> and <a href="/courses/burner-phones-security-culture">Burner Phones & Security Culture</a> before cross‑border travel.
</Callout>

### 5. **Searches With Consent**
- *Schneckloth v. Bustamonte*: Consent must be voluntary — not necessarily informed
- *Florida v. Jimeno*: Scope is defined by what you say “yes” to
- *State v. Lewis*: You can withdraw consent at any time
- *Fernandez v. California*: A co-occupant can consent if you’re not present

### 6. **Searches Incident to Arrest**
- *Chimel v. California*: Cops can search the “grabbable area”
- *Riley v. California*: Phones need a warrant
- *Knowles v. Iowa*: Citation ≠ permission to search

### 7. **Vehicle & Container Searches**
- *Carroll v. US*: Cars can be searched without a warrant if mobile
- *California v. Acevedo*: Containers inside cars can be searched with probable cause
- *Wyoming v. Houghton*: Passengers' containers too

### 8. **Emergency & Exigent Circumstances**
- *Cupp v. Murphy*: Short-lived evidence = quick action allowed
- *Warden v. Hayden*: Hot pursuit allows home entry
- *Minnesota v. Olson*: Felony warrant ≠ emergency by default

<Callout type="important">
State‑level rulings (e.g., Vermont’s Sprague, Massachusetts’ Gonsalves) may grant more protection than federal minimums. Always check your state’s standards.
</Callout>

### 9. **Inventory Searches**
- *South Dakota v. Opperman*: Car impound = allowed to inventory
- *Florida v. Wells*: No standard policy = no random searching

### 10. **Custodial Settings & DNA**
- *Hudson v. Palmer*: Prison = less privacy
- *Maryland v. King*: DNA swabs OK after arraignment, not before
- *Florence v. Freeholders*: Strip searches for all arrests? Upheld.

### Quick Rights Script (Street‑Safe)

- “Am I free to go?” If yes, calmly leave. If no, “I do not consent to any searches.”  
- “I am choosing to remain silent. I want to speak to a lawyer.”  
- Don’t argue law on the street; document safely and get badge numbers if you can.  
- If arrested, route support via <a href="/courses/court-support">Court Support</a> and <a href="/courses/legal-followthrough-jail-support">Legal Follow‑through & Jail Support</a>.
