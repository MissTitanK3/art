# ⚖️ Understanding Your Fourth Amendment Rights
<TrackBadge variant="observation-legal" />
<TrackBadge variant="court-support" />

*Based on Myranda Kazos’ independent study guide — [Read and support](https://substack.com/home/post/p-168170845)*

The Fourth Amendment protects against **unreasonable searches and seizures** — but decades of rulings have redrawn its meaning.  
This course breaks down the most relevant cases for field organizers, legal observers, and dispatchers who need to know what’s truly legal.

## Key Doctrines & Precedents

### 1️⃣ Stops and Frisks
- *Sibron v. NY* — No automatic frisk after stop  
- *Minnesota v. Dickerson* — “Plain feel” doctrine limits seizure  
- *Arizona v. Johnson* — Frisk during stop must be justified  

---

### 2️⃣ Traffic Stops & Occupant Orders
- *Pennsylvania v. Mimms* — Officers can order drivers out  
- *Maryland v. Wilson* — Applies to passengers too  
- *Commonwealth v. Gonsalves* — Must have clear safety basis  
- *State v. Sprague* — Consent cannot be coerced  

---

### 3️⃣ Roadblocks & Checkpoints
- *Michigan v. Sitz* — Sobriety checkpoints allowed  
- *Indianapolis v. Edmond* — Drug stops not allowed  
- *Illinois v. Lidster* — Info-gathering stops permitted  

---

### 4️⃣ Border Searches
- *U.S. v. Montoya de Hernandez* — Body searches require suspicion  
- *U.S. v. Ramsey* — Lower privacy at international borders  
- *Flores-Montano* — Vehicle searches allowed without suspicion  

<Callout type="warning">
Device searches at borders are **not protected** the same way.  
Review [Digital Security Basics](/courses/digital-security-basics) and [Burner Phones & Security Culture](/courses/burner-phones-security-culture) before travel.
</Callout>

---

### 5️⃣ Consent Searches
- *Schneckloth v. Bustamonte* — Consent = voluntary, not informed  
- *Florida v. Jimeno* — Scope = what you agree to  
- *State v. Lewis* — Consent can be withdrawn  
- *Fernandez v. California* — Co-occupant can consent if you’re absent  

---

### 6️⃣ Incident to Arrest
- *Chimel v. California* — Search limited to “grabbable area”  
- *Riley v. California* — Phones require a warrant  
- *Knowles v. Iowa* — Citation ≠ search permission  

---

### 7️⃣ Vehicle & Container Searches
- *Carroll v. U.S.* — Moving cars can be searched without warrant  
- *California v. Acevedo* — Containers may be searched with cause  
- *Wyoming v. Houghton* — Passenger belongings included  

---

### 8️⃣ Emergency & Exigent Circumstances
- *Cupp v. Murphy* — Short-lived evidence allows quick action  
- *Warden v. Hayden* — “Hot pursuit” permits entry  
- *Minnesota v. Olson* — Felony warrant ≠ emergency  

<Callout type="important">
State constitutions may grant **stronger** protections than federal standards.  
Check your local precedent before assuming federal law applies.
</Callout>

---

### 9️⃣ Inventory Searches
- *South Dakota v. Opperman* — Car impound = OK to inventory  
- *Florida v. Wells* — No policy = no random searching  

---

### 🔟 Custody & DNA
- *Hudson v. Palmer* — Prisons = reduced privacy  
- *Maryland v. King* — DNA swabs after arraignment allowed  
- *Florence v. Freeholders* — Strip searches upheld for all arrests

## Street-Safe Rights Script

> “Am I free to go?”  
> “I do not consent to any searches.”  
> “I choose to remain silent and speak to a lawyer.”  

If detained:
- Stay calm and document.  
- Get badge numbers and time.  
- Route support through [Court Support](/courses/court-support) and [Legal Follow-through & Jail Support](/courses/legal-followthrough-jail-support).  

<Callout type="hint">
If this happens during an ART dispatch, alert via [Responding to Dispatch Calls](/courses/responding-to-dispatch-calls) and [Dispatch Coordination](/courses/dispatch-coordination).
</Callout>
