# 🧾 Legal Risk & Tech Use for Activists
<TrackBadge variant="field-safety" />
<TrackBadge variant="movement-strategy" />
<TrackBadge variant="court-support" />

<Callout type="warning">
📌 Using apps, phones, or online tools while organizing can **expose you, your crew, and your contacts** to surveillance or prosecution. This course helps you understand what’s legal, what’s risky, and what questions to ask before taking tech into the field.
</Callout>

---

## 👀 Why This Matters

Activists in the U.S. — especially BIPOC, immigrant, and frontline organizers — have been:

- 📱 Tracked through phones and apps  
- 🕵️‍♀️ Infiltrated by undercover agents  
- 💻 Prosecuted using digital logs or group chats  
- 📁 Targeted with data seized from cloud servers  

Knowing the legal landscape isn't about fear — it's about building power **with eyes open**.

- What’s protected speech vs criminalized organizing

- How to assess and reduce digital risk for yourself and others

## ⚖️ What Is Protected Speech?

🧷 Most protest-related speech is **protected under the First Amendment**, but:

| Protected                                | Risky or Criminalized                       |
|------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| Criticizing the government               | Explicit threats or incitement to violence  |
| Organizing or attending protests         | Coordinated property destruction (RICO)     |
| Sharing public resources or flyers       | Coordinated plans that cross into “conspiracy” |
| Encrypted messaging about ideas          | Group chats that name targets + actions     |

> ⚠️ Intent, association, and *how* things are said often determine risk — not just what is said.

<Callout type="hint">
🧠 **Never assume your chat logs, Signal history, or group DMs are private** — even with encryption.
</Callout>

<Callout type="important">
This course is for public education and operational awareness. It is not legal advice. For case-specific guidance, consult an attorney or your region’s legal lead (see resources below).
</Callout>

## 🧾 Criminal Conspiracy & Digital Organizing

“**Conspiracy**” charges are often used to criminalize organizers.

| Charge | What It Means |
|--------|----------------|
| Conspiracy | You and others planned or agreed to commit a crime, even if it didn’t happen. |
| Aiding/abetting | You helped or encouraged a crime — even passively. |

Digital risks include:
- Being in a Signal group where illegal activity is discussed
- Reposting someone else's plan with no disclaimers
- Linking dispatch tools to an event that becomes criminalized

## ☁️ What Do Apps & Backends Actually Log?

You may trust ART. But what about the services it relies on?

| Tech Provider        | Logged by default                               | Risk Level |
|----------------------|--------------------------------------------------|------------|
| Supabase             | IP addresses, email/SMS, login times, metadata (typical) | 🔥 High    |
| Stripe (if used by your region) | Payment methods, billing zip, IP logs            | ⚠️ Medium |
| Mobile OTA/update services (if used) | Device info, usage stats (unless stripped)       | ⚠️ Medium |
| Firebase (if used)   | Heavy logging, tied to Google accounts           | 🔥 High    |

Even with encryption, **metadata can be enough** to:
- Map social networks  
- Place people at locations  
- Build timelines for prosecution  

<Callout type="info">
Capability notes for ART and region apps:
- Report photos are supported; videos should be shared via secure links rather than direct upload.
- Logs exist on the server side for safety and abuse response; there is no in‑app CSV export or exhaustive audit viewer.
- Maps do not support drawing geofences; use labels and proximity notes instead.
- Notifications are region‑configured; avoid implying generic “location‑based alerts.”
- Use access roles and trust‑based reviews to limit who can see what.
</Callout>

## 🧰 What You Can Do

### 🔐 At the Design Level
- Remove all unnecessary data collection  
- Avoid real names, phone numbers, or emails  
- Disable backups, logging, analytics  
- Use offline modes where possible

### 👥 At the Culture Level
- Don’t treat tech as required  
- Let people opt-in slowly  
- Clearly communicate what is or isn’t logged  
- Use roles to separate who touches tech vs. who is visible

### 🧾 At the Legal Level
- Publish a “what we log, what we don’t” document  
- Work with movement lawyers (NLG, EFF, etc.)  
- Prepare a **warrant canary** and legal policy statement  

<Callout type="info">
🎯 Use the **least dangerous tool** that still gets the job done.
</Callout>

## 🛡️ When Phones Become Evidence

If you're arrested or raided, **your phone is not your friend**:

| Scenario | What Happens |
|----------|---------------|
| Phone unlocked | Everything is accessible — even encrypted apps (if open) |
| Cloud backups enabled | Messages, contacts, and images can be pulled remotely |
| Group chats named or described poorly | Prosecutors can twist screenshots into “evidence” |

📌 *Best Practice:* Leave phones behind for sensitive ops. Use runners, radio, or in-person confirmations.

---

## 🕵️ Who Should Touch What?

### ⚠️ Risk Protocol Suggestion

| Role              | Allowed Tech Use       | Restrictions |
|-------------------|------------------------|--------------|
| OTG Field Member  | Burner phones only     | No access to dispatch backend or Signal groups |
| Dispatcher        | Encrypted devices only | Never goes OTG on same day |
| Media Poster      | Uses templated graphics | Avoids naming or timestamping ops unless approved |
| Coordinator       | Approves messaging only | Role separation with review required |

---

## 🧱 Build Layers of Separation

Use the **"Need to Know" model**:

- Not everyone needs access to everything  
- Dispatch can operate separately from pod leads  
- Field units can operate from flyers, not apps  
- Alerts can be visual (images, banners) with no interactive data

<Callout type="warning">
🚧 Treat every new system as a **potential future compromise**. Design with that in mind.
</Callout>

---

## ✅ Legal Mitigations You Can Demand

- **Conduct legal review** with a trusted movement lawyer  
- Add **terms of use** to every tool, clarifying what is public vs private  
- Provide **“safe usage guidelines”** in every field training  
- Create a **public “Know the Risks” flyer** for new users  
- Consider self-hosting or fully offline options for critical infrastructure

## Practice Scenario

🔍 _Someone in a Signal group suggests escalating an upcoming protest to a property destruction campaign, using ART Dispatch to gather._

**Do you:**
A. Say nothing  
B. Agree with them  
C. Leave the chat  
D. Redirect and report to a trusted ops lead  

✅ Answer: **D**, with C as backup.  
Even encrypted apps are **not safe spaces for plotting illegal activity**. Silence ≠ safety.
