# 🧾 Legal Risk & Tech Use for Activists
<TrackBadge variant="field-safety" />
<TrackBadge variant="movement-strategy" />
<TrackBadge variant="court-support" />

<Callout type="warning">
📌 Using apps, phones, or online tools while organizing can expose you, your crew, and your contacts to surveillance or prosecution.  
This course helps you understand what’s legal, what’s risky, and how to make informed, collective decisions before taking tech into the field.
</Callout>

---

## Overview

Activists in the U.S. — especially BIPOC, immigrant, and frontline organizers — have been:

- 📱 Tracked through phones and apps  
- 🕵️‍♀️ Infiltrated by undercover agents  
- 💻 Prosecuted using digital logs or chat histories  
- 📁 Targeted with seized cloud data  

The goal is not fear, but awareness. **Legal risk literacy is collective defense.**

---

## Learn & Do

### Protected vs. Criminalized Speech

🧷 Most protest-related speech is protected under the First Amendment — but context changes risk.

| Protected | Risky / Criminalized |
|------------|---------------------|
| Criticizing government actions | Explicit threats or calls for violence |
| Organizing or attending protests | Coordinated property damage (RICO exposure) |
| Sharing public resources or flyers | Planning or “conspiring” illegal actions |
| Encrypted chats about ideas | Group chats naming targets or tactics |

> ⚠️ Intent, association, and **context** often determine legal exposure — not just content.

<Callout type="hint">
Never assume your chat logs, Signal messages, or DMs are fully private — even with encryption.
</Callout>

<Callout type="important">
This course provides operational awareness, not legal advice.  
For case-specific guidance, consult a lawyer or regional legal coordinator.
</Callout>

---

### Conspiracy and Digital Organizing

Authorities often use **conspiracy** or **aiding/abetting** charges to criminalize organizers.

| Term | Meaning |
|------|----------|
| **Conspiracy** | Agreement or planning to commit a crime, even if it never happens |
| **Aiding / Abetting** | Supporting or encouraging a crime, even indirectly |

Common risk points:
- Being in a Signal or Discord group where crimes are discussed  
- Reposting someone else’s call-to-action without disclaimers  
- Linking event or dispatch tools to actions later criminalized  

---

### What Apps and Backends Log

Even privacy-oriented systems have exposure points.

| Service | Logs by Default | Risk Level |
|----------|----------------|-------------|
| **Supabase** | IPs, device metadata, login times | 🔥 High |
| **Stripe** | Payment method, billing zip, IP | ⚠️ Medium |
| **OTA/Update services** | Device usage stats | ⚠️ Medium |
| **Firebase / Google APIs** | Extensive tracking, analytics | 🔥 High |

**Metadata alone** can reveal networks, movements, and timelines.

<Callout type="info">
ART-specific notes:
- Report photos are allowed; videos only via secure links.  
- Logs exist for abuse prevention, not bulk export.  
- Maps avoid tracking; use manual notes for direction or proximity.  
- Role-based access and trust lists protect sensitive info.  
</Callout>

---

### Risk Reduction at Three Levels

#### 🔐 Design Level
- Collect only what’s necessary  
- Remove analytics and cloud backups  
- Prefer offline-first tools  
- Avoid personal identifiers (names, phone, email)

#### 👥 Culture Level
- Never require apps to participate  
- Communicate transparently about what’s logged  
- Separate field vs. digital roles  
- Normalize informed consent for digital visibility

#### ⚖️ Legal Level
- Publish a “What We Log” statement  
- Collaborate with movement lawyers (EFF, NLG)  
- Maintain a **warrant canary**  
- Include **safe-use disclaimers** in trainings

<Callout type="info">
🎯 Use the least dangerous tool that still achieves your mission.
</Callout>

### When Phones Become Evidence

If arrested or raided:

| Scenario | Impact |
|-----------|---------|
| Phone unlocked | All data accessible, even “encrypted” apps |
| Cloud backups active | Remote recovery of chats, media, and contacts |
| Poorly named groups | Screenshots can be reframed as “intent” evidence |

> Best practice: Leave phones behind for sensitive operations. Use runners, radio, or in-person relay.

---

### Role Separation for Risk Control

| Role | Device Type | Restrictions |
|------|--------------|--------------|
| Field Team | Burner only | No backend or Signal group access |
| Dispatcher | Encrypted personal device | Never onsite same day |
| Media | Templated, pre-approved graphics | No timestamps or locations |
| Coordinator | Reviews and approves ops | Cannot dispatch self |

---

### Building Layers of Separation

Adopt a **need-to-know** model:

- Each person only accesses what they need  
- Dispatch and pods operate semi-independently  
- Field units can act from printed briefs  
- Public alerts can be images, not live maps  

<Callout type="warning">
Design every system as if it may one day be compromised.
</Callout>

---

### Legal Safeguards You Can Demand

- Require **movement lawyer review** for major tools  
- Add **clear Terms of Use** defining visibility boundaries  
- Provide **user risk disclosures** in every onboarding  
- Offer **public flyers** on safe app use  
- Prefer **self-hosted or offline systems** when possible  

---

### Practice Scenario

> A Signal group discusses property destruction and mentions ART Dispatch as a coordination tool.

✅ Correct response: **Redirect and report** to a trusted lead.  
Leaving the chat is secondary; ignoring it is not protection.  
Encrypted apps ≠ immunity.

---

### Checklist for Safer Ops

- [ ] Do I know what our tools log?  
- [ ] Are illegal acts discussed anywhere I’m present?  
- [ ] Is my device encrypted and backed up safely?  
- [ ] Is there a seizure or arrest plan?  
- [ ] Have we reviewed ART’s privacy practices as a team?

---

### Cultural Bottom Line

Digital risk is shared.  
Safety depends on collective discipline, not individual caution.  
We protect each other **even when someone slips**.

> “We don’t need perfection — we need redundancy.”
