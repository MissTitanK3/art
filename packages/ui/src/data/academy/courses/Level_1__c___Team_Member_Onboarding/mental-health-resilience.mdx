## Overview

Field and dispatch work create emotional strain.  
Stress, exhaustion, or exposure to crisis can blur judgment and reduce safety.  
Resilience is not endurance — it’s recovery through awareness, boundaries, and peer care.  

---

## Learn & Do

### 1. Core Goals
By the end of this course, you will:
- Recognize physical and emotional stress signals  
- Apply grounding techniques for self or others  
- Conduct short, consent-based check-ins  
- Know when and how to escalate for support  

---

### 2. Grounding Techniques

- **5-4-3-2-1 Method:**  
  Identify 5 things you see, 4 you feel, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste.  
- **Box Breathing:**  
  Inhale 4, hold 4, exhale 4, hold 4. Repeat for 3–5 cycles.  
- **Bilateral Tapping:**  
  Tap thighs or alternate steps to re-center attention.  
- **Grounding Script (for others):**  
  *“Can you name five things you see right now? Let’s slow your breathing together.”*

---

### 3. Recognizing Stress Signals

| Type | Examples |
|------|-----------|
| **Physical** | Tremors, dizziness, shaking, nausea |
| **Cognitive** | Confusion, time distortion, memory gaps |
| **Emotional** | Numbness, panic, tears, irritability |
| **Behavioral** | Withdrawal, overwork, silence, freezing |

> Recognizing stress early prevents escalation and reduces risk in the field.

---

### 4. Peer Support Basics

**Three-Minute Check-In**
1. “How’s your body right now?”  
2. “What do you need in this moment?”  
3. “Can I help you get it?”  

**Respect autonomy:**  
Ask before touch, movement, or guidance.  
Keep tone steady and your body language non-urgent.

<Callout type="warning">
You’re stabilizing, not diagnosing.  
Don’t probe trauma or ask for detailed retellings.
</Callout>

### 6. Daily Stability Practices

- Use 2–3 grounding techniques during calm moments.  
- Pair up for **emotional buddy check-ins** at events.  
- Step back when concentration drops — fatigue causes errors.  
- Save **local crisis and warmline contacts** offline.  
- Build quiet routines post-event (walk, tea, hydration, journaling).

---

### 7. Red Lines and Risks

🚫 Never pressure someone to talk about trauma.  
🚫 Never block someone from leaving if they need space.  
🚫 Don’t offer therapy or diagnosis — focus on present safety.  
✅ Respect alternative grounding — prayer, silence, movement, or rest.

<Callout type="important">
Consent & data:  
Always ask before recording or sharing care details.  
Use encrypted tools for documentation.  
Follow [Documentation Safety](/courses/documentation-safety) and [Data Ethics Pledge](/courses/data-ethics-pledge).
</Callout>

---

### 8. Scenario Drill

> A teammate freezes mid-action:  
1. Move them to a quieter space if safe.  
2. Guide slow breathing (Box or 5-4-3-2-1).  
3. Ask, “What do you need right now?”  
4. Escalate to dispatcher or care lead if unresponsive.  

---

### 9. Checklist

- [ ] Can perform 2+ grounding techniques  
- [ ] Can recognize 3+ stress signals  
- [ ] Knows local crisis contacts  
- [ ] Practices peer check-ins regularly  
- [ ] Can tell the difference between stress and trauma  
- [ ] Has shared personal warning signs with a buddy
