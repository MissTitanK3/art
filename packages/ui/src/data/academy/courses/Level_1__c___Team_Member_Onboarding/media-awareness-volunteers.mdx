## Overview

Livestreams and social media are double-edged: they amplify visibility and risk.  
Improper handling can expose undocumented people, compromise safety, or misrepresent events.  
Every volunteer should be able to identify media threats, redirect calmly, and maintain composure under scrutiny.

---

## Learn & Do

### 1. Spotting Media Risks

| Type | Signs | Risk Level |
|------|-------|------------|
| **Livestreamers** | Phone held upright, continuous filming, chatting to camera | ⚠️ Medium |
| **Press / Journalists** | Press badge or mic, asks for quotes or statements | ⚠️ Medium |
| **Doxxers / Impersonators** | No ID, asks about “leaders” or “plans” | 🔥 High |
| **Police posing as press** | Avoids showing credentials, probing for info | 🔥 High |

---

### 2. Core Principles

- Assume every camera is **live**.  
- Protect **faces, names, and identifying details**.  
- Use pre-approved **pod statements** — no freelancing.  
- Direct press or streamers to **designated spokespeople**.  
- Document hostile or suspicious recorders for post-event review.

---

### 3. De-Escalation Scripts

| Situation | What to Say |
|------------|-------------|
| Redirecting cameras | “We’d appreciate you avoiding faces — people’s safety is at risk.” |
| Aggressive press | “I’m not a spokesperson, but I can connect you with someone who can talk.” |
| Protecting vulnerable people | “Let’s move you somewhere less exposed so you’re not on camera.” |

> Keep tone calm, neutral, and brief. Arguing creates clips — not safety.

---

### 4. Working with Press Safely

- Verify credentials (ask for ID, cross-check online).  
- Confirm who is authorized to speak for your pod.  
- Never reveal times, names, or private locations.  
- Assume **everything said is on record** unless clearly agreed otherwise.  
- Share only mission-level info (what the action supports, not who planned it).

<Callout type="info">
If legitimate press coverage is beneficial, coordinate through dispatch or your regional media lead — never spontaneously.
</Callout>

---

### 5. Digital Security for Media Content

- Disable **location tagging** before filming or posting.  
- Blur faces with **ObscuraCam** or **Signal’s blur tool**.  
- Transfer footage via **Signal** or **OnionShare**, not email or cloud drives.  
- Delete raw copies once transferred securely.  

<Callout type="hint">
ART capability notes:
- Reports can include photos; videos must be linked securely.  
- Logs exist only for moderation, not export.  
- Maps show proximity, not precise geofences.  
- Role-based permissions restrict media visibility.
</Callout>

---

### 6. Threat Assessment

Watch for:
- Individuals fixating on faces, badges, or license plates  
- Questions about who’s “in charge”  
- “Friendly” recorders asking for group details  
- Sudden uploads of live content from close range  

If unsure — **redirect, report, document.**

### 8. Rapid Response

1. Assume **everything** is being recorded.  
2. Politely **redirect** cameras away from vulnerable people.  
3. If sensitive activity is captured, **notify your dispatcher** immediately.  
4. Use **approved statements** only.  

<Callout type="warning">
Only trained marshals or media leads should physically block cameras.  
Volunteers focus on redirection, not confrontation.
</Callout>

---

### 9. Documentation Hygiene

- Retain only what’s necessary, for as short as needed.  
- Delete temporary media and chat logs after review.  
- Never post identifiable content without consent.  
- Record incidents through secure dispatch notes, not public threads.

---

### 10. Red Lines

🚫 Never confirm who participated.  
🚫 Never share meeting times or internal details.  
🚫 Never post footage of minors, undocumented people, or ongoing operations.  

If exposure occurs:
1. Notify safety or security leads immediately.  
2. Document what was captured.  
3. Activate mitigation: contact legal or media team to request takedown.
