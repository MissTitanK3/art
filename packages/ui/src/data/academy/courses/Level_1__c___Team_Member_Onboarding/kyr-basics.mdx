## Why This Matters

Knowing your rights reduces harm. In over 70% of wrongful detentions, people didn’t clearly assert legal protections they had. Learn them. Practice them. Teach others.

> 📍 These are based on U.S. law. Check local laws for any state-specific differences.  
> 📚 *Source: ACLU Immigrants’ Rights Project – Detention and Due Process Reports, 2018–2020*

## Your Core Rights

These are the most important rights to remember during an interaction with ICE or law enforcement:

- 🛑 **You have the right to remain silent.**
- 🚪 **You do not have to open the door unless they show a judicial warrant signed by a judge.**
- ❌ **You have the right to refuse consent to a search of yourself, your phone, your bag, or your vehicle.**
- 📷 **You have the right to document public encounters, if it’s safe to do so.**
- 🗣️ **You have the right to ask: “Am I being detained, or am I free to go?”**
- 🧾 **You have the right to an attorney — and to not sign anything without one.**

<Callout type="warning">
Asserting your rights may not stop harm — but it builds a legal record and protects others.
</Callout>

## What Counts as a Warrant?

### ✅ Judicial Warrant
- Signed by a judge
- Lists specific name, address, or location
- Required for ICE to enter a home **or any private area**
- Applies to enclosed spaces not accessible to the public

### ❌ Administrative Warrant
- Signed by ICE or DHS staff
- NOT valid for forced entry or arrest
- You are not required to comply

> 📸 Want to see the difference?  
→ Search: "I-200 vs judicial warrant image"

<Callout type="rabbit-hole">
🔍 Public vs. Private Spaces  
Know the boundary: sidewalks = public; backyards, private driveways, enclosed porches = private

</Callout>

## Home, Street, Vehicle, Transit

| Location      | Can they search without a warrant?       | What to say                                 |
|---------------|-------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| **Home**      | No — must have a *judicial warrant*       | “I do not consent to entry.”                |
| **Street**    | No — unless they have probable cause      | “Am I being detained, or am I free to go?”  |
| **Vehicle**   | No — unless they have probable cause      | “I do not consent to a search.”             |
| **Transit**   | No — unless a warrant or border power     | “What law allows you to stop me here?”      |

> ⚠️ Some states require ID in “Stop-and-ID” zones.  
→ [Check this list](https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/stop-and-identify-laws.html) to see if your state has one.

## How to Assert Your Rights

- Use clear, respectful language:  
  > “I’m choosing to remain silent.”  
  > “I do not consent to a search.”  
  > “I want to speak with a lawyer.”

- Never lie or present false documents.
- Stay calm. Don’t run, argue, or resist physically.
- Document the encounter if safe — or ask someone nearby to.

<Callout type="important">
Never sign anything ICE or police give you without a lawyer present.
</Callout>

## Protecting Each Other

- 🪪 Carry a **Know Your Rights card** (also called Red Cards) in English and Spanish.  
  → Can't buy one? Print a free version from [ILRC’s KYR Cards](https://www.ilrc.org/red-cards) or make your own on cardstock.
- 📱 Set up an emergency contact and memorize the number.
- 📝 Log what happened — date, time, location, officers, quotes.
- 🤐 **Respect confidentiality**. Do not post or share stories, locations, or photos without informed consent — even after the danger feels over.

<Callout type="warning">
Confidentiality saves lives. Assume everything is sensitive unless you’re told otherwise.
</Callout>

### Sample Script if Approached at Home

> “I do not consent to entry. Please slide any warrant under the door. I choose to remain silent and want a lawyer.”

## Emergency Contact Drill

1. **Say**: “I am remaining silent and asking for a lawyer.”  
2. **Call**: [Write down or memorize a trusted number]  
3. **Repeat**: “I do not consent to a search.”

> If needed, write contact info on your arm with sharpie before actions.

## Summary

Knowing your rights is **only the first step** — practicing them in community builds power. Review these regularly. Teach your group. Carry tools. Stay calm.

<Callout type="success">
You don’t need to be a lawyer to protect your rights — just informed and steady.
</Callout>
