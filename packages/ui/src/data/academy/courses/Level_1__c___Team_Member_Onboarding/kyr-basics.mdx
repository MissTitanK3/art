## Overview

Knowing your rights reduces harm.  
In over 70% of wrongful detentions, people didn’t assert protections they already had.  
This course shows how to **recognize**, **assert**, and **practice** those rights safely and collectively.

> 📍 These are based on U.S. law. Check local laws for state-specific differences.  
> 📚 *Source: ACLU Immigrants’ Rights Project – Detention and Due Process Reports, 2018–2020*

## Learn & Do

### Core Rights at a Glance

- 🛑 **Right to remain silent**  
- 🚪 **Right not to open the door without a judicial warrant**  
- ❌ **Right to refuse a search** of yourself, phone, bag, or vehicle  
- 📷 **Right to document public encounters (if safe)**  
- 🗣️ **Right to ask:** “Am I being detained, or am I free to go?”  
- 🧾 **Right to an attorney — and to not sign anything without one**

<Callout type="warning">
Asserting rights may not stop harm immediately, but it builds a legal record and protects others.
</Callout>

### What Counts as a Warrant?

| Type | Signed By | Valid For Entry? | Notes |
|------|------------|------------------|-------|
| ✅ **Judicial Warrant** | A judge | Yes | Must list names and address; required for home entry |
| ❌ **Administrative Warrant** | ICE or DHS | No | Cannot be used for forced entry or arrest |

> 📸 Want to see the difference?  
→ Search: “I-200 vs judicial warrant image”

<Callout type="rabbit-hole">
🔍 Public vs. Private Spaces  
Sidewalks are public. Enclosed yards, driveways, or porches are private.  
→ Search: “NILC Know Your Rights Private Areas Guide”
</Callout>

### Encounters by Location

| Location | Can They Search Without a Warrant? | What to Say |
|-----------|------------------------------------|--------------|
| **Home** | No – requires judicial warrant | “I do not consent to entry.” |
| **Street** | No – unless probable cause | “Am I being detained, or am I free to go?” |
| **Vehicle** | No – unless probable cause | “I do not consent to a search.” |
| **Transit** | No – unless border or warrant authority | “What law allows you to stop me here?” |

> ⚠️ Some states have Stop-and-ID laws.  
→ [Check your state here](https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/stop-and-identify-laws.html)

### How to Assert Your Rights

- Speak clearly and calmly:  
  > “I’m choosing to remain silent.”  
  > “I do not consent to a search.”  
  > “I want to speak with a lawyer.”  
- Never lie or present false documents.  
- Don’t argue, run, or resist physically.  
- Document if safe, or ask someone else to.  

<Callout type="important">
Never sign anything from ICE or police without a lawyer present.
</Callout>

### Protecting Each Other

- 🪪 Carry a **Know Your Rights card (Red Card)** in English and Spanish.  
  → Download from [ILRC’s KYR Cards](https://www.ilrc.org/red-cards) or print your own.  
- 📱 Set and memorize an emergency contact.  
- 📝 Record key details: time, place, officers, quotes.  
- 🤐 Respect confidentiality — don’t share or post without consent.

<Callout type="warning">
Confidentiality saves lives.  
Assume all information is sensitive unless explicitly cleared.
</Callout>

### Home Encounter Script

> “I do not consent to entry. Please slide any warrant under the door.  
> I choose to remain silent and want a lawyer.”

---

### Emergency Contact Drill

1. Say: “I am remaining silent and asking for a lawyer.”  
2. Call: [Write or memorize a trusted number.]  
3. Repeat: “I do not consent to a search.”

> Before actions, write emergency contacts on your arm with a marker.
