## Overview

Burner phones protect you by **limiting exposure** if your primary device is seized, hacked, or tracked.  
They enable **compartmentalization and anonymity**, supporting OPSEC through isolation of data, networks, and identity.  
This only works inside a **culture of security** — a set of shared norms and habits that make breaches less likely and less harmful.

---

## Learn & Do

### What Is a Burner Phone?

A **burner** is a prepaid, short-term device bought without identification and used for a limited operation.

**Typical uses:**
- High-risk protests or surveillance-heavy events  
- Cross-border travel  
- Anonymous organizing or secure temporary channels  

<Callout type="hint">
Use cash for purchase. Never reuse burners for separate operations or campaigns.
</Callout>

---

### Burner Lifecycle

1. **Acquire Anonymously**
   - Pay with cash only  
   - Avoid loyalty cards, linked stores, or identifiable locations  

2. **Setup Securely**
   - Remove biometrics (fingerprint, face unlock)  
   - Add a strong PIN or passphrase  
   - Enable full-device encryption  
   - Disable location, ad ID, and background sync  
   - Turn off Wi-Fi/Bluetooth scanning and “Nearby devices”  

3. **Install Minimum Apps**
   - Use only essentials (e.g., Signal)  
   - No personal logins, cloud sync, or account linking  

4. **Use Once**
   - Employ it for one event or phase only  
   - Then wipe or physically destroy it  
   - Never reactivate burners with the same SIM or identity  

---

### Integrating Burners into Security Culture

- Keep burners **off or in airplane mode** when not needed  
- Avoid **co-travel correlation** — don’t power burners near your personal device  
- **Scrub metadata** from all photos/files (see [Documentation Safety](/courses/documentation-safety))  
- Use **burner SIMs** purchased with cash and avoid ID-registered activations  
- Pair with **offline comms** from [App-Free Tactics](/courses/app-free-tactics)  
- Combine with **mesh or radio backups** from [Mesh Networks Intro](/courses/mesh-networks-intro) and [Comms Collapse](/courses/comms-collapse)  
- Always **debrief** and update threat models post-use ([After-Action Data Hygiene](/courses/after-action-data-hygiene))

---

### Threat Modeling & Limits

- Burners can **attract suspicion** if discovered during searches  
- Avoid pattern overlap (same locations, same purchase points)  
- Device IDs (IMEI/MEID) and SIM IDs (IMSI/ICCID) can be linked to your identity  

<Callout type="warning">
Never activate a SIM with a credit card, ID, or known address.  
Avoid carrying a burner and your personal phone together; tower triangulation can expose both.
</Callout>

---

### Ethical & Community Norms

Security culture is collective, not personal.  
A disciplined community prevents harm better than any single encrypted device.

- Never use burners to store or share private info about others  
- Train your pod on proper setup and disposal routines  
- Keep security culture fresh with yearly zine or guide reviews  

<Callout type="hint">
Know your rights around searches and passwords. See [Know Your Rights (KYR) Basics](/courses/kyr-basics).
</Callout>

---

### Burner Checklist

- [ ] Purchased anonymously, cash only  
- [ ] SIM not ID-linked  
- [ ] Biometrics removed, passcode enabled  
- [ ] Device encryption on  
- [ ] Location/Wi-Fi/Bluetooth disabled  
- [ ] Only secure apps installed  
- [ ] Airplane mode until deployment  
- [ ] Metadata scrubbed before sharing  
- [ ] No co-travel with personal device  
- [ ] No cloud backup or ad ID  
- [ ] Used once, then wiped or destroyed  
- [ ] Logged for post-event review
