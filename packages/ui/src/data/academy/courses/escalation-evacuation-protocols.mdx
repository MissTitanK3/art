# 🛑 Escalation & Evacuation Protocols
<TrackBadge variant="direct-action" />
<TrackBadge variant="field-safety" />

<Callout type="important">
**Golden Rule:** Evacuate *before* you think you need to. Late exits account for most field injuries.
</Callout>

When tensions spike — police formations, counter-protesters, or sudden violence —  
teams without a plan **freeze, scatter, or get injured**.  
This course builds a **clear escalation and exit protocol** so pods can move safely and keep everyone accounted for.

<Callout type="hint">
Anchor your playbooks before you roll: <a href="/academy/dealing-with-chaos">Dealing With Chaos</a>, <a href="/academy/comms-collapse">Comms Collapse</a>, and <a href="/academy/app-free-tactics">App‑Free Tactics</a>. For transport and handoffs, align with <a href="/academy/runners-and-rides">Runners & Rides</a>. Coordinate with dispatch via <a href="/academy/dispatch-coordination">Dispatch Coordination</a> and <a href="/academy/responding-to-dispatch-calls">Responding to Dispatch Calls</a>.
</Callout>

## Enhanced Escalation Framework

### 1. Threat Indicators (5-Minute Warning Signs)

    | Police or Crowd Behavior              | Pod Response Tier                  |
    |----------------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
    | Police lines shifting (line → wedge)  | **Yellow Alert**: Test exits        |
    | Gas masks or uniform swaps observed   | **Orange Alert**: Move vulnerable   |
    | Kettling or flank attempts begin      | **Red Alert**: Execute escape plan  |

---

### 2. Evacuation Triggers

Non-negotiable triggers:

    - Chemical weapons deployed (tear gas, pepper spray).  
    - Live ammunition or rubber bullets fired.  
    - Kettling or mass encirclement tactics.

Discretionary triggers:

    - Loss of 50% communications (Signal, radio).  
    - Medical emergency with no clear route to aid.

<Callout type="info">
During exits, prioritize safety over filming. If documentation is necessary, minimize faces and follow <a href="/academy/documentation-safety">Documentation & Safety</a>. Clean up sensitive media per <a href="/academy/after-action-data-hygiene">After‑Action Data Hygiene</a>.
</Callout>

---

### 3. Exit Route Mapping

Always map **three levels of escape** before any event:

    PRIMARY: [Street] → [Alley] → [Safehouse]  
    SECONDARY: [Park] → [Bike path] → [Community hub]  
    TERTIARY: Split-up → Public transit (no regroup until safe)  

Mark routes discreetly using chalk or tape:

    ▲ = Clear route  
    ▼ = Blocked or dangerous

## Field Communication Protocols

Voice commands:

    - "Bird in flight" = Prep to move (non-alarming).  
    - "Sunset now" = Immediate dispersal (no questions).  
    - "Medic echo" = Follow medic line out.

Signal/Radio format:

    [PRIORITY] [LOCATION] [NEED]  
    "RED – 5th/Broadway – KETTLE"  
    "YELLOW – SE Park – MEDIC"  

<Callout type="hint">
If networks degrade, switch to runners and pre‑agreed brevity codes. See <a href="/academy/app-free-tactics">App‑Free Tactics</a> and harden devices per <a href="/academy/digital-security-basics">Digital Security Basics</a>.
</Callout>

---

## Extraction Drills (Monthly Practice)

    - **Blindfold Navigation:** Navigate to fallback while blindfolded to build route memory.  
    - **Runner Relay:** Pass evacuation orders via runners (no tech).  
    - **Blackout Scenario:** Escape with no lights, no comms, whisper-only coordination.

<Callout type="warning">
**Never** regroup at your dispersal point.  
Fallback locations should be **300+ meters away** to avoid follow-up arrests.
</Callout>

---

## Vulnerability Prioritization

Evacuation order:

    1. Disabled participants, medics, legal observers.  
    2. General participants.  
    3. Trained marshals or safety coordinators (if unavoidable).  

Always assign **buddy pairs** before events to prevent lost individuals.

<Callout type="info">
Language access matters. If interpretation may be needed during movement, consider volunteers with the <a href="/academy/translator-micro-badge">Translator Micro‑badge</a> or <a href="/academy/asl-interpreter-micro-badge">ASL Interpreter Micro‑badge</a>.
</Callout>

---

## Gear Checklist for Exits

    - Reflective or glow tape (for night tracking).  
    - Whistles (3 short bursts = danger signal).  
    - Preloaded burner metro or transit cards (waterproof case).  
    - UV map tattoos (temporary, for night visibility).

<Callout type="warning">
Use only non‑identifying, removable markings. Remove or cover markings after dispersal; do not photograph or share them publicly.
</Callout>

---

## After-Action Musts

    - 15-minute headcount at fallback point.  
    - Trauma check: water, blankets, grounding exercises.  
    - OPSEC cleanup: Clear temporary maps/codes and reduce comms logs per policy. Follow <a href="/academy/after-action-data-hygiene">After‑Action Data Hygiene</a>.

<Callout type="info">
Schedule a brief debrief and ensure care/legal follow‑through: <a href="/academy/crisis-debrief-care">Crisis Debriefing & Mutual Care</a>, <a href="/academy/care-support">Care & Emotional Support</a>, <a href="/academy/court-support">Court Support</a>, and <a href="/academy/legal-followthrough-jail-support">Legal Follow‑through & Jail Support</a>.
</Callout>

---

## Final Checklist (Enhanced)

    - [ ] Can identify police and crowd behavior leading to escalation.  
    - [ ] Has practiced **no-comms escapes** with pod members.  
    - [ ] Knows **3+ fallback points by heart** (not just on paper).  
    - [ ] Has trained at least 2 backups on all exit and de-escalation protocols.

<Callout type="success">
**Remember:** The most effective evacuations look calm and uneventful.  
Practice until **smooth exits become muscle memory**.
</Callout>
