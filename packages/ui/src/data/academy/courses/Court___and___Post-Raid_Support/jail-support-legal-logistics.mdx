# ⚖️ Jail Support & Legal Logistics
<TrackBadge variant="legal-support" />
<TrackBadge variant="field-safety" />

<Callout type="info">
Legal defense begins at arrest. This course shows how to track detainees, support families, and coordinate release safely and ethically — without exposing personal data or putting anyone at further risk.
</Callout>

---

## 🧭 Overview
When arrests happen, **preparation saves lives, time, and trust.**  
Jail support ensures no one faces the system alone, keeps morale high for families and pods, and protects sensitive data from state or media exposure.

This course covers:
- Hotline setup and call flow
- Safe information tracking
- Coordinated release operations
- Care and communication post-release

> Jail support is not reactive — it’s part of pre-action planning.

---

## 🧩 Learn & Do

### 1. Legal Hotline & Intake System
- Use a **dedicated phone number** (Signal, encrypted VoIP, or staffed Google Voice).  
- Maintain **2–3 trusted operators** who log arrests, locations, and time of contact.  
- Store info in **offline spreadsheets** or encrypted notes — never cloud-synced.  
- Create **hand-off protocols** between shifts to avoid message loss.

**Example Call Script**
> “This is Jail Support. Can you confirm your full name, birthdate, and what city or precinct you’re being held in? Do you consent for us to share this with our legal contact for release?”

<Callout type="warning">
Never store or share data without consent.  
If the caller sounds unsafe (e.g., being overheard), keep questions minimal and generic.
</Callout>

---

### 2. Tracking Detainees Safely
**Minimum data to track:**
- Full legal name (if provided)
- Arrest location and time
- Precinct or jail
- Bail or release status
- Assigned legal observer or NLG contact

**Do not store:**
- Political affiliations  
- Immigration or employment details  
- Notes about prior arrests or personal history

Use **non-identifiable codes** if you must coordinate public updates (e.g., “Case Red-04 released 11:30pm”).

---

### 3. Coordination Roles

| Role | Function |
|------|-----------|
| Jail Support Lead | Coordinates all comms, liaises with legal observers |
| Intake Operator | Runs hotline, gathers verified info |
| Rides Coordinator | Tracks vehicles and timing for pickups |
| Care Coordinator | Organizes food, clothes, and rest post-release |
| Legal Liaison | Manages communication with NLG or attorneys |

At least **two people per role** for redundancy.

### 5. Care Packs & Release Logistics
Prepare basic **“release kits”** for each expected person:
- Water, food, phone charger, cash for transport  
- Spare clothes (comfortable, weather-appropriate)  
- Printed legal numbers and support hotlines  
- Notes of affirmation or pod contact info  

Store these at **secure staging sites** or in vehicles.

<Callout type="info">
Pair this operation with [Runners & Rides](/courses/runners-and-rides) for field-ready logistics and fallback dispatching.
</Callout>

---

### 6. Secure Communication Protocols
- Use **Signal or encrypted calls** for sensitive updates.  
- Keep **release updates verbal**, not written, whenever possible.  
- Delete temporary messages daily.  
- Confirm consent before notifying families or pods.

> “They’re safe and released” is enough for most updates — avoid details.

---

### 7. After-Action Legal Debrief
1. Record verified outcomes (released, transferred, pending).  
2. Update trusted roster of legal contacts.  
3. Debrief operators: what worked, what failed, what was stressful.  
4. Offer decompression — legal support burnout is real.  
5. Rotate leads regularly to prevent fatigue.

---

### 8. Safety & Data Hygiene
- Store logs offline or air-gapped; encrypt if digital.  
- Shred or delete data once all cases are resolved.  
- Never post names, mugshots, or charges online.  
- When sharing success stories, anonymize completely.  

<Callout type="warning">
Posting release updates or “mugshot collages” online can directly endanger comrades.  
Visibility ≠ safety.
</Callout>
