# ⚖️ Court Support Essentials
<TrackBadge variant="observation-legal" />
<TrackBadge variant="community-care" />

<Callout type="info">
Use calm, precise language. Court support means being present, not loud.  
This course teaches how to provide safe, consent-based support during immigration and criminal court hearings without overstepping legal or emotional boundaries.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
Courtrooms are often isolating spaces. Showing up communicates: **you are not alone**.  
Your presence provides accountability and solidarity—but must be careful, informed, and discreet.  
You’ll learn how to prepare, what to expect, and how to follow through responsibly after court ends.

---

## Learn & Do

### 1. What Is Court Support
Support roles include:
- Accompanying someone to court  
- Sitting quietly and offering presence  
- Helping with transport, childcare, or translation  
- Debriefing after the hearing  

You are **not a lawyer** or spokesperson — you are a steady ally.

---

### 2. Before the Hearing
**Gather info:**
- Date, time, courtroom number  
- Hearing type (arraignment, bond, trial)  
- Names, pronouns, and consent for presence  
- Confirm whether it’s **immigration** or **criminal** court  

**Plan logistics:**
- Coordinate arrival and meet-up point  
- Dress neutrally (no slogans or all-black protest gear)  
- Check building rules (phones, bags, recording devices)  
- Prepare interpreters or childcare if needed  

<Callout type="info">
For language coordination, review the <a href="/courses/translator-micro-badge">Translator</a> and <a href="/courses/asl-interpreter-micro-badge">ASL Interpreter</a> micro-badges.
</Callout>

---

### 3. During the Hearing
**Your Role: Silent Power**
- Sit calmly and quietly.  
- Make gentle eye contact or place a hand over your heart.  
- Avoid emotional outbursts or commentary.  
- Respect staff directions even when unfair.  

**Avoid:**
- Recording, taking notes (if prohibited), or reacting aloud.  
- Offering advice, translation, or legal input unless qualified.  
- Posting or live-tweeting — even positive updates risk harm.

<Callout type="hint">
If limited note-taking is allowed, use minimal detail. Follow <a href="/courses/documentation-safety">Documentation Safety</a> for secure retention and redaction.
</Callout>

---

### 4. After the Hearing
- Ask: “Would you like to talk about what happened?”  
- Offer practical help (ride home, meal, decompression space).  
- Record next steps privately (future dates, paperwork).  
- Connect with care or legal teams for follow-up.  

<Callout type="info">
Coordinate with <a href="/courses/legal-followthrough-jail-support">Legal Follow-through & Jail Support</a> and <a href="/courses/care-support">Care & Emotional Support</a> for post-hearing support.
</Callout>

---

### 5. Immigration vs. Criminal Court

| Feature | Immigration Court | Criminal Court |
|----------|-------------------|----------------|
| Judge Type | Appointed | Elected or appointed |
| Rights | No public defender | Right to attorney if poor |
| Main Goal | Deportation | Punishment or release |
| Access | Often closed | Usually public |
| Language | Limited interpretation | Interpreters common |

---

### 6. Consent & Safety
- Always confirm the person **wants** your presence.  
- Never share identifying details without consent.  
- Remove names, faces, or case numbers from posts.  
- Coordinate with trusted dispatch or legal observers before publicizing any case.

<Callout type="warning">
Even well-meaning posts can be weaponized by prosecutors or ICE. Protect identities at all times.
</Callout>

---

### 7. If Something Goes Wrong
- **If detained/arrested:** Alert legal hotline, not social media.  
- **If told to leave:** Exit calmly and regroup outside.  
- **If harassment occurs:** Log discreetly and report via support lead.  

Stay steady. Calm presence defuses escalation.

---

### 8. Becoming a Court Supporter
1. Attend a hearing as an observer.  
2. Connect with local mutual aid or immigrant rights orgs.  
3. Shadow experienced volunteers.  
4. Learn trauma-informed communication.  
5. Debrief and log notes securely after every session.

<Callout type="info">
After each support action, file a short debrief and handle notes according to <a href="/courses/after-action-data-hygiene">After-Action Data Hygiene</a>.
</Callout>
