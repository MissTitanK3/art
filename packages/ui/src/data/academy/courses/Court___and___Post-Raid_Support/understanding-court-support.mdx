# ⚖️ Court & Post-Raid Support Coordination
<TrackBadge variant="legal-support" />
<TrackBadge variant="community-care" />

<Callout type="info">
When arrests or raids occur, the crisis doesn’t end at release.  
This course teaches how to sustain legal, financial, and emotional support from first appearance through resolution — without exposing private data or retraumatizing those involved.
</Callout>

---

## 🧭 Overview
Court support is the long tail of community defense.  
It requires persistence, confidentiality, and care.  
Pods that maintain steady communication, clear documentation, and reliable aid through trials demonstrate *real solidarity* — not just reaction.

> “Freedom isn’t only the absence of cages. It’s the presence of care.”

---

## 🧩 Learn & Do

### 1. Build a Court Support Pod
Essential roles:
- **Court Liaison:** tracks dates, coordinates with attorneys.  
- **Family Liaison:** provides updates, childcare, and emotional support.  
- **Finance Coordinator:** manages bail, commissary, and travel stipends.  
- **Comms Lead:** prepares verified updates for trusted channels only.

Create a **post-arrest checklist** shared with legal observers and dispatch teams.

<Callout type="hint">
Pod size can be as small as three people, but must always include a communication redundancy and legal contact.
</Callout>

---

### 2. Secure Information Handling
- Use encrypted chat or in-person briefings for all updates.  
- Never post real names, charges, or photos online.  
- Keep legal documents in **offline encrypted folders** or paper binders stored securely.  
- Delete temporary notes after resolution.  
- Document consent before any public statement or fundraiser.

<Callout type="warning">
Public solidarity ≠ public disclosure.  
Safety and dignity come before publicity.
</Callout>

---

### 3. Coordinating with Attorneys
- Maintain one consistent liaison to reduce confusion.  
- Share logistics support only — never legal advice.  
- Ask attorneys what information is safe to circulate.  
- Track appearances, plea deadlines, and outcomes in a private log.  
- Encourage defendants to request discovery and provide updates when appropriate.

> The goal is support, not substitution for counsel.

---

### 4. Post-Raid Recovery
After raids, focus on **stability** first:
1. Confirm physical safety and legal status of all affected.  
2. Secure housing, food, and communication devices.  
3. Provide emotional check-ins, not interrogations.  
4. Coordinate donation or repair drives discreetly.  
5. Avoid internal speculation or blame until facts are verified.

<Callout type="important">
Never share raid footage or photos online.  
Retraumatization and surveillance harm outweigh public curiosity.
</Callout>

---

### 5. Public Messaging & Solidarity
When safe to speak publicly:
- Use **collective language**, not personal identifiers.  
- Focus on conditions, not charges.  
- Credit collective care work, not individuals.  
- Use regional channels, not national ones, for initial updates.  
- Prepare *redacted statements* vetted by the affected person(s) or attorney.

---

### 6. Long-Term Recovery
Legal and emotional processes often outlast attention spans.

Sustain care by:
- Scheduling monthly check-ins for affected comrades.  
- Creating mutual aid funds for legal costs and mental health.  
- Hosting reflection or learning sessions after cases conclude.  
- Offering re-entry roles that match comfort and safety level.

> Healing and reintegration are part of resistance.
