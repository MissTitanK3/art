# 📡 Comms Collapse Scenarios & Offline Protocols  
<TrackBadge variant="default" />

<Callout type="warning">
🛰️ If Signal goes down, internet gets cut, or your tools are compromised, **what’s your backup?** This course gives you fallback protocols for working securely and effectively when digital infrastructure disappears or becomes unsafe.
</Callout>

---

## 🧠 Why This Matters

Recent years have shown:
- Governments can **shut off mobile service** (e.g. Iran, Sudan, India)
- Protest zones often experience **cell jamming or blackouts**
- Cops are using IMSI catchers, stingrays, and router spoofing
- **Surveillance dragnets** can make even “safe” apps dangerous

You need to train for **the day your phone fails**.

---

## 🎯 What You’ll Learn

- How to build fallback systems before a failure happens  
- How to communicate without phones or internet  
- How to use radios, runners, and printed tools  
- When to fall back, hold, or shut down ops  
- How to practice collapses *without causing panic*

## 🚨 Comms Collapse Scenarios

| Scenario | Real Examples | Result |
|----------|---------------|--------|
| Signal stops working | DDoS, server takedown | Teams go dark mid-op |
| Cell network disrupted | Stingray or protest zone jammer | Location data leaks, apps fail |
| Internet cut off | State shutdown or tower destruction | Dispatch unreachable |
| Surveillance spike | Stingray, facial rec, keyword targeting | All comms risky to use |

## 🔁 The Collapse Playbook

### 🛠️ 1. Build Redundancy (Before Failure)

- Have at least **two ways** to reach any pod (e.g. Signal + paper + radio)
- Print the most important tools: KYR sheets, pod lists, fallback maps
- Build fallback meetup sites or “dead drop” points
- Assign each pod a **comms fallback lead** who knows protocols

<Callout type="info">
If you rely on runners for handoffs, align with <a href="/courses/runners-and-rides">Runners & Rides</a> and your region’s <a href="/courses/responding-to-dispatch-calls">Responding to Dispatch Calls</a> guidance.
</Callout>

---

### 📍 2. Establish Offline Rendezvous Protocols

| Element | What It Does |
|--------|----------------|
| Meetup time slots | “If comms are down, meet here at 9am/12pm/4pm” |
| Fallback sites | Cafes, parks, or known mutual aid spaces |
| Shifted check-ins | Instead of “message me,” say “check the site at X time” |
| Paper updates | Sign-in logs, flyers, QR-less updates |

<Callout type="hint">
Printed materials should follow <a href="/courses/documentation-safety">Documentation Safety</a> norms. Avoid names/faces. Keep retention windows short; see <a href="/courses/after-action-data-hygiene">After‑Action Data Hygiene</a>.
</Callout>

---

### 🧍 3. Use Human Runners

- Bike, walk, or drive updates between pods  
- Wear no logos, carry no identifying documents  
- Carry written updates on paper slips or signal cards  
- Confirm deliveries **verbally** or with a written receipt system  
- Use shared code phrases (e.g. “Grandma’s soup” = safe site OK)

---

### 📻 4. Use Radios (If Safe)

| Tool | Use Case | Risk |
|------|----------|------|
| FRS/GMRS radios | Close-range team comms | Public, unencrypted |
| Baofeng or VHF | Longer range, customizable | Illegal use can be tracked |
| LoRa (Meshtastic) | Silent, encrypted, low power | Requires pre-sync and setup |

📌 Always assume **radio is public** unless you control both ends securely.

<Callout type="info">
LoRa-based tools like Meshtastic can bridge pods with no cell or internet. Great for slow syncs or signal drops.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
Radios are often public. Don’t share PII, plans, or sensitive identifiers over the air. Use pre-agreed codes and non-actionable phrasing.
</Callout>

---

### 📦 5. Pack an Offline Ops Kit

Every pod lead or coordinator should carry:

- [ ] Printed pod list with aliases and risk levels  
- [ ] Map with fallback sites and rally zones  
- [ ] Marker, paper, and dry-erase board  
- [ ] Flash drive with toolkits (read-only mode)  
- [ ] Backup power (solar, hand crank, battery bank)  
- [ ] Scanner radio or Baofeng + charger (if licensed)  
- [ ] KYR flyers, pod cards, Signal starter zines  
- [ ] Burner clipboard for logs and drop messages

<Callout type="info">
For device and SIM compartmentalization during collapse, see <a href="/courses/burner-phones-security-culture">Burner Phones & Security Culture</a>.
</Callout>

## 🔐 Secure Offline Comms Practices

| Practice | Purpose |
|----------|----------|
| Code phrases | Share actionable info without revealing plans |
| Hand signals | Use standard field comms to alert or direct |
| Chalk/marker drops | Signal status of zones (e.g. ⚠️ for ICE seen) |
| Paper-based alerts | “Flash bulletin” graphics for door-to-door info |
| Shifted language | Say “bike drop” instead of “dispatch log” |

<Callout type="hint">
Use mundane, repeatable language. Avoid activist slang or insider terms during collapse.
</Callout>

## 🧪 Practice Drill Example

🧠 _Simulate a comms blackout:_

1. Turn off all phones or internet access  
2. Assign a known issue (e.g. ICE sighting at H-Mart)  
3. Attempt to alert 3 nearby pods using:  
   - Printed flyers  
   - Runners  
   - Radios  
4. Log how long it took. Where did confusion happen? What failed?  

✅ **Debrief afterward. Update your pod’s collapse plan.**
