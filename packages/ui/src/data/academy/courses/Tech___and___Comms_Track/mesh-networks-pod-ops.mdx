## Why It Matters

Pods need **reliable, private communications** that survive cell outages and resist surveillance.  
Meshtastic networks let pods:
- Stay in contact during protests or disaster response.  
- Relay information between scouts, medics, and logistics.  
- Operate **off-grid for hours or days** with minimal gear.

With planning, even a small pod can build a network that **covers entire neighborhoods** without drawing attention.

## What You'll Learn

1. **Choosing and Setting Up Hardware** – Picking the right nodes and antennas.  
2. **Flashing and Configuring Devices** – Installing firmware and pairing with phones.  
3. **Securing Your Network** – Unique channel names, encryption keys, and good practices.  
4. **Deploying Relays** – Extending range in urban or rural environments.  
5. **Running Operations** – Best practices for short-term and mobile deployments.  
6. **Battery and Power Management** – Keeping your network alive for hours or days.

---

## Quick Action Steps

1. Get **2–3 Meshtastic nodes** (LilyGO T-Beams are the standard).  
2. Flash the latest stable firmware using the **Web Flasher** or CLI.  
3. Set a **custom channel name and PSK** (never use defaults).  
4. Place **1 relay node** at elevation (roof, hill, vehicle) to boost range.  
5. Run a **30-minute field test** with your pod to confirm coverage.

## Hardware & Antenna Guide

| Device                      | Best Use                          | Notes                                                    |
|-----------------------------|-----------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| **LilyGO T-Beam**           | Main handheld device             | GPS, long range (5–10 km urban with good antennas). ~$60 |
| **Heltec V3**               | Lightweight backup or relay      | Small, no GPS, moderate range (2–5 km). ~$40             |
| **RAK or ESP32 Relay Node** | Fixed coverage booster           | Needs stable power (AC or solar). ~$80–$120              |
| **High-Gain Antennas (5–9dBi)** | Extending coverage significantly | Pairs with T-Beams and relays for rural or rooftop use.  |

Tips:
- Always **upgrade antennas** for relays (stock ones are weak).  
- Keep a **spare battery bank (10,000–20,000 mAh)** for each active node.  
- Use **solar trickle chargers** for stationary relays during daytime ops.

## Flashing and Configuration

1. **Download the latest firmware** from [Meshtastic.org](https://meshtastic.org/downloads).  
2. Use the **Web Flasher** (Chrome-based) or Meshtastic CLI.  
3. Install the **Meshtastic App** (Android/iOS).  
4. Pair your phone to the node over Bluetooth.  
5. Set a **new channel name and PSK** (pre-shared key).  
6. Disable unnecessary features (like GPS beacons) unless needed for your role.

<Callout type="hint">
Never reuse channel keys for more than one event. Rotate after each action.
</Callout>

---

## Securing Your Channel

Security mistakes can expose your pod:
- Always **generate a random PSK** (avoid obvious words).  
- Share keys in person or over an encrypted channel (never public chat).  
- Use **role-based channels** (separate keys for scouts, medics, logistics).  
- Keep your node labeled with a **code name, not your real name**.

---

## Deploying Relays for Coverage

Relays help extend your network:
- Place them **high** (rooftops, trees, tall vehicles).  
- Use **stationary relays** for known hotspots.  
- For moving actions, use **vehicle-based relays** with power banks.  
- If possible, **camouflage** relays (inside weatherproof boxes or disguised enclosures).

Spacing Guidelines:
- Urban: **1–2 km apart** for consistent coverage.  
- Rural: **5–8 km apart** (requires high-gain antennas).

---

## Power & Battery Management

A network is useless if devices die mid-operation.  
- Carry **spare USB-C battery packs** (10,000 mAh = ~12–18 hrs runtime).  
- For long ops, rotate nodes and **recharge via solar or vehicles**.  
- Stationary relays should have **solar panels or AC adapters** if possible.

<Callout type="warning">
Nodes left unpowered can create gaps. Always have at least **one backup relay** on standby.
</Callout>

## Running Short-Term Operations

For a protest or supply run:
1. Start by testing all devices at base.  
2. Deploy relays first, then field teams.  
3. Use **short messages and code words** to minimize chatter.  
4. Avoid mentioning sensitive plans over the network (even encrypted).  
5. After the operation, **collect all devices** and rotate PSKs for next time.

---

## Pod Drill (Certification)

1. Configure **5 nodes** with a shared encrypted channel.  
2. Deploy a relay at elevation to extend range.  
3. Run a **30-minute drill** where each team must check in every 10 minutes.  
4. Simulate a **relay failure** and reestablish contact by deploying a backup.  
5. Demonstrate **rotating PSKs** and confirming all devices are rekeyed.
