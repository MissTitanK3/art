## 🧭 Overview
Every action carries both **moral and material consequences.**  
Ethical decision-making in direct action isn’t about perfection — it’s about **transparency, consent, and foresight**.

Movements fail when harm to participants or communities is ignored in the name of speed or visibility.  
This course teaches how to weigh impact, risk, and consent so your team can act boldly *and* responsibly.

> “The goal isn’t just to win. It’s to stay human while doing it.”

---

## 🧩 Learn & Do

### 1. Framework for Ethical Evaluation
Before any action, run a **three-part ethical check**:

| Question | Why It Matters |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Who is impacted?** | Identifies direct and indirect harm. |
| **What are the risks?** | Prevents preventable trauma or arrests. |
| **Who consents?** | Ensures agency and voluntary participation. |
| **Who benefits?** | Clarifies motive vs. impact. |
| **What happens afterward?** | Prepares for fallout, repair, and safety. |

Actions that cannot answer these clearly are not ready.

---

### 2. Risk vs. Impact Matrix
Visualize actions using this 2x2 framework:

|  | **Low Impact** | **High Impact** |
|--|----------------|----------------|
| **Low Risk** | Awareness campaign, teach-in | Supply distribution, banner drop |
| **High Risk** | Confrontation with security | Occupation, blockade, exposure of agents |

Your goal: **maximize impact with the least possible harm.**  
If risk cannot be reduced, ensure full, informed consent from all participants.

---

### 3. Informed Consent in Actions
Informed consent means everyone understands:
- The *real* risks (legal, emotional, physical).  
- Their right to decline or step back.  
- That consent can be withdrawn at any time.  
- How their image, data, and story may be used.

Before an action:
1. Hold a **briefing** explaining intent, logistics, risks, and alternatives.  
2. Offer **opt-out options** without pressure.  
3. Verify consent verbally or through agreed signals.  
4. Ensure all documentation (photos, videos) follows clear privacy agreements.

<Callout type="warning">
Never pressure anyone to participate.  
Never document others without explicit, ongoing consent.
</Callout>

---

### 4. Harm Minimization & Exit Planning
Every action should include:
- A **pre-identified exit route** or fallback zone.  
- A **jail or legal support contact**.  
- A **designated medic or care role**.  
- A **clear signal for withdrawal** (“sunset,” “pullback,” etc.).  
- **Post-action debriefs** and emotional check-ins.

> “If you can’t plan the exit, you’re not ready to enter.”

<Callout type="hint">
Use [Escalation & Evacuation Protocols](/courses/escalation-evacuation-protocols) and [Crisis Debrief & Care](/courses/crisis-debrief-care) to integrate safety and recovery steps into your action plan.
</Callout>

---

### 5. Ethical Documentation
Documenting actions can build power — or cause harm.

**Best practices:**
- Blur or crop identifiable features.  
- Never post live updates that reveal location or movement.  
- Securely delete raw media after use.  
- Share outcomes, not faces.  
- Credit collectives, not individuals.

<Callout type="info">
Visibility ≠ safety.  
Visibility ≠ consent.
</Callout>

---

### 6. Accountability After Action
If harm occurs — physical, emotional, or reputational:
1. Pause operations.  
2. Acknowledge the harm publicly *without naming survivors*.  
3. Offer repair or mediation options.  
4. Adjust protocols and briefings for next time.  
5. Log lessons learned for future pods.

> Accountability is what makes movements sustainable, not apology statements.
