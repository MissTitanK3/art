# 🤝 Cross-Regional Solidarity & Cultural Ethics
<TrackBadge variant="movement-strategy" />
<TrackBadge variant="community-care" />

<Callout type="info">
Solidarity fails when respect ends.  
This course builds ethical foundations for working across diverse cultures, regions, and organizing traditions without exploitation or erasure.
</Callout>

---

## 🌍 Overview
In decentralized movements, no two regions look the same.  
Differences in culture, language, risk, and lived experience require **ethical collaboration**, not control.

This course helps organizers navigate cross-regional partnerships with humility, transparency, and consent — ensuring every exchange strengthens the whole network.

> Solidarity is not sameness. It’s alignment in purpose.

---

## 🧩 Learn & Do

### 1. Principles of Ethical Solidarity
Before working with another region or group, clarify:

| Principle | Practice |
|------------|-----------|
| **Autonomy** | Each region sets its own priorities and methods. |
| **Consent** | Always ask before replicating or amplifying others’ work. |
| **Transparency** | Share motives, data use, and expected outcomes. |
| **Mutual Benefit** | Collaboration must improve both sides’ capacity. |
| **Respect** | Never override or tokenize local experience. |

<Callout type="hint">
The goal is mutual reinforcement — not extraction, supervision, or branding.
</Callout>

---

### 2. Avoiding Cultural Extraction
Unethical collaboration often looks like:
- Copying materials or visuals from marginalized groups without credit  
- Translating without consent or cultural review  
- Speaking *about* rather than *with* impacted communities  
- Prioritizing optics over impact  

Replace extraction with:
- Shared authorship and translation rights  
- Fair attribution  
- Consent for adaptation or publication  
- Ongoing dialogue about representation

---

### 3. Building Trust Across Differences
To bridge cultures and regions:
1. Start with **listening sessions**, not plans.  
2. Identify **shared values** rather than identical tactics.  
3. Create **memoranda of understanding (MOUs)** for joint work.  
4. Establish **red lines** — what’s off limits for each side.  
5. Agree on **conflict resolution channels** before problems arise.

> “Trust is slow to earn, fast to burn, and always worth the effort.”

---

### 4. Ethics of Resource Sharing
When sharing funds, materials, or data:
- Use written agreements, even if informal.  
- Specify access limits and expiration dates.  
- Avoid imposing technology or platforms unilaterally.  
- Prioritize equitable resource flow to under-resourced regions.  
- Document transfers transparently for accountability.

<Callout type="warning">
Avoid “digital colonization.”  
Technology and funding should empower, not centralize control.
</Callout>

---

### 5. Representing Other Regions
Never speak *for* a region without clear authorization.  
Instead:
- Ask if they want representation or prefer to speak directly.  
- Share media or updates only with explicit consent.  
- Use anonymous framing (“organizers in X region report…”) if safety requires.

Representation is a **privilege**, not an entitlement.

---

### 6. Handling Cross-Cultural Conflict
When harm or misunderstanding occurs:
1. Pause communications, not relationships.  
2. Engage neutral mediators from outside both regions.  
3. Translate all materials clearly and review for bias.  
4. Focus on repair, not blame.  
5. Document lessons for future collaboration.

> Accountability across cultures is a sign of maturity, not failure.

- What are your red lines for collaboration or representation?
