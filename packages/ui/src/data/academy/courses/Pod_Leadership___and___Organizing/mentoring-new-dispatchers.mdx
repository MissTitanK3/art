---

## Why Mentorship Matters

Strong networks are sustained by people who model reliability and composure. Mentorship helps:

- Prevent burnout through support and debriefs  
- Spot behavioral or procedural gaps early  
- Pass field-tested wisdom to new responders  
- Maintain tone and ethics across dispatch teams  

<Callout type="success">
You don’t need to know everything. You need to **stay steady and transparent** when uncertainty hits.
</Callout>

---

## Core Mentorship Practices

### 1. Teach Calm, Not Control
- Model emotional regulation under pressure.  
- Normalize mistakes and show correction in real time.  
- Encourage reflection before response.  

### 2. Use Feedback Loops
- Debrief every major dispatch cycle.  
- Ask: “What worked? What felt unclear? What should we change?”  
- Keep critiques private, affirm growth publicly.  

### 3. Model Transparent Dispatching
Narrate your logic aloud:  
> “I’m holding this report until I verify GPS data.”

Let trainees see *how* to reason, not just results.

### 4. Document Learning Moments
- Track what each trainee has shadowed or handled.  
- Share old cases as anonymized examples.  
- Keep brief, encrypted notes for progress tracking.

### 5. Protect While Teaching
- Never assign backend access before certification.  
- Start with observation, then supervised action.  
- Step in gently if a trainee freezes, panics, or overloads.

## Common Mentorship Scenarios

| Situation | Mentor Response |
|------------|----------------|
| Trainee reacts too fast | Coach breathing and double verification |
| Missed urgency cues | Review urgency scale (minutes vs. hours) |
| Overuse of activations | Review activation thresholds and harm potential |
| Fear of escalation | Walk through chain of command calmly |

<Callout type="info">
Even experienced dispatchers need periodic refreshers and mentorship check-ins.
</Callout>

## Documentation & Privacy

- Use tags like `#mentoring`, `#shadowing`, `#reviewed-by-[name]`.  
- Log only what’s necessary for improvement, not personal data.  
- Share securely via encrypted or offline files.

<Callout type="hint">
Follow [Documentation Safety](/courses/documentation-safety) and [After-Action Data Hygiene](/courses/after-action-data-hygiene).  
Avoid storing chat logs or raw notes in public drives. Delete progress docs once confirmed.
</Callout>

---

## Ethical Mentorship Boundaries

- Always get consent before reviewing personal messages or case decisions.  
- Never gossip about a trainee’s performance.  
- Prioritize emotional safety — mentorship is not supervision.  
- Use nonviolent feedback: describe behavior, not character.
