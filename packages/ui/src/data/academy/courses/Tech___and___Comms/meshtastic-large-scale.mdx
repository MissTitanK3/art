## Overview
When your pod grows beyond 15 nodes, new risks appear:
- Network chatter increases and power drains faster.  
- Surveillance and pattern exposure rise.  
- Relay or gateway loss can disrupt entire zones.  

This course trains you to design and manage **large-scale mesh networks**—100+ devices across neighborhoods or regions. You’ll learn segmentation, redundancy, relay management, and coordination across pods while keeping the system resilient and secure.

---

## Learn & Do

### 1. Scaling Tactics
To handle growth:
- **Segment channels by role:**  
  - Separate PSKs for medics, scouts, logistics, and leadership.  
  - Reduces chatter and isolates sensitive info.  
- **Relay spacing:**  
  - Urban: 1–2 km apart for overlapping coverage.  
  - Rural: 5–8 km apart with high-gain antennas.  
- **Gateways:**  
  - Limit to 1–2 leadership nodes.  
  - Disable cloud sync; use manual push/pull.  
- **Anonymous relays:**  
  - Hide inside weatherproof boxes or disguised mounts.  
  - Rotate locations to avoid pattern tracking.

### 2. Relay & Gateway Infrastructure
For city-scale networks:
- **Primary relays:** rooftops, water towers, or tall buildings.  
- **Secondary mobile relays:** vehicles, bike trailers, or backpacks.  
- **Gateways:** for leadership only, behind **VPN or Tor**.  

**Power planning**
- 20,000–50,000 mAh per relay.  
- 20–40W solar panels for daytime charging.  
- Rotate relays and batteries every 12 hours.

---

### 3. Power Management
- Assign a **Power Officer** per zone.  
- Keep at least **two charged spares per relay**.  
- Recharge using **vehicle alternators** or small generators.  
- Relays can serve as **charging stations** for team devices if capacity allows.

<Callout type="warning">
Power failures can collapse the network. Always keep replacements and rotation plans ready.
</Callout>

---

### 4. Camouflage & Anti-Surveillance
- Hide relays in **locked, weatherproof containers** labeled generically (“Sensor Node”).  
- Avoid repeating placement patterns; rotate locations.  
- Use **low-power mode** during inactivity.  
- If compromised, **rekey all PSKs immediately** and deploy backups.

---

### 5. Cross-Pod Coordination
- Use **hub-and-spoke design**: local pods connect via a few trusted bridges.  
- Avoid one massive shared channel.  
- Assign **trusted liaisons** for bridging.  
- Sync messages in timed bursts, not constant traffic.

---

### 6. Security & Redundancy
- Rotate PSKs weekly during operations.  
- Keep **encrypted offline logs** (paper + VeraCrypt).  
- Maintain one **backup relay per zone**.  
- Train three operator layers: primary, secondary, tertiary.

<Callout type="hint">
Use minimal, time-limited data storage. Avoid raw logs or chat history in shared apps.  
Always obtain explicit consent before storing or sharing technical or location data.
</Callout>

---

### 7. Large-Scale Drill (Certification)
1. Deploy a 20-node network across two or more neighborhoods.  
2. Maintain 24-hour continuous coverage with relay rotation.  
3. Simulate a relay seizure or failure and restore service within 15 minutes.  
4. Run multi-channel encrypted traffic for three pod teams simultaneously.  
5. Produce a network plan and post-op report for review.

<Callout type="warning">
Respect consent, legal boundaries, and privacy when conducting drills or operations.
</Callout>
