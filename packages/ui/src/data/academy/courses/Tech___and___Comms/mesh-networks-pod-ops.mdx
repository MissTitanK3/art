## Overview
Pods require **reliable, private communications** that can survive outages and resist surveillance.  
Meshtastic networks allow small groups to:
- Stay connected during protests or disasters.  
- Relay information between scouts, medics, and logistics.  
- Operate off-grid for hours or days with minimal gear.  

With planning, even a small pod can cover entire neighborhoods discreetly.  
This course covers hardware, configuration, security, relay deployment, and field operation techniques for pod-scale networks.

## Learn & Do

### 1. Hardware Setup
| Device | Best Use | Notes |
|---------|-----------|-------|
| **LilyGO T-Beam** | Main handheld device | GPS, long range (5–10 km). ~$60 |
| **Heltec V3** | Lightweight backup or relay | Small, no GPS, moderate range (2–5 km). ~$40 |
| **RAK / ESP32 Relay Node** | Fixed coverage booster | Needs steady power (AC or solar). ~$80–$120 |
| **High-Gain Antennas (5–9 dBi)** | Extending coverage | Ideal for rooftops and rural relays. |

**Tips**
- Upgrade antennas on relays (stock ones are weak).  
- Keep spare 10 000–20 000 mAh battery banks for each node.  
- Use solar trickle chargers for stationary relays.

### 2. Flashing and Configuration
1. Download the latest firmware from [Meshtastic.org](https://meshtastic.org/downloads).  
2. Use the **Web Flasher** (Chrome) or **CLI**.  
3. Install the **Meshtastic App** (Android/iOS).  
4. Pair your phone with the node via Bluetooth.  
5. Set a **custom channel name** and **PSK** (never use defaults).  
6. Disable unneeded features such as GPS beacons unless required.

<Callout type="hint">
Rotate channel keys after every operation or event.
</Callout>

---

### 3. Securing the Channel
- Generate random PSKs, not words or dates.  
- Share keys in person or via encrypted messaging.  
- Use **role-based channels** (scouts, medics, logistics).  
- Label devices with **code names only**, not real identities.

---

### 4. Deploying Relays
Relays extend range and network reliability.

**Guidelines**
- Place high: rooftops, trees, tall vehicles.  
- Use stationary relays for known hotspots.  
- Vehicle-based relays can move with supply runs.  
- Camouflage relays in weatherproof boxes.

**Spacing**
- Urban: 1–2 km apart.  
- Rural: 5–8 km apart with high-gain antennas.

---

### 5. Power & Battery Management
- Use 10 000 mAh USB-C banks for 12–18 hr runtime.  
- Rotate nodes to recharge via solar or vehicles.  
- Keep at least **one backup relay** charged.

<Callout type="warning">
If relays fail or lose power, communication gaps form quickly.  
Always prepare replacements.
</Callout>

<Callout type="hint">
Use encrypted, short-term documentation only. Delete temporary device logs after review.  
ART does not store in-app mesh histories; always obtain consent before sharing technical or location data.
</Callout>

---

### 6. Field Operations
**Before deployment**
1. Test all devices at HQ.  
2. Deploy relays first, then field teams.  
3. Use short messages and code words.  
4. Avoid sensitive discussions even on encrypted channels.  
5. After action, collect devices and rotate PSKs.

---

### 7. Pod Drill (Certification)
1. Configure 5 nodes on a shared encrypted channel.  
2. Deploy a relay at elevation.  
3. Run a 30-minute drill with 10-minute check-ins.  
4. Simulate a relay failure and reestablish contact.  
5. Rotate PSKs and confirm rekeying across all devices.

<Callout type="warning">
Respect safety, consent, and privacy during drills and live operations.
</Callout>
