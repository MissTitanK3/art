## Overview
Activist and mutual aid operations depend on privacy, reliability, and trust.  
**Proton** provides secure communication, storage, and networking; **CryptPad** adds encrypted real-time collaboration.  

Together they replace Gmail, Drive, and Docs with privacy-first equivalents designed for community defense and organizing.

This course shows how to:
- Set up Proton and CryptPad accounts safely.  
- Use encrypted mail, file storage, and collaborative editing.  
- Replace common Google tools for secure coordination.  
- Integrate both tools with ART workflows without compromising anonymity.

### 🧠 Why Use Proton & CryptPad Over Other Services

<Callout type="info">
Proton and CryptPad form a privacy-first ecosystem for activists, journalists, and mutual aid networks.  
Together, they replace Google’s surveillance infrastructure with encrypted, jurisdiction-safe tools for communication and collaboration.
</Callout>

---

#### 1. Zero-Access Encryption
Both Proton and CryptPad use **end-to-end encryption**—neither company can read your data.  
- Proton encrypts mail, files, and events.  
- CryptPad encrypts documents and spreadsheets in the browser.  
**Why it matters:** Google Docs and Gmail are fully visible to their parent company and often analyzed for behavioral data.

---

#### 2. Swiss and French Privacy Jurisdictions
- **Proton:** Based in Switzerland, outside U.S. and EU surveillance alliances.  
- **CryptPad:** Hosted in France under GDPR, with strict data minimization.  
**Combined Effect:** Any external data requests must go through privacy-respecting courts, adding time and legal scrutiny before access.

---

#### 3. No Ads, No Profiling, No Tracking
Both operate on user-supported models.  
- Proton: Subscription-based, community-supported.  
- CryptPad: Funded by donations and open development.  
**Result:** No incentive to monitor, sell, or algorithmically profile your activity.

---

#### 4. Metadata Minimization
Proton hides subject lines, attachments, and event data.  
CryptPad stores encrypted content only, with **no readable filenames or IP-based analytics.**  
Even if seized, data is mathematically unreadable.  
Contrast this with Google Workspace, which logs every edit, IP, and timestamp.

---

#### 5. Integrated Privacy Ecosystem
Proton and CryptPad together replace nearly all cloud and comms tools with encrypted equivalents that can be hosted or federated for local use.

**Combined Strengths:**
- Proton = Secure communication, storage, and access.  
- CryptPad = Real-time collaboration and editable documents.  
**Effect:** Full privacy chain from message → file → shared edit → archive.

---

#### 6. Open Source and Independently Audited
Both platforms are fully open source:  
- Proton’s code is independently reviewed and public.  
- CryptPad’s encryption model can be self-hosted or audited by anyone.  
**Transparency ensures that no secret access portals or hidden tracking exist.**

---

#### 7. Movement-Aligned Ethics
Proton and CryptPad both support:
- Journalists and whistleblowers.  
- Decentralized organizations.  
- Communities operating under suppression.  
Their focus on user control and anonymity aligns directly with activist principles of **autonomy, transparency, and consent.**

---

#### 8. Resilient During Crackdowns
Neither service depends on ad networks, telemetry, or centralized analytics.  
They remain functional under censorship and throttling, especially when paired with **Signal**, **Tor**, or **PocketServer**.  
**Outcome:** Encrypted communication and collaboration even in restrictive conditions.

---

<Callout type="warning">
⚠️ Encryption protects data, not human error.  
Always verify recipients, avoid public sharing, and use multiple privacy layers: Proton + CryptPad + Signal = secure workflow.
</Callout>

## Learn & Do

✅ **1. Create Your Secure Workspace**
- Create a Proton account with **minimal personal info**.  
- Sign up for **CryptPad** (free tier or self-hosted).  
- Use aliases for role-based coordination:  
  - `region.ops@proton.me`  
  - `dispatch.docs@proton.me`  
- Enable **2FA** and **Kill Switch** on Proton VPN.  
- Bookmark your CryptPad workspace for encrypted editing.

🔍 *Task:* Create both accounts and add them to your secure workflow map.

---

✅ **2. Proton Mail and Calendar**
- Communicate only via Proton or other encrypted platforms.  
- Keep event names abstract: “Meeting 12” instead of “ICE Action Plan.”  
- Set event access to invite-only.  
- Avoid linking real names or addresses inside event notes.

---

✅ **3. Proton Drive and CryptPad Docs**
- Store rosters and handbooks in Proton Drive.  
- Create working drafts in CryptPad Docs or Sheets.  
- Use **Proton Drive links** for static docs and **CryptPad links** for real-time edits.  
- Set link expiration dates and passwords for each share.  
- Avoid Drive sync on unencrypted local devices.

---

✅ **4. Combine with Signal**
- Send CryptPad or Proton Drive links through Signal or Proton Mail only.  
- Keep Signal groups small and trusted.  
- Label encrypted links clearly (e.g., “AAR Draft – Expires in 24h”).

---

✅ **5. Operational Integration**
- Replace Google tools systematically:
  - Mail → Proton Mail  
  - Drive → Proton Drive  
  - Docs → CryptPad  
  - Chat → Signal  
- Review logs weekly to ensure data cleanup and link expiration.
