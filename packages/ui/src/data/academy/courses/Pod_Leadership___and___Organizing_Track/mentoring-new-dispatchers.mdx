Mentoring is about building a culture of clarity, calm, and care. This module prepares trusted Admins and experienced dispatchers to coach new recruits and foster skillful field coordination.

---

## Why Mentorship Matters

Strong dispatch networks aren’t built through software or policy alone — they rely on *people* guiding each other with intention. Mentorship helps:

- Reduce burnout and isolation
- Spot patterns early
- Transfer field wisdom quickly
- Model trusted practices and tone

---

## What Good Mentorship Looks Like

Mentors lead by example. That doesn’t mean perfection — it means being reliable, honest, and steady. You’re setting the tone for how people respond under pressure.

<Callout type="success">
You don’t need to know everything. You need to model how to stay calm and ask smart questions.
</Callout>

---

## Core Mentorship Practices

### 1. **Teach Calm, Not Control**

- Help new dispatchers regulate their emotional responses.
- Normalize mistakes as learning opportunities.
- Reinforce clear language and breathing room.

### 2. **Use Feedback Loops**

- Debrief after major dispatches.
- Ask: What went well? What would we do differently?
- Keep feedback private, unless affirming publicly.

### 3. **Model Sharp Dispatching**

- Narrate your choices out loud when appropriate:  
  > “I’m not activating anyone until I verify this report’s location.”

- Explain when you escalate and why.

### 4. **Document Learning Moments**

- Keep lightweight notes on what trainees have seen or handled.
- Share learning materials or old case studies.

### 5. **Protect While Teaching**

- Never give backend access without certification.
- Let trainees shadow first, then practice with oversight.
- Intervene gently if someone is freezing, panicking, or spiraling.

---

## Common Mentorship Scenarios

- A new dispatcher is too reactive → coach slowing down, double-checking sources
- A trainee misses urgency cues → teach role urgency scales (e.g., “within 30 minutes” vs. “overnight”)
- Someone overuses activation → walk through when *not* to activate

<Callout type="info">
Mentorship is ongoing. Even trusted dispatchers need space to keep growing.
</Callout>

## Documentation Tools

- Use tags in Dispatch logs like `#mentoring`, `#shadowing`, or `#reviewed-by-[yourname]`
- Keep a shared notebook or secure doc for progress check-ins

<Callout type="hint">
Documentation hygiene: Use minimal, time-bound retention. Avoid storing raw chat logs or sensitive notes in shared apps. Prefer encrypted, access-controlled notes and delete temporary files after review. ART does not support in-app mentoring recordkeeping; use secure external tools if needed and always get explicit consent before sharing feedback or progress notes.
</Callout>

---

## Final Thoughts

Mentorship isn’t just instruction — it’s trust in action. The way you teach will shape how future dispatchers respond to danger, handle mistakes, and support others.

<Callout type="success">
Care is contagious. The more we model thoughtful dispatching, the stronger the network becomes.
</Callout>

<Callout type="important">
Consent & legal: Always ask before sharing feedback, document actions only with consent, and hand off notes securely. Pair this course with Documentation Safety, Data Ethics Pledge, Legal Observer, and After‑Action Data Hygiene for best practices.
</Callout>
