# 🌱 How to Create a Pod
<TrackBadge variant="pod-leadership" />

<Callout type="info">
This course is for members building response capacity in areas without an existing ART pod.  
If you’re the one stepping up — you’re a **Pod Creator**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="hint">
Before starting, align with [Community Consent](/courses/community-consent), [Collective Safety Protocols](/courses/collective-safety-protocols), and your region’s [Admin Tools & Access Roles](/courses/admin-tools).  
Clear trust and boundaries at the beginning prevent confusion later.
</Callout>

---

## What Is a Pod?

A **pod** is a small, local, trusted unit of people who can coordinate and respond to ICE, law enforcement, or emergencies. Pods may:

- Hold varying levels of trust and vetting  
- Operate with different risk tolerances  
- Share info and escalate safely  
- Mobilize local support  
- Build relationships of care and safety  

<Callout type="hint">
You don’t need a large team to begin. Two or three trusted people is enough.  
<DownloadFile file="/ICE_Tea_Tools_Pod_Organizer_Handbook.pdf" label="Pod Organizer Handbook" />
</Callout>

---

## Why Pods Matter

- Enable decentralized, local response when official help fails  
- Strengthen mutual aid and neighbor-level resilience  
- Keep operations nimble and harder to disrupt  

<Callout type="important">
Pods are not formal ART branches.  
They’re **autonomous nodes** connected by shared values and protocols.
</Callout>

---

## Who Can Create a Pod?

Anyone who:

- Lives in an uncovered or under-supported area  
- Upholds abolitionist, consent-based principles  
- Completes training and operates with care  

Pod Creators are fast-tracked through **Verified Dispatcher** support — receiving mentorship, trust-building audits, and safety coaching.

## How to Start a Pod

### Step 1: Assess Your Area
- Are there active ICE or police patterns nearby?  
- Which mutual aid orgs already operate here?  
- Is there a trusted community base or venue?  

### Step 2: Find 1–2 Trusted People
Choose people who:
- Understand risk and privacy  
- Have capacity for quick response  
- Are rooted locally (language, geography, culture)

<Callout type="hint">
If you’re solo, still continue — ART may connect you with others nearby.
</Callout>

### Step 3: Set Up Signal
1. Create a **Signal group**  
2. Enable disappearing messages (6–12h recommended)  
3. Turn on admin settings  
4. Share QR only in trusted spaces  
5. Avoid risky names like “ICE Watch” — use neutral terms  

### Step 4: Define Boundaries & Roles
Clarify:
- Who’s on call  
- What qualifies as urgent  
- Tools used (Signal, Proton, maps, etc.)  
- Backup and debrief roles  

Document these agreements briefly — this forms your pod’s baseline.

### Step 5: Join a General Pod (Optional)
Once established:
- Find your region in the [General Pod Directory](/courses/pod-directory)  
- Reach out to share your location and intent  
- Connect with nearby pods for mentorship  

---

## Weaving Into the Network

You don’t register — you **connect**.  
ART’s structure is based on relationships, not databases.

To integrate:
- Reach out to trusted ART members  
- Share your readiness and tools used  
- Ask about signal groups, dispatch integration, or shared drills  
- Keep it informal — **trust moves at the speed of relationship**

<Callout type="info">
If you list your pod, share aliases only — no personal identifiers.  
Review [Documentation Safety](/courses/documentation-safety) and [After-Action Data Hygiene](/courses/after-action-data-hygiene) for secure practices.
</Callout>

---

## Operational Tips

- Use **Proton Mail** or **Riseup** for communications  
- Encrypt docs via **CryptPad** or **Padlock**  
- Assign a **data cleaner** to delete expired notes  
- Summarize reports — never forward screenshots  

<Callout type="hint">
Low-signal or offline?  
Use [App-Free Tactics](/courses/app-free-tactics), [Collective Safety Protocols](/courses/collective-safety-protocols), and [Runners & Rides](/courses/runners-and-rides) for fallback plans.
</Callout>
