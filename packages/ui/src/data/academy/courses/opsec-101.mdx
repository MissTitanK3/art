## 🔍 What Is OPSEC?

**OPSEC = Protecting information your enemies could use against you.**

This includes:
- Plans, locations, timing  
- Identities and affiliations  
- Devices and movement  
- Communications and tech use  

It doesn’t mean paranoia — it means intention. Good OPSEC gives us the confidence to take action without exposing ourselves or our people.

---

## 🧠 The OPSEC 5-Step Framework

| Step | What It Means |
|------|----------------|
| 1️⃣ Identify Critical Info | What would be dangerous if exposed? |
| 2️⃣ Threat Analysis | Who wants that info? What can they do? |
| 3️⃣ Vulnerability Scan | How could that info leak? |
| 4️⃣ Risk vs Countermeasure | What’s the cost/benefit of protection? |
| 5️⃣ Implement & Repeat | Do it. Reflect. Adapt. |

---

## 🛑 Step 1: Identify What Needs Protection

Ask yourself:
- What are we planning or doing that could be targeted?
- Who do we need to protect — individuals, pods, the whole org?
- What would surveillance, infiltration, or doxxing ruin?

Examples:
- Meeting times and locations  
- Member identities and phones  
- Transportation or storage plans  
- Pod structures and tools  

---

## 🕵️ Step 2: Analyze the Threats

Build a **threat model**:
- Who’s watching? (Cops, ICE, local fascists, online trolls)
- What tech do they use? (Geolocation, facial rec, social scraping)
- What do they already know?
- What would they try to discover?

Use public records like:
- 🔍 [EFF: NSA Surveillance Overview](https://www.eff.org/nsa-spying)  
- 🔍 STRATFOR or Standing Rock infiltration leaks  
- 🔍 [Tempora & PRISM mass surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_surveillance_disclosures_(2013%E2%80%93present))  

<Callout type="warning">
**Don't assume you're too small to be watched.** Surveillance often targets the most vulnerable or effective, not the most visible.
</Callout>

---

## ⚠️ Step 3: Identify Vulnerabilities

Once you know what needs protecting and who’s watching, ask:

| Info | Vulnerability | Example |
|------|---------------|---------|
| Location | Cell phone pings | GPS, Wi-Fi, cell towers track you |
| Identity | Social media | Photos, tags, usernames |
| Comms | Apps, unencrypted chats | SMS and many group chats are plaintext |
| Movement | Purchases, transport | Uber, card swipes, traffic cams |

Then: match to a mitigation strategy.

---

## 🧰 Types of OPSEC Measures

| Category | Method | Example |
|----------|--------|---------|
| 🚫 **Action Control** | Avoid the risk entirely | Don’t bring a phone to meetings |
| 🥷 **Countermeasure** | Mask or disrupt data | Use Faraday bags or burner devices |
| 🎭 **Counter Analysis** | Confuse the adversary | Leave decoys, use fake locations |

> Example: Give your phone to a friend headed elsewhere, while you take another route. Anyone watching your signal is misled.

---

## 📉 Step 4: Risk vs Countermeasure

Ask:

- Will this countermeasure **actually work**?
- Does it make my work harder or impossible?
- Is the threat **active**, or just **potential**?
- Is this info **already leaked**?

Sometimes the best choice is:
- Taking a low-risk path with strong safety  
- Or accepting minor risk for high impact (with backup plans)

---

## ✅ Step 5: Apply and Adapt

Now put your plan into action:
- Use burner phones or offline devices  
- Mask movement patterns  
- Keep personal and activist accounts separate  
- Set up Signal groups with no real names  
- Use aliases for roles (not fake IDs — just compartmentalization)

Then:
- Do a **post-action OPSEC review**  
- Note mistakes, oversights, potential leaks  
- Rotate devices, identities, locations as needed  

<Callout type="hint">
Build OPSEC into your **deployment checklist** and pod roles. Make it routine, not reactive.
</Callout>

<Callout type="hint">
Documentation hygiene: Use minimal, time-bound retention. Avoid storing raw chat logs or sensitive OPSEC notes in shared apps. Prefer encrypted, access-controlled notes and delete temporary files after review. ART does not support in-app OPSEC archiving; use secure external tools if needed and always get explicit consent before sharing plans or protocols.
</Callout>

## 🧱 Building Security Culture

This isn’t about just one person being smart — it’s about **everyone** practicing safety:

- Don’t brag about illegal or risky actions  
- Don’t share info you don’t have to  
- Don’t ask others to do unsafe things  
- Don’t post unless it's deliberate and reviewed  
- Don’t shame folks still learning — teach them instead  

Culture is built by repetition, not rules. Model the habits you want others to adopt.

---

## Practice Scenario: Phone Security

🔍 Situation: You’re organizing a direct action and need to communicate while on the move. What’s your OPSEC strategy?

Best practices:
- Set up a **burner Signal group**  
- Only share time and place *at the last moment*  
- Leave your regular phone behind  
- Use radios or in-person relays if feasible  
- Use coded language that’s clear to insiders, meaningless to outsiders

## OPSEC Deployment Checklist

### Before Action:
- [ ] Identify sensitive info  
- [ ] Determine likely threats  
- [ ] Scan for leaks or exposed patterns  
- [ ] Prep burner tools / aliases  

### During:
- [ ] Avoid unnecessary data creation  
- [ ] Stick to need-to-know comms  
- [ ] Track who knows what, and why  
- [ ] Watch for sketchy behavior or digital trails  

### After:
- [ ] Wipe or rotate gear  
- [ ] Debrief and update opsec protocols  
- [ ] Share lessons with trusted pods
