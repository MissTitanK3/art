## 🧭 Overview
Every trusted region holds sensitive data — volunteer records, dispatch logs, and pod rosters.  
This course outlines how to manage that data safely, respecting **community privacy, informed consent, and legal responsibility**.  
The goal: make transparency a source of trust, not risk.

> Data is a tool for coordination, not control.

---

## 🧩 Learn & Do

### 1. Principles of Data Stewardship
- **Minimize collection:** Gather only what is necessary for safety and operations.  
- **Restrict access:** Limit view/edit privileges to active roles (Dispatcher, Admin, Steward).  
- **Encrypt and verify:** Use end-to-end encryption and signed access tokens.  
- **Document purpose:** Every dataset should have a defined use, owner, and expiry.

| Principle | Implementation Example |
|------------|------------------------|
| Transparency | Post a public “Data Charter” on your region’s site |
| Accountability | Keep a changelog for admin actions |
| Right to Erasure | Volunteers may request full deletion of records |
| Least Privilege | No shared admin accounts; use per-user credentials |

---

### 2. Retention & Deletion Policy Design
Build a schedule defining how long data is stored, who reviews it, and how deletion is verified.

| Data Type | Retention | Review Interval | Delete Method |
|------------|------------|----------------|----------------|
| Dispatch records | 30 days after close | Monthly | Secure wipe |
| Volunteer roster | While active | Quarterly | Remove inactive entries |
| Training logs | Until certification expires | Annually | Archive summary only |
| Trust signatures | Until rotation or revocation | Ongoing | Replace with new key |

<Callout type="hint">
Store deletion records as hashes or logs — never the deleted data itself.  
Deletion proofs can be used in audits to show compliance without revealing contents.
</Callout>

---

### 3. Privacy Communication & Consent
Create a simple **Privacy Overview** visible to all volunteers:
1. What data is collected  
2. Why it’s collected  
3. Who can access it  
4. How long it’s stored  
5. How to request deletion or correction

Post this as `/privacy.md` or inside your volunteer onboarding materials.  

<Callout type="info">
Transparency builds consent. Silence or confusion creates distrust and liability.
</Callout>

---

### 4. Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)
Implement **tiered permissions** inside Supabase, ART Admin, or other regional systems.

| Role | Access Level | Example Actions |
|------|---------------|----------------|
| Field Dispatcher | Incident-level only | Read/update active dispatch |
| Pod Lead | Pod roster only | Add/remove volunteers |
| Region Admin | Full logs | Audit and anonymize |
| Data Steward | Meta only | Manage keys and policies |

> No one should hold more access than needed to fulfill their role.

---

### 5. Audit & Integrity Checks
Conduct quarterly reviews:
- Verify all access keys are current.  
- Test RLS (Row-Level Security) rules for correct enforcement.  
- Review audit logs for unusual reads/exports.  
- Confirm offboarding of inactive users.  
- Re-encrypt any dataset older than one year.

<Callout type="warning">
Never skip key rotation.  
Expired or shared tokens are the #1 cause of data leaks in mutual-aid systems.
</Callout>

---

### 6. Handling Data Breaches or Compromise
If exposure occurs:
1. **Contain:** Revoke credentials and rotate keys immediately.  
2. **Assess:** Identify what was accessed and who is affected.  
3. **Notify:** Alert affected users clearly and promptly.  
4. **Remediate:** Delete or replace compromised data.  
5. **Document:** File an internal incident report and review for future prevention.

<Callout type="critical">
Do not hide incidents.  
Transparency and fast mitigation preserve trust more than silence ever will.
</Callout>

---

### 7. Ethical Oversight & Transparency
Create a **Data Stewardship Council** or assign a rotating “Privacy Officer” in your region.  
Responsibilities include:
- Reviewing new forms or tools for privacy compliance.  
- Approving new integrations (e.g., Stripe, Supabase, analytics).  
- Maintaining a plain-language data ethics statement.  
- Training new admins in safe handling and OPSEC.
