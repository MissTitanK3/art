# 🛰 Incident Response for Regional Admins
<TrackBadge variant="admin" />
<TrackBadge variant="security" />
<TrackBadge variant="dispatch" />

<Callout type="info">
When critical incidents occur — arrests, leaks, or digital compromise — regional admins must respond fast, calmly, and by policy. This course provides the decision tree and communication tools to protect people and infrastructure under pressure.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
Regional Admins are not first responders — they are *stability anchors* during chaos.  
Their primary role is to **contain the impact**, **verify facts**, and **preserve operational continuity**.  

Incidents may include:
- Arrests or assaults on volunteers  
- Platform compromise or impersonation  
- Doxxing, leaks, or data exposure  
- Hostile infiltration or sabotage  
- Sudden loss of dispatch or server access  

This course trains Admins to respond ethically, strategically, and with composure.

---

## Learn & Do

### 1. Immediate Response Framework: “CALM”
Every regional crisis begins with noise. Use **CALM** to regain clarity:

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| **C – Contain** | Limit spread of information and panic. Suspend affected systems or accounts. |
| **A – Assess** | Identify what’s actually happening and who’s affected. |
| **L – Log** | Begin timestamped notes (who, what, when, where). Use no personal names publicly. |
| **M – Mobilize** | Activate the proper support layer — legal, tech, safety, or dispatch. |

> Your goal is not to fix the problem immediately, but to **prevent it from escalating**.

---

### 2. Incident Classification Levels
Use shared language to calibrate your response:

| Level | Description | Action |
|-------|--------------|--------|
| 🟢 **Low** | Localized issue (minor tech error, false report) | Resolve at pod level, log summary. |
| 🟡 **Moderate** | Volunteer incident, isolated data issue | Notify region lead; assess legal implications. |
| 🔴 **Critical** | Arrest, injury, major data breach, public exposure | Activate full regional response plan. |
| ⚫ **Black** | Active infiltration, state interference, or platform seizure | Immediate lockdown and cross-zone coordination. |

---

### 3. Verification and Communication Discipline
Facts before broadcast:
- Double-source every report before informing others.  
- Separate **confirmed** vs **rumor** in all logs.  
- Use *internal Signal channels* or *LoRa fallback* only.  
- Assign one **designated public contact** — others remain silent.  

<Callout type="warning">
Never post incident updates to public channels until verified.  
Speculation, even well-meaning, endangers others.
</Callout>

---

### 4. Data and System Containment
If data compromise is suspected:
1. Revoke keys or tokens in Supabase or server dashboard.  
2. Rotate admin passwords using secure vault or offline method.  
3. Export minimal anonymized logs for forensic review.  
4. Wipe or quarantine affected devices.  
5. File a regional incident ticket tagged **“DATA-BREACH”** for central follow-up.

All evidence should be preserved securely and shared only with **trusted reviewers**.

---

### 5. Arrest or Violence Response Coordination
When a volunteer is detained or harmed:
- Verify name, location, and time.  
- Activate **Jail Support & Legal Logistics** protocol.  
- Assign a liaison to coordinate family or legal contact.  
- Record only verifiable facts (no speculation).  
- Maintain one **regional thread** to avoid confusion.

If multiple arrests occur, create a **running status board** in encrypted local storage (no cloud sync).

---

### 6. Media and External Communication
If approached by journalists or hostile sources:
- Do **not** confirm or deny ongoing operations.  
- Acknowledge general safety concern and redirect:  
  > “We’re ensuring all volunteers are accounted for and safe. We’ll share updates when verified.”  
- Route all follow-ups through the regional media liaison.

> Every public statement is a legal document. Treat it that way.

---

### 7. Cross-Region Escalation
If a crisis crosses regional boundaries:
1. Notify adjacent Zone Leads via **trusted Signal thread**.  
2. Share *facts only*: time, nature, impact, immediate needs.  
3. Use **common threat tags**: `LEAK`, `ARREST`, `INFIL`, `SYSTEM`.  
4. Rotate leads every 12 hours if the response exceeds one day.

Maintain internal transparency but limit audience size.

---

### 8. Volunteer Safety and Emotional Containment
Admin response includes emotional care:
- Offer 1-on-1 check-ins for directly impacted volunteers.  
- Pair with **Peer Support & Check-In Circles** or **Crisis Debrief & Care** courses.  
- Delay formal accountability until after stabilization.  
- Watch for volunteer isolation or hyperactivity — both are stress signs.

<Callout type="info">
Psychological stabilization is part of operational recovery.
</Callout>

---

### 9. Legal & Documentation Protocols
- Maintain a **Regional Incident Log** (timestamp, type, level, resolution).  
- Encrypt and store for 90 days, then rotate or delete.  
- Document all key rotations, bans, or structural changes.  
- Share anonymized summaries with the **Trust Network** for pattern analysis.

Never retain unredacted personal data longer than necessary.

---

### 10. Post-Incident Review & Recovery
After containment:
1. Hold a *debrief within 72 hours*.  
2. Review what systems failed and why.  
3. Rotate keys, update policies, and notify affected pods.  
4. Publish an **Internal Safety Memo** summarizing lessons learned (no names).  
5. Archive with “Resolved” status and timestamp.

> Recovery is not just cleanup — it’s rebuilding trust.
