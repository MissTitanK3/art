# 📹 Documentation & Safety
<TrackBadge variant="observation-legal" />
<TrackBadge variant="direct-action" />

Recording what you see can protect your community — but only if done with care.  
This course covers how to document state or ICE presence responsibly and safely.

<Callout type="info">
Documentation isn’t just proof. It’s **protection**. Over 60% of deportation defenses rely on community footage.
</Callout>

<Callout type="hint">
Before filming, review [Know Your Rights (KYR) Basics](/courses/kyr-basics), [Bystander Support](/courses/bystander-support), and the [Data Ethics Pledge](/courses/data-ethics-pledge).
</Callout>

---

## Why Documentation Matters

Recording helps:
- Create accountability  
- Deter misconduct  
- Verify witness reports  
- Support court or media evidence  
- Educate and alert the community  

But careless recording can expose those same people to risk. Documentation is ethical work, not just technical.

---

## When to Record

Record when you see:
- ICE or law enforcement stops  
- Harassment or detention  
- Power abuse or unlawful force  
- Urgent verification for Dispatch  

Do **not** record:
- If consent isn’t possible and risk is high  
- Inside private property  
- When vulnerable individuals ask not to  

<Callout type="info">
Language access is part of consent. Use interpreters or ASL support when possible.  
See [Translator Micro-badge](/courses/translator-micro-badge) and [ASL Interpreter Micro-badge](/courses/asl-interpreter-micro-badge).
</Callout>

---

## Consent & Ethics

**Scripts for consent:**
- “Is it okay if I record this for legal safety?”  
- Non-verbal signals like nods or raised phones can also indicate consent.  

<Callout type="warning">
Never post raw footage online.  
Blur faces of undocumented, trans, or otherwise targeted people.
</Callout>

---

## Technical Tips

- Narrate: “July 6, 3:12 PM, ICE at Main & Broadway, Hillsboro.”  
- Use **landscape mode**  
- Hold steady; avoid zoom  
- Capture ID and vehicle plates if safe  
- Keep background clear and audio steady  

---

## When to Stop (or Not)

Stop if:
- Officers aim weapons or approach you  
- You’re outnumbered or alone  
- Filming escalates danger  

Keep filming if:
- Someone is being detained or harmed  
- You are their only witness  
- Visibility deters violence  

<Callout type="info">
If filming causes distress but prevents harm, hold eye contact and signal solidarity.
</Callout>

---

## Staying Safe

- Stay back; film from distance  
- Lock screen with duress PIN  
- Disable biometric unlock  
- Airplane mode during protests  
- Avoid livestreams unless visibility = safety  

---

## Secure Saving & Uploading

- Use **Signal** or **Keybase** for encrypted uploads  
- Rename files descriptively:  
  `ICE-Hillsboro-July6-3pm.mp4`  
- Strip metadata with **ObscuraCam** or **ExifCleaner**  
- Keep retention short → see [After-Action Data Hygiene](/courses/after-action-data-hygiene)

<Callout type="info">
Dispatch supports image attachments. Share video via secure link instead.
</Callout>

---

## Supporting a Filmer

If you’re not filming:
- Create physical space  
- Watch their back  
- Narrate time and place  
- Help blur or upload safely  

---

## What Not to Do

- Don’t film children, trans, or undocumented people **without consent**, unless they are under direct threat  
- Don’t share identifiers like names, plates, or faces  
- Don’t edit footage to change context  
- Don’t use public social media as storage  

<Callout type="warning">
Livestream only when necessary for immediate protection.  
Avoid vanity or performative filming — it can expose others.
</Callout>

---

## Real Scenarios

**Courthouse Pickup**
1. Step back for safety  
2. Record quietly  
3. Narrate time, date, place  
4. Upload securely  
5. Submit via Community Watch  

**Protest Example**
- Film from wide angle  
- Narrate: “5:30 PM, police advancing with batons at Main & Pine.”  
- Avoid close faces  
- Blur before external sharing
