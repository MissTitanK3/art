# 🧩 Social Worker: Crisis Care & Resource Navigation
<TrackBadge variant="specialized-role" />

<Callout type="info">
Social Workers turn crisis into connection. They help people regain stability, access essential services, and rebuild trust after stressful or traumatic events.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
When individuals experience displacement, arrest, or trauma, they often need **immediate care and long-term follow-up**.  
**Social Workers** serve as the bridge between crisis response and sustainable recovery. They connect people to food, housing, healthcare, and emotional support systems—without judgment or bureaucracy.  

This course teaches you to:
- Conduct safe, trauma-informed crisis assessments.  
- Navigate local support systems effectively.  
- Coordinate with medical, legal, and mental health partners.  
- Maintain boundaries and ethical documentation in activist settings.

---

## Learn & Do

✅ **1. Ground your role in trust and care**
- You are the **stabilizer** after crisis moments.  
- Approach every interaction with calm tone and visible compassion.  
- Prioritize **consent and autonomy**—offer options, not orders.  
- Understand cultural and situational sensitivity—avoid assumptions about background or capacity.  
- Protect confidentiality as you would in clinical settings.

🔍 *Task:* Write your personal crisis care principles in three short sentences—what people can expect from you every time.

---

✅ **2. Conduct initial crisis assessments**
- Quickly identify **safety needs** first: shelter, food, medication, emotional regulation.  
- Ask short, clear questions:
  - “Do you feel safe right now?”  
  - “Do you have somewhere you can rest tonight?”  
- Watch for red flags:
  - Disorientation or panic attacks.  
  - Physical signs of dehydration or injury.  
  - Verbal indications of hopelessness or harm.  
- Prioritize what can be stabilized *immediately* before referring elsewhere.

🧩 *Example:* After a rally, a participant shows panic symptoms. You move them to a quiet area, offer water, and guide slow breathing before connecting them to Mental Health Support.

✅ **3. Navigate support systems efficiently**
- Maintain an updated **local resource directory**:
  - Shelters and housing programs.  
  - Food banks and mutual aid kitchens.  
  - Free or sliding-scale clinics.  
  - Legal aid and immigration support.  
- Verify resources regularly—many programs change hours or funding.  
- Build relationships with case managers and front-line staff to speed referrals.  
- Accompany individuals through forms or phone calls if capacity allows.

⚙️ *Tip:* Call ahead before sending someone to a service—reduces retraumatization and wasted trips.

---

✅ **4. Provide trauma-informed care**
- Speak slowly and predictably.  
- Always ask permission before physical contact.  
- Explain what’s happening before taking any step.  
- Avoid retraumatizing questions or forcing story repetition.  
- Normalize responses like numbness, crying, or silence.  
- Reassure safety through consistency and follow-through.

<Callout type="warning">
⚠️ Never promise outcomes—promise process.  
Say “We’ll figure this out together,” not “I’ll fix this.”
</Callout>

---

✅ **5. Coordinate across roles**
- Work with:
  - **Medics** for physical stabilization.  
  - **Mental Health** for emotional regulation.  
  - **Dispatch** for transportation or volunteer tracking.  
  - **Legal Support** for post-arrest care.  
- Maintain clean, factual logs (no opinions).  
- Use initials or coded IDs if data might be sensitive.  
- Debrief cross-team after high-intensity operations.

🧩 *Example:* You support a family after an eviction raid by connecting them to a housing nonprofit, providing hygiene kits, and scheduling mental health follow-up within 24 hours.

---

✅ **6. Maintain ethical and emotional boundaries**
- Don’t overpromise or absorb responsibility for systems beyond your control.  
- Share emotional load with team peers—no one should carry cases alone.  
- Use reflective supervision or group debriefs when possible.  
- Respect that not everyone wants help immediately—allow agency.  
- Keep your own support structure—hydration, rest, and grounding.

🔍 *Task:* Write one line you can use when someone refuses help while staying open for future connection (e.g., “If you change your mind, we’ll still be here.”)

---

✅ **7. Ensure continuity and follow-up**
- Confirm next points of contact before leaving a situation.  
- Schedule check-ins within 48–72 hours for complex cases.  
- Log outcomes: stabilized, referred, ongoing, or closed.  
- Update databases or share anonymized summaries with the coordination hub.  
- Follow through—trust is built when help continues beyond first contact.
