# 🗺️ Dispatch Assistant: Coordination, Tracking & Communication Support
<TrackBadge variant="specialized-role" />

<Callout type="info">
Dispatch Assistants are the operational backbone of coordination. They track who’s where, what’s happening, and keep every moving part connected—quietly ensuring missions stay safe and synchronized.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
In any operation, information flow can decide success or chaos. **Dispatch Assistants** maintain that flow. They help dispatchers manage calls, log reports, and monitor team statuses.  

This role suits detail-oriented individuals who can stay calm amid rapid updates. Dispatch Assistants ensure that **field reports, logistics, and alerts** reach the right people at the right time—without data loss or confusion.

You’ll learn how to:
- Track live operations and volunteer statuses.  
- Relay verified updates across comms channels.  
- Document activity for After-Action Reviews (AARs).  
- Operate within dispatch systems and privacy protocols.

---

## Learn & Do

✅ **1. Understand your function within dispatch**
- You are **support, not secondary**—you maintain the dispatcher’s bandwidth.  
- Handle updates, confirmations, and data entry so the dispatcher can focus on decision-making.  
- Maintain calm tone and structured process under stress.  
- Know the chain of communication: who can send, approve, or act on updates.

🔍 *Task:* Create a simple communication map for your team—who reports to whom, and through which channel (Signal, mesh, radio, etc.).

---

✅ **2. Master communication clarity**
- Speak or type concisely.  
- Use consistent phrasing:
  - “Team Alpha → at location → status GREEN.”  
  - “Report confirmed → via photo → timestamp 18:22.”  
- Avoid filler or opinion—stick to facts.  
- If unclear, request confirmation: “Confirm—two volunteers en route to CDC Tacoma?”  

🧩 *Example:* Dispatcher handles strategy while you transcribe real-time location reports from three pods and update the roster dashboard.

✅ **3. Track team and asset statuses**
- Maintain live roster or map view:  
  - Active teams, locations, and roles.  
  - Supply drops or completed deliveries.  
  - Incident logs and follow-ups.  
- Use **color codes or status flags**:
  - 🟢 Active / Safe  
  - 🟡 Awaiting confirmation  
  - 🔴 Urgent / Needs support  
- Timestamp every entry.  
- Cross-verify updates from multiple sources before marking complete.

⚙️ *Tip:* Double-check everything—one incorrect status can trigger wrong deployment or missed rescue.

---

✅ **4. Coordinate logistical communication**
- Track movement of vehicles, supplies, or medics.  
- Confirm when requests are fulfilled.  
- Forward verified resource needs to Logistics or Admin teams.  
- Relay updates between teams who can’t reach each other directly.  
- Summarize ongoing ops for dispatcher every 15–30 minutes or at phase changes.

🧩 *Example:* “Medic 2 delivered kit to 12th & G. Supply truck ETA 10 mins. Team Hilltop off comms 6 minutes.”

---

✅ **5. Use tools effectively**
Familiarize yourself with:
- **Dispatch dashboards or ART interface** (status fields, filters, timestamps).  
- **Signal, Zello, or radio protocols**—push-to-talk discipline, brevity codes.  
- **Offline fallback methods**: paper logs, backup radios, or mesh sync.  
- Encryption and privacy basics—never store sensitive data in unsecured apps.

<Callout type="warning">
⚠️ Do not forward sensitive details (names, license plates, locations) to public or unverified channels.  
Data control equals safety.
</Callout>

---

✅ **6. Support after-action work**
- Archive logs, timestamps, and messages securely.  
- Flag patterns: communication delays, redundant calls, or recurring gaps.  
- Contribute to the After-Action Review (AAR) by summarizing:
  - What went right (accuracy, speed).  
  - What caused confusion or duplication.  
  - How to improve coordination next time.

🔍 *Task:* After your next simulation or event, write a three-sentence summary capturing clarity, timing, and coordination quality.

---

✅ **7. Self-regulate under pressure**
- Keep breathing steady; prioritize clarity over speed.  
- Take 2–3 second pauses before confirming critical details.  
- Avoid multitasking beyond your limit—delegate or batch if needed.  
- Rotate shifts to prevent fatigue or tunnel vision.  
- Log emotional load in debrief—information stress is real.
