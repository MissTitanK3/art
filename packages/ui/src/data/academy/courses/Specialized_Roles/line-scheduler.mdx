# ⏰ Line Scheduler: Early Arrival & Queue Rotation Coordination
<TrackBadge variant="specialized-role" />

<Callout type="info">
Line Schedulers keep courthouse access fair and organized. They arrive early, manage rotations, and ensure that community members don’t lose their place while maintaining calm order under pressure.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
In high-demand hearings—especially solidarity cases or major community trials—seats and entry spaces can fill up fast. **Line Schedulers** ensure that everyone has equitable access to court observation without burnout, confusion, or conflict.  

This role blends **logistics, communication, and trust**. The scheduler manages rotations for supporters waiting in line, verifies identities or wristbands if used, and relays updates to Dispatch or Court Support teams.

You’ll learn to:
- Coordinate early arrivals and manage sign-in lists.  
- Organize rotation schedules fairly.  
- Communicate real-time changes clearly.  
- Handle courthouse staff and public interactions diplomatically.

---

## Learn & Do

✅ **1. Prepare before the crowd forms**
- Arrive early—ideally **30–60 minutes before doors open.**  
- Coordinate with **Court Support Lead** or **Legal Liaison** to confirm:
  - Start time for proceedings.  
  - Expected attendance size.  
  - Rotation plan (time limits or seat-sharing schedule).  
- Bring:
  - Clipboard or digital check-in list.  
  - Pens, tape, and clear signage.  
  - Charged phone or radio for updates.  
  - Water and small supplies for early arrivers.

🔍 *Task:* Build a simple check-in template with columns for name (or alias), arrival time, and assigned rotation slot.

---

✅ **2. Manage queue flow calmly**
- Greet people as they arrive and explain the rotation plan.  
- Use friendly authority:
  - “We’re making sure everyone gets a turn inside. You’ll be in Group A.”  
- Keep line spacing organized—avoid clustering near courthouse doors.  
- Use markers (chalk, cones, or signs) for visual clarity.  
- Check in periodically with courthouse security to prevent confusion.  

🧩 *Example:* You maintain a 20-person rotation list for a sentencing hearing. As attendees exit, you guide the next group in smoothly, avoiding overlap or crowding.

✅ **3. Communicate updates efficiently**
- Send status reports every 15–30 minutes to:
  - **Dispatch** (for overall coordination).  
  - **Court Support** (for attendance tracking).  
- Keep updates brief:
  - “Line at 32 people, 10 inside, Group C entering.”  
  - “Security requested 5-minute pause for crowding.”  
- Use group Signal chats or radio channels when available.  
- Avoid posting times or details publicly—protect privacy and predictability.

⚙️ *Tip:* Assign a backup scheduler for long days to prevent fatigue or bias in rotation calls.

---

✅ **4. Handle conflicts and stress**
- Stay neutral, even when tensions rise.  
- Use calm, factual language:
  - “I understand, but we’re keeping to the rotation for fairness.”  
  - “You’ll be next in line after Group D.”  
- Never raise your voice or make promises outside your authority.  
- If disputes escalate, signal **Court Support** or **Security Witness** for assistance.  

<Callout type="warning">
⚠️ Always assume someone nearby may record interactions.  
Maintain transparency, neutrality, and composure at all times.
</Callout>

---

✅ **5. Track attendance and transitions**
- Keep notes on who entered, when, and who replaced them.  
- Record any incidents or changes (delays, denied entry, closures).  
- Share summary with Dispatch or Court Support after proceedings.  
- Mark no-shows or duplicates for review.  
- Store sign-in sheets securely—never post online or share publicly.

🧩 *Example:* After the hearing, you hand off your attendance sheet to the Legal Liaison for court log verification.

---

✅ **6. Wrap up and debrief**
- Once the line clears, check with courthouse staff that all attendees exited safely.  
- Confirm attendance totals for record-keeping.  
- Return signage and supplies.  
- Debrief with Court Support to refine rotation protocols for next time.
