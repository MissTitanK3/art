# 💜 Survivor Escort: Courthouse Navigation & Trauma-Informed Presence
<TrackBadge variant="specialized-role" />

<Callout type="info">
Survivor Escorts walk beside—not ahead of—those navigating legal systems. They help survivors or witnesses move through courthouse spaces safely, calmly, and with their dignity intact.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
Courthouse environments can retraumatize survivors through surveillance, bureaucracy, or proximity to abusers. **Survivor Escorts** offer steady, trauma-informed companionship that centers consent and emotional safety.  

This role is not advocacy or legal representation—it is **emotional and logistical support** through a process often designed without survivor comfort in mind.  

You will learn to:
- Navigate courthouse logistics while protecting emotional boundaries.  
- Support trauma recovery through calm, grounded presence.  
- Communicate clearly with court personnel and legal teams.  
- Know when and how to step back or hand off to mental health or legal professionals.

---

## Learn & Do

✅ **1. Prepare with clarity and care**
- Review day’s logistics: time, courtroom, contact person, and exit routes.  
- Coordinate with **Court Support**, **Legal Liaison**, or **Mental Health** if part of a team.  
- Pack essentials:
  - Water, tissues, mints, and quiet grounding items.  
  - A small notebook (no names or personal details).  
  - Charger, spare mask, and optional comfort item for survivor.  
- Center yourself before arrival—steady breath, neutral tone, grounded posture.

🔍 *Task:* Before any escort, take 60 seconds of silence to release your expectations and focus fully on presence.

---

✅ **2. Establish consent and emotional boundaries**
- Begin by asking:
  - “What would help you feel supported today?”  
  - “Would you like me to speak for you with staff, or just stay nearby?”  
- Explain your role clearly: you’re there for support, not legal guidance.  
- Respect all boundaries—some survivors prefer quiet proximity, others need conversation.  
- Never touch or lead without explicit consent.

🧩 *Example:* Before entering security screening, you say, “Would you like me to walk next to you, or a few steps behind?” letting them choose pacing and proximity.

✅ **3. Navigate courthouse environments calmly**
- Know your physical routes:
  - Entrances and exits  
  - Bathrooms, elevators, quiet corners  
  - Private waiting rooms if available  
- Walk beside survivor; match their pace, not your schedule.  
- Manage interactions with security or staff respectfully:
  - “We’re here for a scheduled appearance in courtroom 304.”  
  - If challenged, stay neutral and calm; never escalate.  
- Offer sensory grounding breaks between stages (before/after testimony, waiting periods).

⚙️ *Tip:* Keep phone on silent; courthouse noise alone can heighten stress.

---

✅ **4. Maintain emotional regulation under pressure**
- When emotions rise, stay still and slow:
  - Lower your voice instead of raising it.  
  - Remind survivor gently they’re not alone.  
- Normalize reactions:
  - “It’s okay to take a moment.”  
  - “You don’t have to hold it together for anyone.”  
- Avoid phrases that rush or minimize feelings:
  - ❌ “You’re strong, you’ll get through this.”  
  - ✅ “You have the right to feel what you feel.”  

<Callout type="warning">
⚠️ Courthouse trauma is cumulative. Your calm presence protects not just one person—but everyone watching that care modeled.
</Callout>

---

✅ **5. Communicate discreetly with legal teams**
- Coordinate updates only when survivor consents:
  - “Is it okay if I let the attorney know you’ve arrived?”  
- During hearings, stay in visual range if allowed.  
- If survivor exits early, notify **Court Support** or **Legal Liaison** quietly.  
- Never disclose personal details or private conversations.  
- After proceedings, escort them safely to transportation or care station.

🧩 *Example:* After testimony, you walk survivor to the elevator, ask if they’d like to debrief, then signal Court Float for follow-up care coverage.

---

✅ **6. Manage post-court decompression**
- Offer grounding activities before parting:
  - Deep breath together, walk to fresh air, or sit in silence.  
- If survivor appears dissociated or panicked:
  - Ask permission to stay nearby.  
  - Notify **Mental Health** or **Care Station Coordinator**.  
- Encourage rest and hydration.  
- After parting, write brief factual notes for coordination (no identifiers).

🔍 *Task:* Create a personal grounding practice to release residual stress after emotionally charged escorts.

---

✅ **7. Reflect, debrief, and rest**
- Emotionally heavy work requires decompression.  
- Meet with peer support or Mental Health team for debrief.  
- Note environmental barriers or improvements (e.g., signage, safe waiting areas).  
- Acknowledge successes: presence itself is a form of justice.  
- Take time off between high-intensity days.
