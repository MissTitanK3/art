# 🕵️ Arrest Tracker: Detention & Jail Support Coordination
<TrackBadge variant="specialized-role" />

<Callout type="info">
Arrest Trackers ensure no one disappears into the system. Their role is to identify who’s been detained, record key details quickly, and hand that information to legal or jail support without compromising safety.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
During actions, chaos can make it hard to know who’s been detained or where they were taken. Arrest Trackers protect participants by documenting arrests in real time and keeping that data secure for legal and jail support teams.  

This course covers:
- How to identify and confirm arrests safely.  
- What details to record and what to avoid.  
- Communication with Legal Support, Jail Support, and Dispatch.  
- Handling sensitive information under pressure.  

Arrest Trackers are the **information lifeline** for those in custody and their communities.

---

## Learn & Do

✅ **1. Understand your role**
- You are the **bridge between the field and legal aid**.  
- Your goal: collect accurate, minimal identifying information and verify it through reliable witnesses or friends.  
- You do **not intervene or film arrests** up close.  
- Instead, you track what happened, to whom, where, and when.

🔍 *Task:* Write a one-sentence summary of your job that you could explain to another volunteer in the field.

---

✅ **2. Observe and identify detentions**
- Watch for:  
  - Individuals being separated from crowds or placed in custody.  
  - Officers leading someone away or placing them in a vehicle.  
  - Group detentions (kettling, line arrests, or crowd sweeps).  
- Take note of:  
  - Time, location, and number of people detained.  
  - Vehicle type, license plate, or transport direction if visible.  
  - Uniform type or agency marking (e.g., “Tacoma PD,” “Sheriff”).

---

✅ **3. Collect essential information safely**
Record **only what’s needed** for jail support to locate the person:
- Full name (if confirmed).  
- Clothing description.  
- Time and place of detention.  
- Apparent condition (injured, calm, resisting).  
- Witness contact if available.  
- Vehicle or officer identifiers.

⚠️ Do **not** post or transmit over open channels. Use encrypted Signal or hand-deliver to dispatch or legal team.

🔍 *Task:* Build a “Field Arrest Log” template with columns for time, location, name/alias, description, witness, and notes.

---

✅ **4. Communicate quickly and clearly**
- Use short, factual language.  
- Avoid speculation or emotional commentary.  
- Report directly to **Legal Support or Jail Support** via secure channel.  
- If you lose connection, use mesh radio, or hold until back in contact range.  

🧩 *Example message:*  
> “3 confirmed arrests, Broadway & 12th, Tacoma PD van, left southbound, no visible injuries, all appeared calm.”

---

✅ **5. Manage sensitive data**
- Arrest data is **protected information**. Treat it as confidential.  
- Never store on unlocked devices or share through group chats.  
- Encrypt logs or hand them directly to a verified Legal or Jail Support contact.  
- Delete field notes after secure transfer is confirmed.  

<Callout type="warning">
⚠️ Mishandling personal or arrest data can endanger detainees and volunteers.  
Always verify who you’re sending information to.
</Callout>

---

✅ **6. Work as part of a team**
- Operate in pairs for safety and accuracy.  
- Designate one person for observation, one for note-taking.  
- Coordinate with Legal Support for confirmation of detainee transport.  
- Sync with Jail Support for updates and release times.  

---

✅ **7. After-action follow-up**
- Compare your field logs with Jail Support and Legal reports.  
- Note any discrepancies or unverified detentions.  
- File a short After-Action summary:
  - Number of arrests confirmed  
  - Notable agency behavior  
  - Communication issues or breakdowns  
- Debrief with your partner to review what worked and what to improve.
