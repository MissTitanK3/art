# 🛰️ Tech Jammer: Counter-Surveillance & Digital Protection
<TrackBadge variant="specialized-role" />

<Callout type="info">
Tech Jammers defend privacy in real time. They detect, mitigate, and neutralize surveillance attempts—using knowledge, not chaos—to keep communities safe from digital tracking and interception.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
**Tech Jammers** specialize in counter-surveillance: identifying and interrupting hostile tracking or monitoring systems that threaten operational security.  
Their goal is not to “fight technology” but to **create safe space through signal hygiene, hardware control, and awareness**.  

This work demands radical ethics, discretion, and a deep understanding of the line between lawful defense and unlawful interference.

You will learn to:
- Detect surveillance attempts and digital intrusion.  
- Apply countermeasures legally and ethically.  
- Advise teams on secure communications setup.  
- Respond quickly and quietly to threats in the field.

---

## Learn & Do

✅ **1. Define your ethical boundaries**
- You defend **privacy**, not attack infrastructure.  
- Work only within **protective and preventative** actions:
  - Signal shielding, deactivation, safe disconnection.  
  - Radio discipline and mesh routing management.  
  - Awareness training for device hygiene.  
- Never jam emergency frequencies or interfere with public safety systems.  
- Maintain written documentation of ethical compliance for accountability.

🔍 *Task:* Write your own “Rules of Engagement” for counter-surveillance that keep your methods legal and transparent to your team.

---

✅ **2. Detect and assess surveillance**
- Watch for signs of active monitoring:
  - New unknown Wi-Fi networks nearby.  
  - Sudden battery drain or overheating on devices.  
  - Repeated unusual radio static or mirrored frequencies.  
  - Drones, plain vehicles with antenna clusters, or repeated camera presence.  
- Confirm patterns before acting—false positives are common.  
- When confirmed, isolate the vector (digital, visual, physical).

🧩 *Example:* During a rally, your mesh devices begin dropping connections in sequence. You identify a rogue access point and switch to encrypted peer-to-peer fallback.

✅ **3. Apply safe countermeasures**
- Use **passive interference and mitigation**, not active broadcast jamming:
  - Disable unnecessary radios or Bluetooth.  
  - Activate mesh encryption layers.  
  - Use Faraday pouches for sensitive gear.  
  - Rotate device identifiers and IP addresses.  
  - Relocate sensitive conversations to shielded areas.  
- Report suspected trackers discreetly to Dispatch or Admin teams for documentation and pattern tracking.

⚙️ *Tip:* The most effective “jammer” is proper **signal discipline**—silence, encryption, and mobility.

---

✅ **4. Support teams with secure setup**
- Before deployments:
  - Audit device permissions.  
  - Remove nonessential apps or connections.  
  - Preload offline maps and encrypted messengers.  
  - Sync contact keys manually, not via cloud.  
  - Test comms under low-signal or airplane-mode conditions.  
- During actions:
  - Rotate networks and signal bands.  
  - Avoid centralized channels for sensitive coordination.  

<Callout type="warning">
⚠️ Never run unverified tools or scripts on shared devices.  
Malware disguised as “privacy tools” is a common infiltration method.
</Callout>

---

✅ **5. Coordinate response to digital compromise**
- If a device or channel is suspected compromised:
  - Immediately isolate it (power off, remove SIM).  
  - Notify Dispatch or Admin tech leads.  
  - Secure alternative communications route (radio, LoRa, in-person courier).  
  - Wipe compromised devices only after data is safely preserved for analysis.  
- Document incident details:
  - Device type, symptoms, time, location, actions taken.  
  - Maintain log in secure encrypted storage.  

🧩 *Example:* You receive word that a volunteer’s phone may be cloned. You guide them to cut network connections, remove the SIM, and switch to mesh-only comms while you verify.

---

✅ **6. Practice discretion and debrief**
- Never boast about interference or tech actions.  
- Avoid jargon or “spy language”—it raises suspicion and alienates trust.  
- Keep countermeasures compartmentalized: only those who need to know, know.  
- Debrief after events:
  - What signals were seen?  
  - What countermeasures worked?  
  - Were there false positives?  
- Update training docs and briefings accordingly.

🔍 *Task:* After a simulated event, write a short incident summary highlighting one success and one improvement area.

---

✅ **7. Maintain readiness and resilience**
- Keep a **“signal kit”**: Faraday sleeves, offline laptop, SDR scanner, and encrypted drives.  
- Update software and firmware monthly.  
- Study legal updates around communications privacy in your jurisdiction.  
- Train peers to detect early signs of surveillance so you’re not the only line of defense.
