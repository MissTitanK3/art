# 💬 Mental Health Support: Emotional Care & Trauma Recovery
<TrackBadge variant="specialized-role" />

<Callout type="info">
Mental Health Support volunteers hold space for others in distress. Their work protects the invisible layer of safety—helping participants process fear, grief, or shock so they can return to stability and community connection.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
Actions, disasters, and high-stress mutual aid work leave emotional imprints that can last far beyond the moment. **Mental Health Support volunteers** serve as peers, listeners, and stabilizers during and after those experiences.  

Their purpose is not to “fix” anyone—it’s to **witness, ground, and guide toward recovery resources**.  

You’ll learn how to:
- Recognize emotional and trauma responses.  
- Offer immediate psychological first aid.  
- Support safe emotional release and grounding.  
- Debrief individuals or teams after high-stress events.

---

## Learn & Do

✅ **1. Understand your scope**
- You are **not** a therapist unless formally licensed.  
- You **are** a grounding presence who can normalize reactions, reduce isolation, and connect people to further care.  
- Maintain confidentiality except when safety or consent is at risk.  
- Always work in pairs or teams when possible—supporters also need support.

🔍 *Task:* Write three sentences that define your personal scope: what you will do, what you won’t, and when you’ll refer to a professional.

---

✅ **2. Recognize trauma and stress responses**
Common signs:
- Shaking, crying, or emotional shutdown.  
- Hypervigilance or startle response.  
- Rapid breathing, blank stare, or confusion.  
- Sudden anger or withdrawal.  
- Physical distress without clear injury (stomach pain, dizziness).

🧩 *Example:* After an arrest event, a volunteer can’t stop pacing and repeating details. You listen calmly, encourage deep breathing, and help them name sensations (“Your feet are on the ground. You’re safe right now.”).

---

✅ **3. Offer psychological first aid**
Use the **LOOK – LISTEN – LINK** model:
- **LOOK:** Observe safety, needs, and visible distress.  
- **LISTEN:** Allow emotional expression without judgment.  
- **LINK:** Connect to next steps—water, medics, quiet space, trusted contact.  

Core steps:
1. Introduce yourself, ask permission to talk.  
2. Ground the person in the present moment.  
3. Validate feelings without trying to fix them.  
4. Encourage slow breathing and hydration.  
5. Stay until someone else can take over.

⚙️ *Tip:* Your calm tone helps regulate others. Speak softly, use few words.

---

✅ **4. Grounding and stabilization techniques**
- **5-4-3-2-1 Method:** name 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste.  
- Encourage physical grounding—feet on floor, hand on object, deep exhale.  
- Offer tactile items (stone, fabric, water bottle) to anchor the senses.  
- Avoid forced eye contact or touch—always ask consent first.  
- Use simple phrases:  
  - “You’re safe right now.”  
  - “We can slow down together.”  

🔍 *Task:* Practice guiding a friend through 5-4-3-2-1 grounding after mild stress.

---

✅ **5. Facilitate team or group debriefs**
- Gather participants after incidents in a quiet area.  
- Allow short check-ins: “What did you see, what stuck with you, how are you now?”  
- Avoid graphic detail—focus on feelings and lessons.  
- End with an action for closure: deep breath, group shake-off, gratitude.  

🧩 *Example:* After a night shift, the team meets for 10 minutes. Each person shares one word describing their state. They end by naming one thing that went well.

---

✅ **6. Manage your own emotional load**
- Monitor signs of **compassion fatigue**: irritability, numbness, exhaustion.  
- Set emotional boundaries—some stories will stay with you; acknowledge them, then release.  
- Debrief with another supporter after every major incident.  
- Maintain hydration, food, and rest—self-neglect undermines your care work.

<Callout type="warning">
⚠️ Unprocessed stress can accumulate quietly.  
Your stability determines your effectiveness—schedule decompression after intense deployments.
</Callout>

---

✅ **7. Build ongoing community resilience**
- Connect with therapists or trauma-informed allies willing to volunteer post-event.  
- Share mental health resource lists with your pod or region.  
- Encourage a culture of care: check-ins, quiet spaces, and rest breaks are part of logistics.  
- Advocate for emotional safety planning in every operation.
