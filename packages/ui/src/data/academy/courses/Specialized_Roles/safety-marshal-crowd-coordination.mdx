# 🚦 Safety Marshal: Crowd Coordination & Hazard Prevention
<TrackBadge variant="specialized-role" />

<Callout type="info">
Safety Marshals keep movement organized and people safe. They see the big picture, guide crowds with confidence, and prevent accidents or panic before they start.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
In fast-moving or crowded environments, confusion can become dangerous. **Safety Marshals** act as the movement’s traffic controllers—keeping people oriented, coordinated, and protected from physical hazards.  

This role is critical in marches, supply distributions, and CDC operations. Safety Marshals combine awareness, communication, and calm direction to help the group move as one while avoiding unnecessary confrontation or injury.  

You’ll learn to:
- Read crowd dynamics and anticipate movement.  
- Communicate clearly and assertively under pressure.  
- Identify and mitigate environmental or security risks.  
- Coordinate with dispatch, medics, and logistics leads.

---

## Learn & Do

✅ **1. Understand your mission**
- Maintain safety, not control.  
- Keep people informed, aware, and moving cohesively.  
- Anticipate risks—don’t wait for accidents to happen.  
- Work as part of a safety network (with medics, de-escalators, and logistics).

🔍 *Task:* Write one sentence that defines the purpose of the Safety Marshal role in your region’s operations.

---

✅ **2. Know your area and crowd**
- Walk the route or site early; identify:
  - Bottlenecks, stairs, and tight corners.  
  - Vehicle routes and emergency exits.  
  - Areas of limited visibility or sound.  
- Estimate crowd density—how many people per lane or space.  
- Assign coverage zones among marshals if possible.  

🧩 *Example:* At a march route, you and another marshal divide roles: one stays near the lead banner to control pace, the other stays near the tail to prevent stragglers.

✅ **3. Communicate clearly and confidently**
- Use simple, directional commands:
  - “Keep moving forward slowly.”  
  - “Let’s make space on the right.”  
  - “Step back, give room to medics.”  
- Speak with calm authority, not aggression.  
- Use visible signals (hand gestures, flashlight, or colored flag).  
- Pair with a buddy if possible—one talks, one observes.  

⚙️ *Tip:* People mirror energy; steady voice and posture help others stay composed.

---

✅ **4. Identify and mitigate hazards**
- Watch for:
  - Tripping or crowding risks (cables, uneven ground).  
  - Heat or dehydration.  
  - Obstructions, vehicle access, or aggressive individuals.  
  - Medical emergencies or signs of panic.  
- Take early action:
  - Reroute the crowd.  
  - Call medics.  
  - Signal dispatch of developing risks.  

<Callout type="warning">
⚠️ Never attempt to handle a physical threat alone.  
Alert nearby marshals, de-escalators, or dispatch and create space.
</Callout>

---

✅ **5. Maintain group cohesion**
- Keep visual and verbal contact across marshals.  
- Use walkie, Signal, or mesh comms for updates (“Front slowing for turn,” “Tail clear,” etc.).  
- Watch for fragmentation—people separating or groups stuck behind barriers.  
- Adjust speed or direction to maintain visibility between leads and tail.  

🔍 *Task:* In a team drill, practice guiding a group of 10–20 people safely around obstacles using only hand signals and calm voice direction.

---

✅ **6. Manage emergencies**
- Stay calm, set the tone for others.  
- Direct people *away* from danger, not just “back.”  
- Create clear lanes for medics or exits.  
- Use plain, consistent language for commands:
  - “Walk, don’t run.”  
  - “Follow the green flag.”  
  - “Medics to the left lane.”  
- Communicate incident details to dispatch (time, location, type).  

🧩 *Example:* Smoke appears from a nearby building. You calmly guide the crowd left along side streets, ensuring medics and vehicles have a clear path.

---

✅ **7. Debrief and document**
- After the event:
  - Note any hazards encountered and how they were handled.  
  - Share feedback with Safety or Logistics Leads.  
  - Log times of major crowd movements or interventions.  
- Regular debriefs help improve routes, signage, and team coordination.
