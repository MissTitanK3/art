# 🧸 Child Specialist Role
<TrackBadge variant="community-care" />

The **Child Specialist** supports the emotional and physical well-being of children present during enforcement actions, community disruptions, or crises.

<Callout type="info">
This role requires emotional steadiness, trauma-informed awareness, and strong boundaries.  
It is not a childcare or therapy role, but a focused field support position within response teams.
</Callout>

<Callout type="important">
📊 Over 25% of children who witness ICE raids show PTSD symptoms.  
A calm, informed presence can significantly reduce long-term trauma.
</Callout>

<Callout type="hint">
If language or accessibility barriers exist, plan early.  
- [Translator Micro-Badge](/courses/translator-micro-badge)  
- [ASL Interpreter Micro-Badge](/courses/asl-interpreter-micro-badge)
</Callout>

---

## Purpose of the Role

Children may be:
- Witnessing ICE or police actions  
- Attending rallies or protests  
- Left behind after caregiver detention  
- Living in displacement or fear  

Your job is to ensure they feel **seen, safe, and connected**—without creating dependency or exposure.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Do No Harm** — never add confusion or risk.  
2. **Presence Over Performance** — calm > clever.  
3. **Consent & Agency** — offer options, not orders.  
4. **Follow the Child’s Pace** — they decide engagement.  
5. **Support, Don’t Substitute** — you assist caregivers, not replace them.

---

## What to Carry

| Item | Purpose |
|------|----------|
| Snacks / water | Comfort and stability |
| Small toys / fidget items | Regulation and distraction |
| Soft blanket / towel | Physical containment |
| Child-friendly KYR cards | Empower older youth |
| Crayons / paper | Expression, grounding |
| Cultural or spiritual tokens | Identity affirmation |

<Callout type="warning">
Never give edible, cultural, or religious items without guardian consent or written team protocol.
</Callout>

---

## How to Talk to Kids in Crisis

Keep words short, honest, and safe.

> “I’m here to help.”  
> “You’re safe right now.”  
> “You can talk or be quiet.”  
> “Would you like to sit or stand?”

Avoid legal talk, false promises, or pressure.  
Use grounding: **breath, senses, movement, choice.**

---

## Trauma Responses by Age

<Callout type="warning">
⚠️ Never force eye contact or touch. Let the child set the pace.
</Callout>

| Age | Common Reactions | Support Approach |
|------|------------------|------------------|
| Toddlers | Crying, hiding, regression | Gentle tone, physical space |
| School-Age | Clinging, repetitive questions | Simple answers, predictability |
| Teens | Anger, shutdown, defiance | Respect autonomy, offer peers |

Your task is containment, not correction.

---

## Supporting Youth & Teens

Teens often feel distrust or shame.  
What helps:
- Respect privacy and autonomy  
- Be direct, not patronizing  
- Connect to older peers, legal aid, or counselors

## Scenarios

### 1. ICE Raid at Home
Two kids (6 and 10) left with an aunt. One hides, non-verbal.  
**Steps:**  
- Introduce yourself softly  
- Confirm caregiver presence  
- Offer sensory comfort (if consented)  
- Stay through care transfer  

**Debrief:** What cultural norms shaped comfort?

---

### 2. Protest with Police Aggression
Youth scattered; minors remain.  
**Steps:**  
- Announce “Safety support” role  
- Move to safe zone  
- Ground emotionally  
- Notify dispatch if separated  

**Debrief:** How could prep or briefings reduce panic?

---

### 3. Language Barrier at Checkpoint
A 7-year-old speaks only Mixtec; caregiver detained.  
**Steps:**  
- Use calm gestures and proximity  
- Avoid crowding  
- Request bilingual teammate  

<Callout type="hint">
Never record children.  
Review [Documentation Safety](/courses/documentation-safety) before visual documentation.
</Callout>

**Debrief:** What nonverbal tools help bridge gaps?

---

## Grounding & Recovery Tools

- Press feet into ground  
- Name 5 things around  
- Hold soft item  
- Breathe with hand or bubbles  
- Draw freely  

Simple sensory grounding rebuilds control.

---

## Coordination & Handoffs

Collaborate with:
- **De-escalators** – protect physical space  
- **Legal Observers** – log presence for accountability  
- **Rideshare / Logistics** – ensure safe transit  
- **Care Teams** – manage follow-up and decompression  

Always confirm the next responsible adult.

<Callout type="info">
After incidents, follow [After-Action Data Hygiene](/courses/after-action-data-hygiene).  
Do not retain child data unless required by legal partners.
</Callout>

---

## Legal & Ethical Boundaries

| Never Do | Always Do |
|-----------|------------|
| Transport a child alone | Work in teams of two+ |
| Speak with police about a child | Refer to legal leads |
| Record images or audio | Document presence anonymously |
| Promise custody or reunification | Facilitate verified contacts |

If follow-through is required, link with  
- [Court Support](/courses/court-support)  
- [Legal Follow-Through & Jail Support](/courses/legal-followthrough-jail-support)

## Final Reminders

- You are not the rescuer — you are the anchor.  
- Calm is contagious.  
- Always work in pairs or within dispatch structure.  

<Callout type="success">
Your stillness may become the only safe memory that child keeps.  
Hold that power with gentleness.
</Callout>
