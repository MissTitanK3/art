# 🌿 Grounding Support: 1-on-1 Emotional Stabilization & Presence
<TrackBadge variant="specialized-role" />

<Callout type="info">
Grounding Supporters hold the line of calm. They help people return to safety and awareness during moments of emotional overload, using presence, consent, and gentle technique rather than fixing or diagnosing.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
Activism, crisis response, and high-energy spaces can overwhelm even the most prepared participants. **Grounding Support** is a peer-to-peer practice of helping someone stabilize emotionally, physically, or psychologically when they’re distressed—but not in medical crisis.  

You’re not a therapist or medic. You are a **calm, witnessing presence** that anchors another person back to the here and now.  

This course will teach you to:
- Recognize signs of overwhelm and early distress.  
- Offer grounding using trauma-informed, consent-based methods.  
- Know when and how to hand off to mental health or medical teams.  
- Maintain your own boundaries and calm regulation.

---

## Learn & Do

✅ **1. Recognize signs of overwhelm**
- Common indicators:
  - Rapid breathing or blank stare.  
  - Shaking, crying, or freezing up.  
  - Sudden silence, withdrawal, or pacing.  
  - Repeating “I can’t” or dissociating (seeming detached).  
- Notice early changes in tone, posture, or focus.  
- Approach gently, without rushing or startling.

🔍 *Task:* Write down three early signs of distress you’ve seen in others and how you might approach calmly.

---

✅ **2. Ask for consent before engaging**
- Always start with permission:
  - “Would you like some company?”  
  - “Can I sit near you?”  
  - “Would it help if I stayed quiet with you?”  
- If they say no, respect it fully.  
- Offer presence at a slight distance if appropriate.  
- Never initiate physical touch without explicit verbal consent.

🧩 *Example:* You notice someone sitting on the curb breathing heavily. You ask, “Can I sit beside you for a minute?” They nod, and you sit quietly, letting them lead any conversation.

✅ **3. Use simple grounding techniques**
- **Breath anchoring:**  
  “Let’s try breathing together. In through the nose, out through the mouth.”  
- **Sensory grounding:**  
  “Can you name five things you can see?”  
- **Body awareness:**  
  Encourage slow movement—roll shoulders, feel feet, stretch hands.  
- **Environmental grounding:**  
  Offer cool water, steady footing, and shade.  

⚙️ *Tip:* Keep your tone low, even, and rhythmic. The body entrains to calm sound.

---

✅ **4. Maintain low intervention and quiet presence**
- The goal is not to fix, only to stabilize.  
- Silence is often more effective than reassurance.  
- Avoid judgmental or minimizing phrases:
  - Don’t say “You’re okay” or “Calm down.”  
  - Instead: “You’re safe right now,” or “You can take your time.”  
- Stay at eye level or below.  
- Match breathing pace—don’t force synchronization.

<Callout type="warning">
⚠️ Never escalate contact, raise volume, or restrain someone unless safety is immediately at risk.  
Respect autonomy above all.
</Callout>

---

✅ **5. Know when to hand off**
- Escalate to **Mental Health** or **Medic teams** if:
  - Person is incoherent or unresponsive.  
  - Expresses suicidal thoughts or medical distress.  
  - Physical symptoms (fainting, chest pain, hyperventilation) appear.  
- Explain what’s happening:
  - “I’m going to call someone with more experience to help.”  
- Stay until help arrives unless asked to leave.

🧩 *Example:* Someone begins trembling uncontrollably. You radio the Care Station and stay nearby, softly reminding them they’re not alone until trained support arrives.

---

✅ **6. Care for yourself after grounding others**
- Discharge emotion after the interaction:
  - Walk, stretch, or shake out hands.  
  - Take deep breaths and hydrate.  
- Write brief notes for debriefing (no identifying details).  
- If you feel residual stress, talk with peer support or decompress privately.  
- Grounders must ground themselves.

🔍 *Task:* Create a short personal ritual to reset after emotionally intense moments (e.g., hand rinse, breath, affirmation).
