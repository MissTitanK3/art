# 🎥 Press Coordination: Media Engagement & Participant Protection
<TrackBadge variant="specialized-role" />

<Callout type="info">
Press Coordinators manage the delicate balance between visibility and safety. They guide journalists, protect consent, and ensure that coverage uplifts movements without endangering participants.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
Media presence can amplify truth or weaponize narrative. **Press Coordinators** ensure that reporters and cameras operate responsibly around direct actions, courthouse gatherings, or mutual aid events.  

This role requires diplomacy, assertiveness, and clear ethical grounding. Press Coordinators act as the **interface between movements and media**, ensuring safety, consent, and factual integrity.  

You’ll learn to:
- Facilitate fair and safe press access.  
- Protect individuals from unwanted exposure.  
- Communicate movement messages with precision.  
- De-escalate tensions between press, law enforcement, and community members.

---

## Learn & Do

✅ **1. Prepare before media arrives**
- Coordinate with **Comms or Legal teams** to define:
  - Approved talking points.  
  - Areas where filming or photos are permitted.  
  - Sensitive zones (Care Station, Medics, minors).  
- Identify press-friendly representatives or speakers.  
- Set up **Press Perimeter Zones** where journalists can film safely.  
- Brief volunteers: who can talk, who cannot, and what “off the record” means.

🔍 *Task:* Create a 3-point “Press Readiness Checklist” for any event (e.g., boundaries, spokesperson, media entry path).

---

✅ **2. Greet and orient press calmly**
- Approach first—don’t wait for press to wander unescorted.  
- Greet with composure:
  - “Hi, I’m the press coordinator. I can help you find the best location for coverage.”  
- Request press credentials or outlet name if unknown.  
- Explain boundaries clearly and concisely:
  - “Please avoid filming faces in the Care Zone.”  
  - “Interviews happen at the west end with consent only.”  
- Keep tone neutral, not adversarial—professional earns cooperation.

🧩 *Example:* A freelance reporter approaches filming crowd faces. You calmly redirect: “We’re protecting participant privacy—shooting from this angle avoids IDs.”

✅ **3. Protect consent and confidentiality**
- Always confirm consent before any recording or photography of individuals.  
- If someone says “no,” intervene immediately and enforce it politely.  
- Minors, undocumented individuals, and trauma-affected persons **must never be filmed** without explicit permission.  
- Post visible signs at sensitive zones: “No filming—Decompression Area.”  
- Provide alternative visuals (supplies, signage, wide shots) for press coverage.  

<Callout type="warning">
⚠️ Unauthorized photos or interviews can expose people to retaliation.  
Protect community safety over publicity every time.
</Callout>

---

✅ **4. Manage press–community interactions**
- Monitor tone and body language.  
- Step in early if you sense discomfort or intrusion.  
- Offer to stand nearby during interviews as neutral support.  
- Keep an eye on officers or agitators using press as cover to gather footage.  
- Redirect conflict with grounding language:
  - “Let’s pause here—everyone take a breath.”  
  - “We can continue once consent is confirmed.”  

⚙️ *Tip:* A steady demeanor discourages escalation more effectively than volume.

---

✅ **5. Handle live coverage and misinformation**
- If livestreams are active:
  - Ask streamers to blur faces or delay broadcast.  
  - Avoid saying names or specific locations on open mic.  
- Monitor social media for miscaptioned or misleading posts.  
- Report disinformation to **Media Monitor** or **Comms Lead** for correction.  
- Never argue publicly—issue clarifications through verified channels only.

🧩 *Example:* You see a viral post mislabeling a supply distribution as a “riot.” You alert Comms, who publish a factual correction via trusted account.

---

✅ **6. Build relationships with ethical media**
- Keep a vetted list of cooperative reporters.  
- Thank those who respect boundaries.  
- Offer resource packets:
  - Movement background  
  - Accessibility and consent guidelines  
  - Contact for follow-up verification  
- This fosters trust and future collaboration.  
- Avoid giving exclusives or favoritism—transparency builds credibility.

🔍 *Task:* Write a sample short message to send to a reporter after a respectful interaction, maintaining rapport for future coordination.

---

✅ **7. Debrief and document**
- After event:
  - Note which outlets attended and any issues encountered.  
  - Record unauthorized footage incidents or consent violations.  
  - Share updates with Legal or Comms.  
- Keep a “Press Engagement Log” with:
  - Date, outlet, reporter, notes on behavior.  
  - Follow-up needed (e.g., retraction request, correction).  
- Archive securely; never post logs publicly.
