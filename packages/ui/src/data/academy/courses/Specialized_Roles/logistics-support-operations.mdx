# 🚚 Logistics Support: Supply, Setup & Sustenance Operations
<TrackBadge variant="specialized-role" />

<Callout type="info">
Logistics Support ensures everyone has what they need to keep going. Food, water, rest, and safety don’t appear by accident—they’re coordinated by sharp planners who keep operations humane and sustainable.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
Every movement depends on logistics. **Logistics Support volunteers** handle the flow of physical supplies—ensuring hydration, nutrition, rest spaces, sanitation, and signage are reliable and replenished.  

This role suits detail-oriented planners and quick problem solvers who anticipate needs before they arise. A well-run logistics system keeps morale high, prevents burnout, and allows teams to stay focused on their mission.  

This course trains you to:
- Manage inventory, transport, and drop-offs efficiently.  
- Maintain sanitation and rest infrastructure under varying conditions.  
- Support long operations or high-stress deployments.  
- Communicate clearly with dispatch, safety, and medical teams.

---

## Learn & Do

✅ **1. Understand the logistics lifecycle**
- **Pre-deployment:** inventory, pack, transport planning.  
- **Active operations:** supply drop-offs, hydration checks, area maintenance.  
- **Post-deployment:** cleanup, waste sorting, and restock.  
- Maintain a **needs tracker** shared with dispatch or coordination hub.  
- Operate with both **planned supply chains** and **emergency improvisation**.

🔍 *Task:* Build a simple table of “high-frequency needs” for your next action (food, water, PPE, signage) and mark which are perishable, reusable, or single-use.

---

✅ **2. Manage essential categories**
- **Hydration:** calculate 1L per person per 2 hours minimum; use refill stations over single bottles.  
- **Food:** focus on non-perishable, allergen-aware, energy-sustaining options.  
- **Sanitation:** provide accessible restrooms or portable options; stock soap and gloves.  
- **Rest & Shade:** set up designated zones for breaks; rotate volunteers.  
- **Signage:** clear, weatherproof, multilingual as needed.  

🧩 *Example:* During a 6-hour mutual aid distribution, your team maintains hydration stations and rotates volunteers through shaded rest tents while dispatch tracks overall resource levels.

✅ **3. Coordinate supply chains**
- Track quantities and restock triggers.  
- Assign drivers or runners for deliveries.  
- Confirm delivery handoffs with timestamps.  
- Keep supply cache separate from active staging area to prevent depletion.  
- Always have a **“rapid pack”** for emergencies—water, snacks, first aid, PPE.

⚙️ *Tip:* Label crates by category and event. Example: “CDC-Bathroom 3,” “Hilltop-Food North.”

---

✅ **4. Communicate effectively with dispatch**
- Provide regular updates on resource levels and delivery ETAs.  
- Log requests and completions in shared systems or via radio.  
- Flag shortages early—never let others discover them mid-event.  
- Confirm each transfer: “10L water dropped at south tent, time 15:42.”  
- Coordinate with Safety Marshals for crowd routing around resupply points.

<Callout type="warning">
⚠️ Never reveal storage locations or supply routes on public channels.  
Even basic info can be exploited to disrupt operations.
</Callout>

---

✅ **5. Maintain accessibility and inclusion**
- Ensure all food and rest spaces are wheelchair-accessible.  
- Provide labeled allergen-free options.  
- Include menstrual, infant, and hygiene supplies.  
- Translate signs for multilingual communities.  
- Prioritize shade, seating, and sensory calm areas for neurodivergent or disabled participants.

🧩 *Example:* Your logistics team sets up a “quiet zone” tent near hydration with noise-dampening and shade cloth for overstimulated volunteers.

---

✅ **6. Plan for disruptions**
- Always have Plan B:
  - **Weather shift:** waterproof covers, alternate storage.  
  - **Vehicle breakdown:** local runners or bikes.  
  - **Loss of comms:** fixed drop zones with posted schedules.  
  - **Supply theft:** log loss and redistribute remaining stock strategically.  
- Build redundancy—store 10–20% extra of critical items.

🔍 *Task:* Create a 3-scenario checklist: heavy rain, police presence, vehicle loss. What changes in your logistics flow?

---

✅ **7. Closeout and review**
- Collect unused supplies for redistribution or storage.  
- Document what ran out first and why.  
- Sanitize and repack reusable containers.  
- Thank volunteers and donors explicitly—morale sustains logistics.  
- File report with photos, inventory, and notes for next event.
