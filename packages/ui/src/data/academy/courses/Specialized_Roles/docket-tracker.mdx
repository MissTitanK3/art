# 🗂️ Docket Tracker: Court Calendar Management & Legal Coordination
<TrackBadge variant="specialized-role" />

<Callout type="info">
Docket Trackers keep everyone aligned. They monitor court schedules, record attendance, and distribute updates to ensure that no hearing or appearance slips through the cracks.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
Courts operate on shifting calendars—cases get moved, merged, or canceled with little notice. **Docket Trackers** maintain situational awareness of all upcoming hearings, ensuring that community defense teams, legal observers, and families are informed in real time.  

You’ll learn to:
- Monitor court dockets and calendars for updates.  
- Communicate schedule changes clearly to relevant teams.  
- Maintain secure, up-to-date tracking logs.  
- Prevent confusion during multi-case or multi-defendant hearings.

---

## Learn & Do

✅ **1. Understand the structure of a docket**
- A *docket* lists court events by **date, time, case number, and location**.  
- Entries can include arraignments, hearings, motions, or continuances.  
- Updates may occur multiple times per day.  
- Docket data is public, but confidentiality rules apply to some proceedings.  
- Learn where your jurisdiction posts updates:
  - County clerk’s site, state court portal, or physical bulletin boards.  

🔍 *Task:* Identify your local court’s online docket system and bookmark it. Learn how to filter by case number or defendant name.

---

✅ **2. Set up your tracking system**
- Create a shared spreadsheet or encrypted log with:
  - Defendant name or case code  
  - Courtroom number or judge  
  - Date/time of hearing  
  - Attendance (supporters, observers, family)  
  - Notes and outcome summary  
- Use color codes:
  - 🟩 Confirmed & Attended  
  - 🟨 Rescheduled  
  - 🟥 Canceled / No Show  
- Maintain daily backup copies.

🧩 *Example:* You maintain a regional tracker with 15 cases. As updates come in, you mark reschedules in yellow and add the new date in comments.

✅ **3. Monitor for changes**
- Check official docket sources at least twice daily (morning and midday).  
- Watch for:
  - Case movement to different judges or rooms.  
  - Canceled hearings (often labeled “stricken”).  
  - New motions added to the same day.  
- Compare to your internal tracker and highlight differences.  
- Confirm updates with **Legal Liaison** before broadcasting.

⚙️ *Tip:* Screenshot or export the docket daily for reference, in case online data changes later.

---

✅ **4. Communicate updates effectively**
- Use brief, structured messages:
  - “Case 22-416: Rescheduled to 4/3, 9:00 AM, Courtroom 302.”  
  - “Observer needed for 2:30 PM, Tacoma Courthouse.”  
- Send updates to:
  - Legal Liaison or defense attorney contact.  
  - Court Support or Courthouse Marshal teams.  
  - Dispatch for record sync.  
- Avoid personal commentary—stick to verified facts and next steps.

<Callout type="warning">
⚠️ Never share confidential case notes or private defense info.  
Only communicate what’s publicly verifiable or approved by the legal team.
</Callout>

---

✅ **5. Track attendance and outcomes**
- Record who attended each hearing (supporters, observers).  
- Log key outcomes:
  - Continuance granted  
  - Charges dropped or reduced  
  - Hearing postponed  
  - Next appearance date  
- Send end-of-day summary to **Legal Liaison** or **Court Support Lead**.  
- Archive each week’s sheet for historical tracking.

🧩 *Example:* You log that 6 supporters attended Monday’s arraignment. The judge postponed to Friday. You update the sheet and message the new time to the court support group.

---

✅ **6. Stay organized and ethical**
- Treat docket data as semi-public but sensitive.  
- Redact full names if sharing with general volunteers.  
- Keep timestamps on every entry.  
- Use neutral, professional tone in all records.  
- When uncertain, verify with courthouse clerk directly.

🔍 *Task:* Draft a daily workflow checklist for checking, updating, and reporting dockets efficiently.

---

✅ **7. Closeout and backup**
- At week’s end:
  - Export logs to PDF or encrypted drive.  
  - Purge temporary notes from phones or chats.  
  - Share summary table with Legal and Dispatch.  
- Reflect on patterns—how many reschedules or missed updates occurred?  
- Adjust system for smoother coordination next cycle.
