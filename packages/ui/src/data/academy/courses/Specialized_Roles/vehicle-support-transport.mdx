# 🚐 Vehicle Support: Transport, Evacuation & Mobile Safety
<TrackBadge variant="specialized-role" />

<Callout type="info">
Vehicle Support keeps people and supplies moving. Drivers enable rapid response, safe exit, and reliable logistics in unpredictable conditions—acting as the mobile backbone of every operation.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
When situations shift quickly, transportation becomes critical.  
**Vehicle Support** ensures mobility for people, gear, and medical or logistical supplies. These volunteers operate as calm, alert, and coordinated drivers who prioritize safety and discretion over speed.  

You’ll learn to:
- Plan routes and contingencies.  
- Transport people and cargo safely.  
- Communicate effectively with dispatch and field teams.  
- Prepare and maintain vehicles for operational readiness.  

This role requires situational awareness, patience, and the ability to stay grounded under pressure.

---

## Learn & Do

✅ **1. Prepare your vehicle and yourself**
- Check all essential systems before every deployment:
  - Fuel, brakes, tires, lights, wipers, spare, and fluids.  
- Keep emergency items:
  - First aid kit, fire extinguisher, phone charger, map, flashlight, gloves.  
- Have basic tools for quick fixes (fuses, tire plug kit, coolant).  
- Clean inside to reduce distractions and maximize visibility.  
- Plan personal readiness—hydration, rest, and calm focus.

🔍 *Task:* Build a “Vehicle Go-Bag” with everything you need for 12 hours of continuous use.

---

✅ **2. Plan safe and flexible routes**
- Identify **primary**, **secondary**, and **emergency** routes before every action.  
- Use offline navigation apps or printed maps in case of service loss.  
- Know nearest hospitals, exits, and fuel stations.  
- Avoid predictable routes and avoid leading convoys through chokepoints.  
- Share route plans only with dispatch or authorized leads.

🧩 *Example:* During an event, one route becomes blocked by police. You calmly switch to pre-mapped back roads and notify dispatch without broadcasting location publicly.

✅ **3. Load and transport safely**
- Secure all gear—nothing loose that can shift or fall.  
- Keep passenger count within safety limits.  
- Maintain communication via hands-free radio or passenger relay.  
- Do not text or operate devices while driving.  
- Prioritize calm demeanor—passengers mirror your state.  

⚙️ *Tip:* Designate a **navigator or comms partner** for longer operations.

---

✅ **4. Manage pickups and drop-offs**
- Confirm pickup signals and rendezvous points ahead of time.  
- Keep headlights dim or off in sensitive areas when requested for privacy.  
- Park facing exit direction for rapid departure.  
- Never block driveways or emergency lanes.  
- Double-check that everyone and everything is accounted for before leaving.  

<Callout type="warning">
⚠️ Never leave an assigned zone or person without explicit clearance.  
Accountability prevents confusion and risk during emergencies.
</Callout>

---

✅ **5. Coordinate with Dispatch and other teams**
- Maintain check-ins before and after each leg of transport.  
- Use code names or route identifiers instead of personal details.  
- Log mileage and times for transparency.  
- Immediately report:
  - Route changes  
  - Delays or detours  
  - Mechanical issues or hazards  
- Keep radio chatter short, factual, and calm.

🧩 *Example:* You notify dispatch, “Team Bravo dropped at Point Delta, returning for gear pickup,” ensuring all movements stay tracked.

---

✅ **6. Handle emergencies and breakdowns**
- Pull off road safely, use hazards, and notify dispatch.  
- Keep passengers calm and move to safe location if necessary.  
- Use fire extinguisher only if small and safe to handle.  
- If unable to continue, coordinate another vehicle or tow.  
- Do not leave gear or personal info unsecured in vehicle.  
- Document incidents for follow-up and lessons learned.

🔍 *Task:* Make a 3-step personal checklist for handling a flat tire or minor breakdown under stress.

---

✅ **7. Post-operation recovery**
- Refuel and restock immediately after return.  
- Clean vehicle interior and remove personal data (maps, rosters, notes).  
- Log hours, mileage, and any issues needing maintenance.  
- Reflect on what improved or slowed your response.  
- Schedule downtime—fatigue is a safety hazard.
