# 🌤️ Court Float Support: Post-Hearing Presence & Gentle Community Care
<TrackBadge variant="specialized-role" />

<Callout type="info">
Court Float Supporters are the bridge between courtroom stress and community care. They meet people where they are—outside the building, in the moment, and without judgment.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
Legal proceedings are emotionally heavy. People leaving court—defendants, family, supporters—often exit overwhelmed, confused, or in silence. **Court Float Supporters** act as gentle anchors outside the courthouse, helping individuals transition from high-stress environments to safer emotional ground.  

They practice **presence, not persuasion**, providing an empathetic ear and grounding assistance while ensuring that no one leaves alone in visible distress.  

This course will teach you to:
- Offer quiet, trauma-informed support after court.  
- Recognize emotional exhaustion and burnout.  
- Coordinate discreetly with Care, Legal, or Logistics teams.  
- Maintain neutrality and discretion at all times.

---

## Learn & Do

✅ **1. Prepare your environment and mindset**
- Arrive 15–30 minutes before proceedings end.  
- Choose a visible yet calm position—sidewalk benches, shade near exits.  
- Bring:
  - Water, tissues, and soft snacks.  
  - Small notepad (no names).  
  - Optional calming items: stones, mints, cooling cloths.  
- Center yourself first—slower breathing, relaxed posture, open attention.  
- Remember: your presence matters more than your words.

🔍 *Task:* Before your next support shift, list 3 physical cues that remind you to stay grounded and receptive.

---

✅ **2. Read emotional cues**
- Watch for:
  - Shaking hands, shallow breath, staring, pacing.  
  - Sudden quiet or tears.  
  - Family conflict or confusion about logistics.  
- Approach softly:
  - “Would you like some water?”  
  - “Would you like me to sit nearby?”  
- Avoid prying or forcing conversation.  
- Some people just need silent proximity.

🧩 *Example:* After a sentencing, you offer a tissue and calm presence while someone gathers themself to call a relative. No questions, just space.

✅ **3. Practice grounded, low-intervention support**
- Keep voice low and steady.  
- Mirror calm body language—steady stance, soft gaze.  
- Offer short grounding suggestions if asked:
  - “Try feeling your feet on the ground.”  
  - “Let’s take a slow breath together.”  
- Redirect crowd attention away if needed, protecting privacy.  
- Provide information cards for next steps only when requested (e.g., Legal Support or Care Station).  

<Callout type="warning">
⚠️ Avoid offering legal advice, interpretation of rulings, or personal opinions about outcomes.  
You are emotional support, not legal counsel.
</Callout>

---

✅ **4. Coordinate quietly with teams**
- If someone seems lost or unsafe:
  - Alert **Care Station Coordinator**, **Mental Health**, or **Court Support Lead**.  
  - Never leave them alone if visibly disoriented.  
- Radio or Signal update (no names):  
  - “Person outside east exit appears distressed, requesting Care follow-up.”  
- Escort gently only if welcomed; never touch without consent.  
- Record time and general description for internal debrief.

⚙️ *Tip:* Pair shifts—one Floater observes, the other quietly handles communication.

---

✅ **5. Maintain discretion and cultural humility**
- Listen more than you speak.  
- Avoid assumptions about relationships, gender, or roles.  
- Offer multilingual or ASL access when available.  
- Respect cultural boundaries around comfort and emotion.  
- Keep all observations confidential unless safety demands escalation.

🔍 *Task:* Write three phrases you can use to show presence without pressure, such as “I’m here if you need,” or “Take your time.”

---

✅ **6. End the day with closure**
- Check out with Care or Dispatch before leaving.  
- Debrief any notable patterns (conflict, exhaustion, confusion).  
- Return supplies and tidy space.  
- Release emotional residue—walk, breathe, hydrate.  
- Log lessons for next shift’s continuity.

🧩 *Example:* You debrief noting that many attendees left confused about future hearings. You flag this to Docket Tracker for communication improvement.
