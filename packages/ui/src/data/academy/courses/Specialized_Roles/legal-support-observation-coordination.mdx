# ⚖️ Legal Support: Observation & Coordination
<TrackBadge variant="specialized-role" />

<Callout type="info">
Legal Support volunteers act as the eyes and ears of justice on the ground. Their job is to observe, record, and coordinate—not to intervene—so that when rights are violated, evidence exists and accountability follows.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
Legal Support is the operational bridge between **community defense** and **legal advocacy**. These volunteers document law enforcement behavior, ensure rights are respected, and coordinate with attorneys and legal hotlines after events.  

This role protects the movement by building a factual record that can withstand scrutiny in court, media, or public reports.  

Legal Support can include:
- Acting as an independent legal observer.  
- Gathering documentation of detentions, arrests, or abuses.  
- Communicating verified details to regional legal teams.  
- Tracking detainees and ensuring follow-up through jail support.  

This course will help you move from bystander to structured witness.

---

## Learn & Do

✅ **1. Understand your legal role**
- Legal observers **do not intervene**—they witness and record.  
- Stay neutral, calm, and visible (often with identifying hats or vests).  
- Your role is to protect *facts*, not sides.  
- If approached by law enforcement, identify yourself and step aside; do not argue.  

🔍 *Task:* Write down three sentences that define what you can and cannot do as a Legal Support volunteer in your region.

---

✅ **2. Know your rights and limits**
- Learn local protest laws and **public vs. private property** rules.  
- Recording is protected in public spaces, but do not cross police lines.  
- If ordered to move, comply and continue observing safely.  
- Never obstruct, provoke, or conceal evidence.  
- Familiarize yourself with your local **National Lawyers Guild (NLG)** or **legal observer team protocols**.

✅ **3. Observe and document clearly**
- Record: time, date, location, and event description.  
- Capture **who, what, where, when, how**, and any visible identifiers (badge numbers, vehicle markings, location signs).  
- Avoid commentary or assumptions; stick to observable facts.  
- Use timestamps, or announce time periodically when recording.  
- If photographing or filming:
  - Keep faces of community members blurred when possible.  
  - Focus on uniformed personnel and context.  

🧩 *Example:* “At 16:42, near 12th & Pine, four officers from Tacoma PD formed a line blocking the east intersection. No dispersal order heard. One protester detained; badge #438 visible.”

---

✅ **4. Maintain safety and distance**
- Always work in pairs or teams.  
- Keep at least 10–15 feet between you and active engagement.  
- Avoid confined areas or enclosed crowd pockets.  
- Stay aware of exits and medical aid locations.  
- Carry minimal gear: clipboard, pen, phone or camera, ID (if needed).  

⚙️ *Task:* Walk around a large public space and identify safe observation points with multiple exits.

---

✅ **5. Coordinate with legal teams**
- Legal Support’s value depends on **timely communication**.  
- Send notes or recordings to your region’s **Legal Coordination Lead** after each event.  
- If arrests occur:
  - Note names, physical descriptions, and time/location of detention.  
  - Relay details to **Jail Support** or **Legal Hotline** as soon as safe.  
- Maintain chain of custody for all files—timestamp and encrypt.  

---

✅ **6. Identify violations quickly**
- Watch for:
  - Unlawful dispersal or kettling.  
  - Use of force without warning.  
  - Confiscation or destruction of protest materials.  
  - Denial of medical or legal aid.  
  - Targeted arrests (press, medics, organizers).  
- When in doubt, document fully, mark “Potential Rights Violation,” and flag for review.  

<Callout type="warning">
⚠️ Do not share footage publicly before legal review.  
Public leaks can jeopardize cases and expose witnesses.
</Callout>

---

✅ **7. After-action coordination**
- Meet with your Legal Coordinator or dispatch contact post-event.  
- Store notes securely (encrypted cloud or hardcopy in locked storage).  
- Summarize findings for After-Action Review (AAR):  
  - What was observed  
  - Any potential rights violations  
  - Law enforcement behavior trends  
- Debrief emotionally with peers—witnessing violence can cause trauma.
