# 🧾 Know Your Rights Observer: Accountability & Documentation
<TrackBadge variant="specialized-role" />

<Callout type="info">
Know Your Rights Observers are impartial witnesses who help hold power accountable. Their calm, factual observation preserves evidence and protects the community from misinformation or retaliation.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
A **Know Your Rights (KYR) Observer** monitors interactions between authorities and civilians during actions, mutual aid events, or public gatherings. Their mission: document potential civil rights violations, ensure transparency, and create a reliable factual record for future legal or advocacy work.  

This role requires **focus, neutrality, and discipline**. KYR Observers are not protesters or participants. They stand slightly apart—visible but calm—capturing data others might miss.  

Key responsibilities include:
- Watching for rights violations or misconduct.  
- Recording factual, verifiable details without bias.  
- Relaying documentation securely to legal or oversight teams.  
- Supporting post-event accountability without escalating conflict.

---

## Learn & Do

✅ **1. Understand your mission**
- Observe, don’t intervene.  
- Document objectively, even when it’s uncomfortable.  
- Protect truth, not emotion—your notes may become legal evidence.  
- If law enforcement or security confront you, remain polite, identify your role, and step back.  

🔍 *Task:* Write a personal code of conduct that defines how you’ll maintain neutrality and composure under stress.

---

✅ **2. Know the rights you’re observing**
You must understand what counts as a **rights violation** to recognize it accurately.  
Common triggers:
- Use of force without dispersal order or warning.  
- Denial of medical care or safe egress.  
- Arrest or detention without clear cause.  
- Seizure or destruction of press or medical equipment.  
- Interference with filming in public space.

📘 *Quick reference:*  
- **First Amendment:** Protects peaceful assembly, speech, and recording public officials.  
- **Fourth Amendment:** Protects against unlawful search and seizure.  
- **Fifth Amendment:** Right to remain silent.  
- **Fourteenth Amendment:** Equal protection under law.

✅ **3. Observe systematically**
- Note **time, location, and actors involved**.  
- Record **specific actions**, not emotions or assumptions.  
- Example: “15:37 – Officer #492 pushed individual with shield, no prior dispersal order heard.”  
- Use consistent shorthand:  
  - “L” for law enforcement  
  - “P” for participant  
  - “O” for observer  

⚙️ *Task:* Practice writing five neutral observations from a recorded protest video.

---

✅ **4. Record responsibly**
- Prioritize audio or video from a **safe distance**.  
- Keep camera steady, zoom instead of approaching.  
- Record signage, agency patches, or vehicle markings for later verification.  
- Blur or redact faces of protesters when publishing or archiving footage.  
- Announce timestamps periodically if possible (“It’s 16:22 now”).  

<Callout type="warning">
⚠️ Never post live footage or identifiable media during ongoing operations.  
Doing so can endanger participants and compromise cases.
</Callout>

---

✅ **5. Maintain calm and neutrality**
- Stay detached; your presence should stabilize others, not heighten tension.  
- Avoid verbal confrontation.  
- If targeted by law enforcement:
  - Comply calmly.  
  - Continue recording if legally permitted.  
  - Document the interaction later, including officer names or badge numbers.  

🔍 *Task:* Rehearse a neutral script if confronted:  
> “I’m an observer documenting public activity. I’ll comply and move as directed.”

---

✅ **6. Coordinate with legal and media teams**
- After the event, transfer all documentation securely to:
  - **Legal Support Lead** (for review of potential violations).  
  - **Media Verification Team** (for public statement prep if appropriate).  
- Keep an **Observation Log** with:
  - Event date/time.  
  - Key incidents.  
  - File names or reference numbers.  
  - Recipients of transferred data (chain of custody).  
- Store backups encrypted and offline if possible.  

---

✅ **7. Support accountability after the fact**
- Work with Legal or Data teams to verify:
  - Which rights may have been violated.  
  - Which agencies or individuals were involved.  
  - Whether follow-up action (complaints, FOIA, legal filings) occurred.  
- Document policy patterns (e.g., repeated use of kettling, weapon deployment).  
- Archive verified cases for long-term advocacy.

🧩 *Example:* “In three separate events, same unit used unlawful dispersal tactics without notice. Filed documentation through legal team and ACLU partner.”
