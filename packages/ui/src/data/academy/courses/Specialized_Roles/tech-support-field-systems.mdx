# 🧰 Tech Support: Field Systems, Security & Comms Maintenance
<TrackBadge variant="specialized-role" />

<Callout type="info">
Tech Support keeps the network alive when everything else fails. They ensure radios connect, devices encrypt, and livestreams run safely—so operations continue even under stress.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
**Tech Support volunteers** manage the invisible infrastructure that keeps a region running. They configure hardware, maintain comms tools, and fix system disruptions in real time.  

In crisis or large-scale deployments, they become the backbone of coordination—preventing information blackouts and protecting privacy.  

This course teaches how to:
- Diagnose and fix common tech failures in the field.  
- Manage secure communication devices.  
- Support livestreams and encrypted systems safely.  
- Operate under pressure while keeping data integrity intact.

---

## Learn & Do

✅ **1. Understand your operational scope**
- Tech Support maintains **comms, devices, and security layers**—not content moderation.  
- Focus on uptime, encryption, and reliability.  
- Know who to notify for escalations (Comms Lead, Dispatcher, or Admin).  
- Always assume tools may fail and plan fallbacks.

🔍 *Task:* Draw a quick network map of your current operation—radios, mesh nodes, backup Wi-Fi, or encrypted chat links.

---

✅ **2. Configure and maintain comms gear**
- Radios:  
  - Verify frequencies, privacy codes, and call signs.  
  - Keep spare batteries charged and labeled.  
  - Log channel use during events.  
- Mesh or LoRa devices:  
  - Sync IDs and region keys before deployment.  
  - Run test pings or message loops every hour.  
- Cell / Wi-Fi networks:  
  - Use VPN or Tor for public connections.  
  - Disable unnecessary location services.  

🧩 *Example:* Before an action, you test all LoRa nodes, confirm encryption keys, and hand backup radios to Safety Marshals.

✅ **3. Handle livestream and data feeds safely**
- Livestream only when **authorized by the action lead or media team**.  
- Avoid showing faces, license plates, or sensitive tactics.  
- Use delay or blur filters for live coverage.  
- Archive safely—prefer encrypted local storage over cloud.  
- Maintain ethical consent: no filming without explicit agreement.  

⚙️ *Tip:* Treat cameras and mics as potential security leaks—cover or disable when not in use.

---

✅ **4. Troubleshoot under pressure**
- When systems fail:
  1. **Identify the layer**—hardware, network, or software.  
  2. **Restart lowest layer first** (device → router → server).  
  3. **Isolate variables**—swap cables, check battery, verify settings.  
  4. **Test alternate paths**—switch mesh channel, fallback SIM, or peer relay.  
- Keep a **paper log** of issues and fixes for AAR.  

🧩 *Example:* During a protest, Dispatch loses radio contact. You trace the issue to interference and switch both units to backup frequency within 90 seconds.

---

✅ **5. Manage encryption and privacy tools**
- Use end-to-end encrypted apps for communication (Signal, Session, Briar).  
- Rotate encryption keys periodically.  
- Disable telemetry and unnecessary sync services.  
- Store passwords securely using local vaults (Bitwarden offline, KeePass).  
- Never screenshot or copy sensitive logs.  

<Callout type="warning">
⚠️ Every message could become evidence.  
Encrypt, minimize retention, and avoid unnecessary backups.
</Callout>

---

✅ **6. Support others calmly**
- Explain fixes clearly without jargon.  
- Write simple “if-then” instructions for non-technical volunteers.  
- Create labeled kits (Chargers, Radios, Cables, SIMs).  
- Be patient—panic spreads faster than failure.  
- Focus on restoring *function*, not perfection.

🔍 *Task:* Write a one-page “Tech Emergency Guide” for your region with 5 bullet fallback protocols.

---

✅ **7. Post-operation recovery**
- Power down, clean, and inventory all devices.  
- Wipe sensitive temporary data.  
- Document recurring issues or missing gear.  
- Review what worked, what failed, and update configuration guides.  
- Check in with your own energy—information fatigue is real.
