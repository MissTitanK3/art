# ⚖️ Courthouse Marshal: Legal Site Coordination & Public Safety Flow
<TrackBadge variant="specialized-role" />

<Callout type="info">
Courthouse Marshals protect the calm at high-tension legal sites. They manage flow, watch law enforcement patterns, and help ensure that court support actions remain safe, organized, and accessible.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
Courthouses often become focal points for stress—families waiting, media present, and officers maintaining unpredictable presence.  
**Courthouse Marshals** maintain physical and emotional order in that environment, coordinating between **community supporters, legal observers, and dispatch** to ensure everyone’s safety and clarity.  

You’ll learn to:
- Direct crowd flow safely around courthouses.  
- Recognize and report shifts in law enforcement presence.  
- Maintain calm communication during tense moments.  
- Coordinate effectively with field legal and support teams.

---

## Learn & Do

✅ **1. Understand your operational role**
- You are the **anchor point** between public supporters, dispatch, and legal aid.  
- Your tasks include:
  - Managing space so lines or clusters stay accessible and respectful.  
  - Keeping communication open with court support and legal observers.  
  - Watching for de-escalation opportunities when tension rises.  
- You do not engage in legal advocacy; you protect flow, not outcomes.

🔍 *Task:* Write a short list of what you **will** and **will not** do in this role to clarify boundaries and prevent overreach.

---

✅ **2. Prepare your environment**
- Scout the courthouse area before the event:
  - Entrances, exits, ramps, shade, and restrooms.  
  - Areas of visibility and blind spots for cameras or police staging.  
  - Emergency medical access paths.  
- Coordinate with:
  - **Legal Observers** – to share notes on law enforcement presence.  
  - **Court Support Teams** – to assist families and defendants.  
  - **Safety Marshals or Floaters** – for crowd well-being.  
- Identify quiet fallback areas for emotional decompression.

🧩 *Example:* Before a hearing, you map three zones: public area (press), supporter area (families), and legal coordination area (private). You relay this plan to your team on radio.

✅ **3. Manage flow and presence**
- Maintain clear walking paths, especially near courthouse doors.  
- Greet entrants neutrally: “Court support is to the left; press line is straight ahead.”  
- Prevent clustering that blocks visibility or entrances.  
- When emotions spike:
  - Lower your tone, not volume.  
  - Suggest movement—“Let’s take a few steps this way.”  
  - Keep a soft hand signal system for coordination.  
- Avoid physical direction unless invited or necessary for safety.

<Callout type="warning">
⚠️ Avoid arguments with law enforcement or court personnel.  
Your calm body language is often more powerful than words.
</Callout>

---

✅ **4. Monitor and report law enforcement behavior**
- Track:
  - Number of visible officers and shifts in posture or positioning.  
  - Introduction of new vehicles or surveillance equipment.  
  - Approaches toward supporters, especially photographers or families.  
- Report patterns, not speculation:
  - “Two additional officers arrived, standing by east entrance.”  
  - “Media cameras moved closer to detainee transport area.”  
- Document discreetly—avoid holding phones conspicuously or recording faces of civilians.

⚙️ *Tip:* Use short, clear radio codes to avoid overloading channels (e.g., “Blue presence increased east side, 2 officers”).

---

✅ **5. Coordinate with legal and dispatch teams**
- Maintain regular check-ins every 30–60 minutes.  
- Relay only verified updates.  
- In case of arrests:
  - Record names (if publicly volunteered), time, and location.  
  - Notify **Legal Observer Lead** and **Dispatch** immediately.  
  - Direct witnesses to safe debrief areas, not confrontation zones.  
- If media approach, redirect to designated spokespeople or court support team.

🧩 *Example:* You overhear someone shouting at officers near the steps. You step in calmly, redirect them to the Care Station, and notify dispatch of the minor incident for record.

---

✅ **6. Maintain calm authority**
- Your demeanor sets tone for others.  
- Keep steady posture—upright, shoulders relaxed, eyes scanning calmly.  
- Speak slowly and with measured pauses.  
- Avoid emotional contagion—people mirror what they see.  
- Model consent in physical space (ask before entering clusters).  

🔍 *Task:* Practice a neutral grounding phrase like, “Let’s slow down,” or, “We’re still okay here,” to use during high-tension moments.

---

✅ **7. Close operations and debrief**
- Once court adjourns or support concludes:
  - Confirm all supporters and observers accounted for.  
  - Log any incidents or patterns noticed.  
  - Share summary with Dispatch or Legal Coordination Lead.  
  - Return signage, radios, and equipment.  
- Take personal recovery time before re-entering the next event.  
- Document lessons for improving future courthouse deployments.
