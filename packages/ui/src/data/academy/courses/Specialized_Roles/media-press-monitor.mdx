# 🎥 Media & Press Monitor: Coverage Oversight and Protection
<TrackBadge variant="specialized-role" />

<Callout type="info">
Media and Press Monitors act as bridges between the movement and the media. They facilitate responsible coverage while ensuring no participant’s safety or consent is violated in pursuit of a story.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
Public actions attract press, livestreamers, and independent journalists. Their presence can amplify truth—or expose participants to risk. Media Monitors ensure that coverage remains **accurate, ethical, and safe** for all involved.  

This role requires diplomacy, quick thinking, and awareness of both **press rights** and **participant privacy**.  

You’ll learn how to:
- Identify and support legitimate press.  
- De-escalate harmful or intrusive filming.  
- Communicate with journalists under pressure.  
- Protect vulnerable individuals from exposure or misrepresentation.

---

## Learn & Do

✅ **1. Understand the press landscape**
- Press includes professional journalists, independent media, and citizen reporters.  
- All have varying ethics and standards; your task is to navigate that diversity calmly.  
- Know who’s present—press passes, station tags, livestream handles.  
- Recognize legitimate credentials but remember that *press freedom ≠ immunity from accountability.*  

🔍 *Task:* Research three local press outlets that commonly cover actions. Note their tone and approach toward community movements.

---

✅ **2. Know rights and boundaries**
- Journalists have the right to film or photograph in public spaces, **but participants have a right to safety and consent** in non-public zones (medical tents, CDC interiors, minors, survivors).  
- It’s acceptable to ask press to:
  - Avoid faces or identifying markers in sensitive contexts.  
  - Step back during emotional or vulnerable moments.  
  - Confirm quotes or facts before publication.  
- Never physically block or seize equipment. Use calm, verbal redirection only.

📘 *Tip:*  
Say: “Hey, this area’s for care and privacy—can you shoot from that side instead?”  
Avoid: “You can’t film here!”

✅ **3. Identify and support legitimate press**
- Verify outlet names and credentials.  
- Track which outlets are covering the event—note who’s friendly, neutral, or hostile.  
- Offer factual briefings about the purpose of the action and ground rules for coverage.  
- Maintain a calm presence nearby to deter misrepresentation or conflict.  

🧩 *Example:* “Seattle Mutual Aid is distributing supplies. We ask press to focus on logistics, not individuals receiving aid.”

---

✅ **4. Protect vulnerable participants**
- Step in if journalists film minors, undocumented individuals, or survivors without consent.  
- Provide alternate visuals (signs, crowd shots, resource tables) that meet media needs safely.  
- Maintain a **buffer role**—you redirect, not confront.  
- Signal to Legal or Dispatch if a press situation escalates or becomes harassing.  

<Callout type="warning">
⚠️ Never shame or threaten reporters.  
Engage with respect, even when setting limits.  
Your professionalism protects credibility and trust.
</Callout>

---

✅ **5. Maintain accurate media tracking**
- Log press names, outlets, and what stories they’re covering.  
- Record:
  - Arrival/departure times.  
  - Whether filming was cooperative or invasive.  
  - Any incidents involving refusal of consent or misinformation.  
- Share reports with Media or Legal teams for follow-up and response.  

⚙️ *Task:* Create a “Press Presence Log” with columns for outlet, contact, topic, and behavior notes.

---

✅ **6. Respond to misinformation**
- If a story misrepresents events:
  - Notify your region’s Media Response or Legal Team.  
  - Collect screenshots or broadcast clips as evidence.  
  - Issue **calm, factual corrections**—never emotional rebuttals.  
  - Prepare short, verified statements for dispatch or organizers to share.  

🧩 *Example correction:*  
> “To clarify, today’s CDC distribution was peaceful and permitted. Reports of police confrontation are inaccurate.”

---

✅ **7. Coordinate with internal comms**
- Stay in contact with Dispatch and Comms leads.  
- Inform them when media coverage begins or changes tone.  
- Relay any safety concerns involving livestreams or public tracking.  
- After events, participate in After-Action Reviews to update press protocols.
