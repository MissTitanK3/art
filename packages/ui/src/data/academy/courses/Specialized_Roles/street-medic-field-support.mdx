# 🚑 Street Medic: Field First Aid & Trauma Support
<TrackBadge variant="specialized-role" />

<Callout type="info">
Street Medics are the frontline care providers during actions and emergencies. They bridge the gap between bystander and professional medical response—stabilizing, grounding, and protecting community members in crisis.
</Callout>

---

## Overview
**Street Medics** operate in unpredictable, high-stress environments—protests, mutual aid sites, climate actions, or disaster zones. Their mission is simple: **preserve life, reduce harm, and maintain calm** until higher care is available.  

They are not emergency responders under command; they are autonomous caregivers embedded in community movements.  

This course focuses on:
- Basic trauma and first aid skills.  
- Safe triage and prioritization in the field.  
- Chemical exposure and panic response.  
- Ethical and consent-based medical care in public settings.

---

## Learn & Do

✅ **1. Understand your role and limits**
- You are a **stabilizer, not a hospital.**  
- Provide immediate care to sustain breathing, circulation, and calm.  
- Do not diagnose or administer prescription meds unless licensed.  
- Always ask for **consent** before touching or treating.  
- Respect patient dignity—even in chaos.

🔍 *Task:* Write your own “scope of practice” statement: what you can do, what you should not, and when you must call for advanced medical help.

---

✅ **2. Carry and maintain your kit**
Your pack is your lifeline. At minimum:
- Gloves, masks, sanitizer  
- Gauze, tape, bandages, tourniquet  
- Saline or water for eye and wound rinse  
- Instant cold packs  
- Trauma shears, gloves, flashlight  
- Electrolytes, glucose tabs, foil blanket  
- Notepad for field log  

Keep:
- One **“Red Bag”** for trauma.  
- One **“Blue Bag”** for general first aid and wellness.  
- One **“Green Bag”** for mental health and grounding tools (earplugs, fidget, blanket).

🧩 *Example:* Two medics coordinate—one handles medical triage, the other documents vitals and communicates with dispatch.

---

✅ **3. Assess and triage quickly**
Follow a simplified **ABCs + MARCH** pattern:
- **A:** Airway – clear obstruction, check speech.  
- **B:** Breathing – observe rate and effort.  
- **C:** Circulation – control bleeding first.  
- **M:** Massive bleeding  
- **A:** Airway  
- **R:** Respiration  
- **C:** Circulation  
- **H:** Head injury / Hypothermia  

Use color code or simple tags:
- 🔴 **Critical:** Immediate life threat.  
- 🟡 **Serious but stable:** Needs care soon.  
- 🟢 **Minor:** Walks or self-manages.  
- ⚫ **Deceased:** No pulse or breathing after primary check.

⚠️ Never abandon a patient until transferred to another trained caregiver or until the area becomes unsafe.
</Callout>

✅ **4. Handle chemical exposure**
For tear gas or pepper spray:
- Move the person upwind and away from the source.  
- Flush eyes with **clean, cool water or saline** (no milk, baking soda, or oils).  
- Instruct to blink rapidly, not rub.  
- Remove contaminated clothing if safe and bag it.  
- Offer verbal reassurance; panic worsens symptoms.  

🧩 *Example:* During a protest, a participant runs from a gas cloud choking. You lead them upwind, flush eyes for 2 minutes, and monitor breathing until stable.

---

✅ **5. Address trauma and panic**
- Speak slowly and clearly: “You’re safe now. I’m here.”  
- Encourage breathing in through the nose, out through the mouth.  
- Offer grounding techniques: describe sounds, textures, or colors nearby.  
- Avoid forced physical contact; ask permission to hold a hand or steady a shoulder.  
- Watch for delayed shock—pale skin, dizziness, confusion.

⚙️ *Tip:* If someone is hyperventilating, coach slow breathing rather than using a paper bag—it can reduce oxygen dangerously.

---

✅ **6. Coordinate with dispatch and other roles**
- Relay patient info clearly:
  - Location  
  - Approximate age/gender  
  - Condition and priority  
  - Actions taken  
- Keep comms short and factual.  
- Use radio or Signal for secure updates, not public chat.  
- Coordinate with Safety Marshals for crowd clearance.  
- Handoff to professional medics or transport as soon as safe.

---

✅ **7. Maintain self and team care**
- Hydrate, rest, and rotate after every major incident.  
- Debrief emotionally; witnessing injury can create trauma.  
- Clean and restock kit immediately post-event.  
- Store medical logs securely and redact identifying data.  
- Never share photos or patient stories without consent.
